# <span class="furling-pin">Varavel'shala</span> | How We Classify Stars

> *Varathal looked at Vara's light through a prism in Era 14 and saw, for the first time, that the light contained a pattern — specific colours present and absent, a fingerprint in the spectrum. They understood immediately that this pattern could identify what Vara was made of. It took longer to understand that the same technique, applied to distant stars, could tell us what they were made of too, and how hot they were, and how old, and how long they had left. The prism was the first tool for reading the universe at a distance.*
> — Archive note, Skelavara'shala Division, Era 21

## What Stellar Classification Does

The Varathal classification system assigns every star to a type based on its spectral characteristics — the specific pattern of wavelengths present and absent in its light. These characteristics are determined by the star's surface temperature, which in turn correlates with its mass, its colour, its luminosity, and its expected lifespan.

A star's type, once determined, tells you a great deal about it without any further measurement. A K4V star like Vara is warm-to-cool, stable, long-lived, and produces light that is orange-gold in colour. A hotter star of the same mass would be shorter-lived and more energetic. A cooler star would be redder and even more long-lived. The classification carries all of this information in condensed form.

## The Classification System

Stars are classified by letter, number, and luminosity code. The letter describes temperature class, the number refines it within that class, and the luminosity code describes the star's evolutionary stage.

**Temperature classes (simplified):**

| Class | Colour | Temperature (lath) | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| O | Blue-white | Very high | Rare, extremely luminous, short-lived — tens of tael'kora at most |
| B | Blue-white | High | Luminous, short-lived — hundreds of tael'kora |
| A | White | Moderate-high | Bright, moderate lifespan |
| F | Yellow-white | Moderate | Similar to G, slightly hotter |
| G | Yellow | Moderate | The class of most familiar inhabited-world stars in the survey |
| K | Orange | Moderate-cool | Stable, long-lived — the class of Vara |
| M | Red | Cool | Very common, very long-lived, lower luminosity |

The number (0–9) refines position within the class: K0 is the hottest K star, K9 the coolest. Vara is K4 — towards the cooler end of the K class but not at the extreme.

The luminosity code "V" indicates a main sequence star — one that is fusing hydrogen in its core in the stable way that defines most of a star's active life. A star that has exhausted its hydrogen supply evolves off the main sequence into giant or dwarf configurations, denoted by different luminosity codes.

**Vara's classification — K4V:** K class (orange, stable), position 4 (mid-K, neither extremely hot nor extremely cool for its class), V (main sequence, still in active hydrogen fusion). A star with these characteristics has a total main sequence lifespan measured in tens of thousands of tael'kora. Vara is approximately 4 tael'kora old. It has many thousands of tael'kora remaining.

## Why Vara as the Reference Standard

The choice to make Vara the 1.0 reference standard for all stellar measurement was Varathal's, made in Era 14. The full reasoning is in the companion record [[Vara-Mira-shala|Vara — The Complete Record]].

The consequence for archive users: every stellar measurement expressed in Furling units carries an implicit relationship to Vara. A star with a mass of 2.3 vara'keth is 2.3 times as massive as Vara. A luminosity of 0.4 vara'val means a star that produces 40% of Vara's light output. The numbers are not arbitrary: they describe how the star differs from the star above our heads, in terms any Furling raised on Mira would find immediately meaningful.

Stars in the archive records that are more massive than Vara are generally hotter, brighter, shorter-lived. Stars less massive are cooler, dimmer, longer-lived. Vara sits towards the lower-mass end of the range where habitable worlds have been found — not the only range, but a well-populated one.

## What the Survey Finds

The Kigal Galaxy survey, accumulated across 130,000 thara'keth of observation, documents the stellar population of the galaxy in detail. The full data is at Student access. What the visitor-level record can offer is the summary: most stars in the Kigal Galaxy are M-class, cool and red and very long-lived. K-class stars like Vara are the next most common type in the survey. G-class stars are significantly less common. Stars hotter than G are rare.

This distribution means that the conditions under which Miran life evolved — calm, orange light, stable energy output across billions of thara'keth — are not unusual in the galaxy. Many stars provide similar conditions. The archive's species catalogue reflects this: most inhabited worlds found in the survey orbit K or M stars, with the habitable zone adjusted inward for the cooler output.
