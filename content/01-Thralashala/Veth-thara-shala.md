# <span class="furling-pin">Veth'thara'shala</span> | Evolutionary Biology

> *The hardest part of understanding evolution, for students who encounter it for the first time, is that it has no goal. Selection does not move life toward something not complexity, not intelligence, not any particular form. It eliminates what fails to reproduce and retains what succeeds, in the conditions that currently exist. That is all it does. That it produces, across long enough time, organisms of extraordinary sophistication and variety, is not because it is aiming at those outcomes. It is because reproduction with variation, filtered by survival, turns out to be sufficient to produce almost anything.*
> — Vetha Omavel-Rala, Era 88

## The Mechanism

Life reproduces with variation. The reproduction part is what makes life self-continuing: new organisms are made from existing ones. The variation part is what makes evolution possible: the new organisms are not exact copies, and some of the differences are heritable they can be passed to the next generation.

Variation arises from multiple sources: errors in the copying process, the mixing of genetic material that occurs in most forms of sexual reproduction, chemical or radiation damage to information molecules, and others. Most variation has no significant effect on the organism's ability to survive and reproduce. Some is harmful. A small fraction improves the organism's fit to its current environment, making it more likely to survive and reproduce.

When variation is heritable, the variants that survive and reproduce more successfully pass their properties to more offspring. Over generations, this accumulates: the population becomes, on average, better fitted to its environment than it was. This is selection: not active choice, but the passive consequence of differential survival and reproduction.

The combination of variation and selection, operating across generations, is sufficient to produce all the diversity of life the archive documents. This claim was controversial when first proposed. It is no longer controversial. The mechanisms have been confirmed in detail in every species studied, and the pattern of relationship between all known life showing the historical connections between current species is consistent with a single branching process of variation and selection operating over very long periods.

## Time and the Depth of History

The timescales of evolution are difficult to hold intuitively. Mira has been inhabited by life for approximately 3,400 tael'kora 3.4 billion thara'keth. The earliest life was microbial, with nothing resembling the complexity of current organisms. The progression from simple to complex is not linear, and complexity is not the point many of the most successful organisms across that history have been very simple but the history of life on Mira does show, across its full length, the gradual accumulation of new forms from earlier ones.

The Furlings themselves are a recent development in this history. Our ancestors diverged from other Miran primates approximately 40,000 thara'keth ago. Our own species recognisably Furling in body plan and roughly modern cognitive capacity appeared approximately 5,000 thara'keth ago. The spacefaring civilisation that built the archive has existed for 134,000 thara'keth.

In the context of Mira's 3.4 billion thara'keth of life, we have been here for a short time. In the context of the life of the star above us, for an even shorter time. This is not a cause for existential distress. It is simply the scale of things, worth holding accurately.
