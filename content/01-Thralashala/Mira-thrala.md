# <span class="furling-pin">Mira'thrala</span> | Classical Mechanics

> *Mevathara derived the gravitational law in Era 8 from the observation that every body in the Vara'skela system moved as though something was pulling it. The something turned out to be every other body. This is the foundational insight of mechanics: nothing in the universe moves except in relation to everything else. Isolation is not a physical state. It is an approximation we use when the other relationships are too small to matter.*
> — Omira Vel-Thara, Era 91

## What Classical Mechanics Describes

Classical mechanics is the science of how objects move, why they move, and what causes them to change their motion. It operates at the scales of everyday life and the astronomical: from a tool falling from a workbench to the orbital path of Thalavar around Vara. At extreme scales — very small, very fast, or very massive — its predictions become imprecise and other frameworks are required. Within its range, it is exact.

The framework was built across the first nine eras of systematic Furling science, developed by observers who began with careful measurement and worked backward to the laws that must produce those measurements.

## The Three Laws of Motion

The core of classical mechanics is a set of relationships between force, mass, and motion that were formalised by Mevathara in Era 8–9 but had been observed in partial form by earlier scholars.

**The first law:** An object remains in its current state of motion — whether at rest or moving at constant velocity — unless a net force acts on it. This law was counterintuitive to early Furling science, because everyday observation suggested that moving things naturally stop. The insight was that this stopping is itself caused by force: friction, air resistance, the constant small pushes and pulls of a world that provides no perfect frictionless surface. In the absence of any force at all, an object would move forever.

**The second law:** The acceleration of an object is proportional to the net force applied to it and inversely proportional to its mass. More force: more acceleration. More mass: less acceleration for the same force. This relationship allows the precise prediction of motion in any system where forces can be measured or calculated.

**The third law:** When one object exerts a force on another, the second object exerts an equal and opposite force on the first. Forces do not exist alone. Every push has a corresponding push in the other direction. This is why walking is possible, why ships move through water, why a portal firing its field exerts a countervailing force on its own frame.

## Gravity

The gravitational law — that every body with mass attracts every other body with mass, with a force that depends on both masses and the distance between them — was derived by Mevathara from the orbital data Velashan and earlier observers had accumulated about the Vara'skela system.

The derivation required the recognition that Mira's moons, the planets of the system, Vara itself, and a stone dropped from a height were all subject to the same force, operating by the same law across all scales and distances. This unification — that the force that makes things fall on Mira is the same force that keeps Thalavar in orbit — was the most significant conceptual achievement of early Furling science.

The gravitational reference unit — **mira'thrala**, Mira's own surface gravitational acceleration — is defined as 1.0. All other surface gravity values are expressed as ratios. A world with a surface gravity of 0.8 mira'thrala is meaningfully less massive or larger than Mira; one at 1.4 mira'thrala is meaningfully more. These figures appear throughout the planetary records in this branch.

## Applications

Classical mechanics underlies every engineering calculation in the archive. The structural loads of bio-organic buildings are calculated with these laws. The trajectories of spacecraft — before the portal network made most interplanetary travel unnecessary — were planned using them. The geothermal tap systems draw on the mechanical properties of rock under pressure.

Portal engineering, at the deeper levels, is not classical mechanics — the physics of wormhole formation requires frameworks that did not exist in Era 9. But the physical frames of the portals themselves, the mechanical systems of the key and the dialling rings, the engineering of the structures that house them: all classical.
