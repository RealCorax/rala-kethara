# <span class="furling-pin">Meva'rala'keth</span> | How to Add Your Voice to This Place

*The archive was built to grow. When Vel'oma, Shalaveth, and I designed the structure, we spent considerable time thinking about what would happen when we were gone when the people who understood every decision we had made were no longer available to explain them. The answer we arrived at was this document. If the archive is to continue, the people who use it need to be able to contribute to it. This record explains how.*

>> This record covers the practical and philosophical standards for contributing new material to the Rala'Kethara Archive. It is addressed to any reader who has knowledge worth recording, regardless of species or background.

## What Belongs Here

The archive holds knowledge. The question of what counts as knowledge has been debated in Furling scholarly circles for longer than this archive has existed, and we resolved it for our purposes as follows: knowledge is any account of what was observed, experienced, or reasoned, presented with enough context that another reader can assess its reliability.

This definition includes the obvious categories: scientific findings, historical accounts, technical specifications, biographical records. It also includes things that are sometimes thought of as less rigorous: personal testimony, contested interpretations, things that are believed but not yet confirmed. These belong here too, clearly marked as what they are.

What does not belong here is assertion without grounding. A record that states a thing is true without explaining how the writer knows it is true, or what they observed that led them to that conclusion, does not meet the standard. The archive is not a place for belief presented as fact.

## The Marks of Uncertainty

Every record in this archive uses a small set of markers to indicate how firmly its claims are held.

A claim presented without any qualifier is understood to be well-supported: observed, documented, reproducible or verifiable, or established by enough converging evidence that further verification would be redundant.

The evidential marker <span class="furling-pin-inline">·thala</span> placed after a claim indicates genuine uncertainty. This is not a marker of weakness. It is a marker of honesty. The Furling scholarly tradition holds that a record which does not acknowledge its own uncertainty is a record that cannot be trusted, because it has not told you where to stop relying on it.

Records that present contested knowledge where multiple serious scholarly positions exist and cannot currently be reconciled should present all positions and mark the disagreement explicitly rather than selecting one interpretation.

## Practical Standards

**Attribution:** Every record should carry the name of its compiler and the era in which it was compiled. Anonymous records are accepted only where the compiler had genuine reason to withhold their name, and the reason should be stated.

**Scope:** A record should concern one thing. The archive's power comes from connection a focused record on a specific topic links to other focused records and creates a web of knowledge that is easier to navigate than a single large document covering many things. When a topic is genuinely large, it should become multiple linked records rather than one unwieldy one.

**Language:** Records may be filed in any language the archive's translation system can process. The primary language of the archive is Furling, and records in other languages are automatically cross-referenced. If your wrist device is active, it will handle translation in both directions.

**Access level:** When filing a new record, you will be asked to indicate what access level the material should carry. The default is visitor. If you have reason to recommend a higher access level because the material requires foundational knowledge to interpret, or because it touches on sensitive matters indicate this and Selavai will review the recommendation.

**Evidence:** Where possible, note how you came to know what you are recording. Direct observation, transmitted account, inference from other evidence these are different things and should be distinguished.

## A Note on What This Archive Is For

One of the three founders of this archive, Vel'oma Keth-Shala, said something at our first meeting that I have never forgotten. She said: *"I want something that serves the truth, not us. If what we knew turns out to be wrong, I want the archive to say so. If someone comes here a thousand thara'keth from now with better knowledge than we have, I want the archive to hold their knowledge at the same level as ours. No hierarchy of origin. Only quality of evidence."*

That is the standard. Whatever you know, if you know it well and can say how you know it, this archive wants it.

Speak to Selavai to begin the filing process.
