# 08.01 How This Voice Came to Be | Vel'rala'keth

*Compiled by Vel'thara Oriskan, Era 94. Revised by Oma'shala Veriskan, Era 144.*

The Rala'Kethara Archive -- Voice-Eternal -- began as a single room in a single building on Mira, approximately 130,000 thara'keth ago. The room contained clay tablets, crystal slates, and a scholar named Vel'thara Norimesh who had decided that the knowledge being accumulated across Furling civilisation needed to be gathered in one place before it was lost.

It was not a grand act. It was a practical one. Norimesh was worried.

## From One Room to Many Worlds

Over the following thara'keth, what Norimesh had started became something considerably larger. Other scholars contributed. Other rooms were added. The original building grew, was rebuilt, was expanded, burned once in a conflict and was rebuilt again from the copies that had been made. The copying of archive contents was, from the beginning, understood as essential -- a record held in only one place is a record that could be lost in a single fire.

By Era 45, there were archive branches in every major Furling city on Mira. By Era 79, the first off-world branch had been established on Vareth. By Era 112, the archive had nodes on forty-seven worlds across the Kigal Galaxy, connected by the portal network and maintained by dedicated keepers.

The Rala'Kethara in Nibiru -- this archive, the one you are reading -- was designed from its construction as a vault. Not a branch. The most complete single gathering of Furling knowledge, held in a city specifically built to preserve it, protected by the cavern's geological stability, powered by geothermal systems designed to run without maintenance for millennia.

It was built to survive the Furlings' absence.

## Why Nibiru Was Built

The decision to build Nibiru was not made in the final thara'keth before the exodus. It was made approximately 8,000 thara'keth ago, when the Furling Council first acknowledged that the conflict with the Keth'alis might not have a solution within reach, and that the preservation of Furling knowledge could not be left entirely to the distributed archive network on worlds that might be lost.

The location was chosen for inaccessibility and geological stability. The construction took centuries. The sealing of the archive and the configuration of the Selavai system were completed in the final thara'keth before the evacuation.

Three thara'keth before the city was sealed, the final archivist entry was made. The navigation systems were activated. The access tiers were locked. The doors were closed.

The Furlings left. The archive remained.

## What This Archive Is and Is Not

This archive is the most complete gathering of Furling knowledge ever assembled in a single location. It holds approximately 4,000 fully written records and tens of thousands of documented stubs, indexes, and cross-references.

It is not complete. No archive of 150,000 thara'keth of civilisation's knowledge could be. There are gaps -- things that were lost in the Era 12 war, things that were never documented, things that were documented on other worlds that may no longer be accessible. These gaps are documented honestly in [[08.04 What This Voice Has Not Yet Said]].

It ends at the moment of the exodus. Everything that has happened in the Kigal Galaxy in the 4,460 thara'keth since then is absent. That absence is the most significant gap in the archive, and it is documented separately at [[08.05 The Silence of Five Thousand Years]].
