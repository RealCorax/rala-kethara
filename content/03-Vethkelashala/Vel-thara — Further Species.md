# <span class="furling-pin">Veth'rala — Vel'shala</span> | Vel'thara — Further Species

> *"There is no right order for introducing minds to each other. I have arranged these by the quality of the encounter rather than chronology — by what each species taught us rather than when we met them. I hope this serves a new reader better than a list of dates."*

>> Brief profiles of eleven additional species encountered across the Kigal Galaxy. Full biological and historical records are held at Student access and above. What appears here is enough to know who they are.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Chorathi</span>

Insectoid. A caste-structured civilisation organised around Resonants — a small sub-group capable of producing and interpreting complex harmonic frequencies that function as both language and social coordination for the entire colony. A Chorathi colony without its Resonant caste does not function. Their history with the Furlings is long, complex, and includes a period of significant conflict. The detailed record is held at Student access. Navigation note: the inner systems of the Kigal Galaxy's third arm are Chorathi territory. The boundary was established by formal agreement and is not to be crossed without full awareness of what you are doing. Status at archive seal: unknown — contact was lost before the exodus.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Velhari</span>

Gaseous consciousness. Distributed. Exists within planetary atmospheres as a collective of conscious particles — not a single entity, but not simply a cloud. The Furlings caused catastrophic and irreversible harm to a Velhari population during the early expansion period, before we had identified them as sentient. What followed was one of the most painful records in this archive. The Velhari refused all contact after the incident and named us world-destroyers. We complied with their demand that we leave, immediately and permanently. The zone around their world is marked in all Furling charts as Vel'thala — the guarded life. Navigation: absolute prohibition. No exceptions. We owe them that, forever.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Keshari</span>

Nomadic. Travel in large family groups across multiple worlds using their own early portal technology — of independent origin, predating Furling contact. The Furlings found the Keshari already moving between worlds when we encountered them, which was unusual. They are diplomatic peers rather than uplift candidates — they had developed their own interstellar capability independently. Trade-focused culture, widespread across a significant portion of the Kigal Galaxy's mid-systems. At the archive seal they remained mobile and widespread.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Threnai</span>

Arboreal. Small, quick, with exceptional spatial memory. Live in the canopy layers of dense forest worlds. Their civilisation is distributed — no cities, no fixed centres, but a network of knowledge-keeping sites spread across thousands of kilometres of canopy. The Furlings found their approach to knowledge preservation philosophically fascinating and incorporated elements of it into early archive design. What they taught us about distributed record-keeping is visible in how this archive is structured.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Moravel</span>

Silicon-based. One of the few non-carbon life forms the Furlings formally contacted. Long-lived to a degree that made communication difficult — their subjective experience of time is vastly slower than most species. We spent several thara'keth developing communication methods before meaningful exchange was possible. Their knowledge of geological and crystalline processes is without parallel in the archive. The patience required for that communication taught us something about the assumption that speed equals intelligence.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Ohvathi</span>

Aquatic. Evolved in deep-ocean environments with no natural light. Their sensory biology relies entirely on pressure sensitivity and chemical detection — no visual system in any conventional sense. Their understanding of their world is so different from most species that early contact required us to learn to think in a different shape. The Furling scholar Vel'oma Mir-Keth described this contact as more philosophically disorienting than any other encounter in their career, and the most valuable for exactly that reason.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Rethani</span>

Avian. Fast-moving, with exceptional pattern recognition. Their civilisation was oral at contact — no written tradition. We worked with them to develop a recording system compatible with their biology and culture. The result was a music-based archive system that influenced Furling thinking about non-written knowledge preservation. The Rethani contribution to the archive exists entirely as sound.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Selvori</span>

Warm-blooded, broadly similar in physical type to Furlings but larger and slower-moving. A contemplative species. Had developed a sophisticated ethical philosophy independently — one the Furlings studied with great interest, because it reached several conclusions similar to the <span class="furling-pin-inline">Kora'thesh Veshúnar</span> by entirely different reasoning. The comparative philosophy section of the archive has extensive Selvori content. This convergence — two species in different parts of the same galaxy arriving at similar ethical conclusions independently — is one of the archive's more interesting open questions.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Nahari</span>

Colony organism. Individual members are small but form larger coordinated structures when working together. Exceptionally capable engineers. Their construction of habitat structures using only biological materials, before any Furling contact, produced results comparable to early Furling architecture. They are one of the species we describe as having already understood something we took a long time to learn — that living material is more durable than dead material.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Mirathi</span>

Twin-evolved. The Mirathi are not a single species but two that exisited on the same world in a state of biological interdependence each producing compounds the other requires. They are inseparable not by social preference but by biology. Their civilisation is built entirely around this duality. The Furlings found them philosophically instructive on the subject of dependency, cooperation, and what it means for two different things to be genuinely necessary to each other.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Thalveni</span>

Subterranean. Evolved in deep cave systems with no surface-dwelling tradition. Their technology developed around geothermal energy and crystal acoustics — they communicate partially through vibration in stone. The Furlings found significant overlap between Thalveni crystal-resonance techniques and our own crystal-lattice memory storage. A productive exchange resulted that improved both systems. The Thalveni remain one of the more accessible species for Furling researchers to collaborate with, because we share a foundational vocabulary around crystal systems.
