# <span class="furling-pin">Vel'veth'rala</span> | The First Record of Every Living Kind

> *"I have spent the last twelve thara'keth trying to find the right word for what this list is. It is not a catalogue of things we discovered. It is a record of minds we encountered. There is a difference. I wanted to name it correctly before I filed it."*

>> This index records every intelligent species formally documented by the Furling civilisation. It is not complete. The full record is held at deeper access levels. What appears here is the visitor selection — species whose records are safe to share openly, and whose basic details are appropriate for a new arrival to know.

## Contact Types

Each species in this record is assigned a contact classification based on the nature of our relationship with them at the time of last contact.

**Uplifted** — Species formally supported by the Furling uplift programme. Extended, structured assistance with scientific and social development. Four species reached this classification.

**Peer** — Species encountered as civilisational equals. No uplift offered or requested. Diplomatic relationship maintained as equals. The Keshari are the clearest example.

**Observed** — Species detected and catalogued but never formally contacted. Records exist of their existence and general characteristics only.

**Alliance** — The three other members of the Four Great Races: the Alterans, the Asgard, and the Nox. A category apart.

**Complex** — Species whose relationship with us was significant and difficult. The Chorathi and Velhari fall here for different reasons.

## A Note on Completeness

This index represents a fraction of the species the Furlings encountered and documented. The full catalogue is held at deeper access levels. Some entries are withheld entirely — not because they are secret, but because naming them openly in a publicly accessible record would expose them to risk. Selavai holds those names. They are preserved.

The archive seal date means we have no knowledge of events after the exodus. Species whose status reads "stable at last contact" may have changed significantly in the 4,460 thara'keth since we left. We cannot know. What we recorded is what was true when we recorded it.
