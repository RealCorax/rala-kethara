# <span class="furling-pin">Vel'hala Kigal</span> | The Three Who Held This Sky Together

> *"The greater alliance reached across galaxies, and I have written of it elsewhere with something close to wonder. This one I write of differently. It reached only as far as our own sky, and its three members stood closer to us than the four ever did, in age, in temperament, in the plain fact of sharing a single galaxy's worth of stars. That is, I think, why its ending sits heavier in me than the other. A thing that far away can fade. A thing that close has to be broken."*
> — Archivist Shela Vorn-Mirath, personal note, Era 146

>> The Kigal Alliance joined the three peoples of this galaxy who had reached comparable heights of advancement and held comparable convictions about what that advancement was for: the Furlings, the Vorah, and the Lumenari. This record covers the alliance at visitor level. The diplomatic archive, including the final Council transmissions of the war period, is held with it.

## What It Was

This alliance is not the Oma'vara'veth, the alliance of four great races recorded elsewhere in this branch, and we ask the reader not to fold the two together. That alliance joined civilisations of immense age, scattered across several galaxies, whose paths had diverged so far from our own that partnership with them was itself a kind of achievement. This alliance was closer to home in every sense. The Vorah and the Lumenari were not our elders, as the Alterans were. They were our contemporaries, matched to us in the reach of what we could each build, and matched to us, we believed, in what we each thought that reach was owed to.

We had sat at a table of four and learned what such a table could hold. We wished our own galaxy to have one, built among peoples who were not simply capable, but who wanted the same things done with that capability.

We write what follows knowing exactly what this record is. It is written by the hand of a people who did not save what it describes.

## The Three Peoples

**The Furlings** were the educators and mediators, as we were in the greater alliance. Our contribution was the portal network binding the member worlds, and the teaching we extended to any of the three willing to receive it. When the Vorah and Lumenari disagreed, which happened rarely but did happen, Furling representatives were usually the ones who found the language to bring both back to the table. This role suited us. It also meant we carried a share of the alliance's friction that the other two, by temperament, rarely created themselves.

**The Vorah** valued discovery and peace in equal measure, and treated the two as a single conviction rather than as separate goals held in balance. They did nothing of consequence alone. Vorah families and communities held bonds across many generations, and a Vorah faced with a decision would gather kin, colleagues, or fellow travellers before acting rather than trust the judgement of one mind alone. This extended to the wider people: the Vorah moved and worked in groups as a matter of course, and solitary action was treated as a last resort rather than a virtue. They possessed the means to end almost any dispute by force, however we never once saw them reach for it first. Their First Voice held one of the three permanent seats of the Alliance Council, and no Vorah commitment made at that table was, in our record, ever broken.

In the era of the greater alliance, a company of Vorah volunteers accompanied Furling explorers through the Astra Porta into the Alteran home galaxy, at a time when our two peoples still travelled together freely between the two great networks. They settled there, on a world rich with animal life across its land and its waters, and made a home of it. What became of that settlement afterward is not recorded here.

**The Lumenari** turned their considerable gift for craft toward a single deep question: the nature of time itself, and how far a people might learn to hold it, slow it, or turn it toward their own purpose. We did not come to understand this pursuit as fully as we would have liked, but what we observed of it told us it was no small ambition, and that they pursued it with the same precision they brought to everything else. Their observers were trusted by every party in every dispute the alliance held, because a Lumenari record contains only what was seen, nothing supposed and nothing assumed.

Three peoples, matched in reach, and united, we believed, in purpose. We found in both of them partners we trusted fully, which is not a thing we say of every people this archive records.

## What the Alliance Built

The alliance maintained the portal network across its member worlds and beyond, extended the reach of Furling teaching to peoples who had not yet built portals of their own, and stood as the nearest thing this galaxy had to a shared conscience. Its founding statement bound the three of us to a wider purpose than our own advancement: that the younger peoples of this galaxy would be helped, protected, and welcomed toward partnership as they grew. Many smaller peoples stood with the alliance in this way, as friends and lesser allies of the three, receiving teaching and the protection of their boundaries, with the stated intent that in time some would take a full seat of their own.

None reached that point. We record this without laying the failing at their feet. It was time that was taken from them, as it was taken from us.

## The Accord

The founding Accord, the Vetha'rala'oma, the Living Statement of Peace, was written three times, once in each member tongue, and no version was called the original. Each people bound itself in its own words, and the three texts stood together in every Council hall the alliance kept.

We hold the Furling text here in full.
