# <span class="furling-pin">Athela'kinara</span> | Vel'thara — The Athel'kin

> *"I am aware that a survey report is not the place for this, but I find I cannot write an accurate account of what we observed without including it: these people are extraordinarily beautiful. Not in appearance, though they are that too, in their way. In what they make. Their cities are not built — they are composed."*

>> The Athel'kin were discovered by a Furling long-range observation team in Era 110. This record covers who they were at the time of first contact and through the period of the Furling-Athel'kin partnership. What occurred after that partnership ended is held at deeper access levels.

## Who They Were

The Athel'kin were a peaceful, nomadic, pre-industrial species organised into five Houses. Their natural lifespan was 30-40 thara'keth — intelligent and creative, but losing accumulated knowledge with every generation. Every cycle of elders dying took irreplaceable wisdom with it. They were perpetually racing against their own mortality.

Despite this constraint, they were not a primitive people. By the time the Furlings found them they had developed sophisticated tools, basic metallurgy, early written language, trade networks between the five Houses, strong oral traditions, and a deep knowledge of medicine — particularly among the Ngo'verath (Forest People). They had simply reached the ceiling of what a short lifespan permits you to build. Every ambitious project was inherited, abandoned, and restarted by the next generation, who had to rediscover what their predecessors already knew.

Teaching children was the highest cultural value across all five Houses. Knowledge preservation was not merely important — it was survival.

## Physical Appearance

The Athel'kin were humanoid. Somewhat shorter and lighter in build than the Furlings, with proportionally larger eyes well-adapted for low-light conditions. Their skin ranged across warm earth tones — ochre, brown, deep amber — with subtle patterning across the shoulders and upper arms that darkened or lightened with emotional state, though this was not bioluminescent. Hair was typically dark, worn practically across all Houses except the Ngahzoka, who maintained elaborate ceremonial styles. Their hands had four fingers and a thumb, proportionally longer than Furling hands, which contributed to the exceptional fine detail of their craft work.

## The Five Houses

The Athel'kin were not a unified species in the political sense. They were five distinct cultures that shared a homeworld and a broad philosophical tradition of non-violence and knowledge-sharing, but who governed themselves separately and had their own distinct characters.

**Rrozok — the Ridge Walkers.** Direct, efficient, practical. Organised around a single strong leader whose authority was earned through demonstrated capability. The most technically sophisticated House at contact — their metalwork was exceptional.

**Ngahzoka — the Water People.** Ceremonial, deeply concerned with memory and continuity. Governed by a hereditary blood dynasty, which was an exception to Athel'kin norms. The Ngahzoka were the primary keepers of oral history — their memory traditions were elaborate and precise, capable of preserving complex knowledge across multiple generations without written record.

**Geirroh — the Sky People.** Open, curious, collective. Governed by a Knowing Circle — the twelve individuals judged to hold the broadest understanding of the world at any given time. The Geirroh produced the most varied art of any House and were the first to develop a formal written language, which they shared freely with the other four Houses.

**Zrohga — the Fire People.** Practical and survival-focused, living in harsher terrain than the other Houses. Leadership earned through demonstrated capacity rather than inherited or appointed. The Zrohga had the most developed knowledge of geology and mineral resources, and their engineering traditions were robust and innovative.

**Ngo'verath — the Forest People.** The smallest and most distinctive House. Semi-nomadic within a dense ancient forest they knew intimately. Other Houses told children's stories about them: do not wander toward the still forest, the Ngo'verath will watch you from the trees. Not violent stories — the Ngo'verath in those tales simply knew things about you before you spoke. Their knowledge of medicine and plant systems was extraordinary. The Furling survey team specifically noted that Ngo'verath healers had identified and catalogued compounds unknown to Furling medicine at the time of contact.

## Their Art

The quality of Athel'kin creative work was the feature of the initial survey report that received the most commentary. Forty thara'keth of observation records contain more references to their art than to any other single characteristic.

Their cities were not built — they were composed. Public spaces were not open areas between functional buildings but deliberate works in themselves, designed to be inhabited and experienced. The Athel'kin had developed a tradition of art-making that assumed the work would outlast the maker by many generations. Everything was built for the grandchildren of grandchildren who would live under it, pass through it, or hold it in their hands.

This was, the survey team concluded, a direct response to the brevity of their lives. When you know you will not see a work completed, you learn to make it beautiful at every stage of incompleteness. When you know you will not survive to see whether your ideas were right, you make the ideas as honest as you can and trust the people who come after.
