# <span class="furling-pin">Oma'shala'veth — Vel'thara</span> | The Four Uplifted Species

> *"Each of these species was already remarkable before we arrived. What we offered was not a gift of capability — it was a gift of time and contact. What they did with those things was entirely their own."*

>> Four species received the formal Furling uplift — sustained, structured support with scientific and social development — during the expansion era. This record introduces each of them at visitor level. Full biological and historical profiles are held at deeper access levels.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Shal'kora</span> — the Communal People

The Shal'kora are amphibious, evolved on a world of shallow inland seas and dense riverine forest. They do not thrive alone — communal living is not a preference for them but a biological reality. Their social structures are built around large extended family groups who share not just space but decision-making, care, and memory.

Before Furling contact they had developed sophisticated percussion-based communication across water, capable of transmitting complex meaning over long distances. This communication system was, in certain technical respects, more precise than early Furling long-range communication methods. The Furlings studied it with genuine interest.

The uplift partnership gave the Shal'kora access to energy systems compatible with their aquatic world, and to a broader community of other minds — which proved as transformative for them as the technology. Their mastery of water-based energy systems became one of their defining contributions to the wider alliance network. At the time of the archive seal they were thriving across multiple worlds, all of them water-rich.

The Furling scholar Oma'vel Keth-Shala, who worked with the Shal'kora for eighteen thara'keth, noted: *"They taught us as much as we taught them. I believe this is what a successful uplift looks like."*

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Vethari</span> — the Long-Thinkers

The Vethari are desert-dwelling, and long-lived by nature — their natural lifespan was approximately 180-200 thara'keth even before Furling contact, which made them unusual. They are predominantly solitary intellectuals. Their written philosophical tradition was already extensive and sophisticated when the Furlings found them: several centuries of careful, accumulated thought on questions that the Furlings had been wrestling with for far longer.

What uplift offered them was not a longer life or better technology. It was contact with other minds.

The Vethari had been thinking alone for a very long time. Meeting other species — the Furlings first, then the wider network — transformed their work in ways their own historians describe as comparable to a second emergence. The encounter became the defining event of Vethari intellectual history. They are now renowned for environmental engineering and communal architecture: a particular irony, noted with affection by Vethari historians, given that they began as a solitary desert species.

At the archive seal they were stable on a single primary world with several research outposts.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Lúmenari</span> — the Light-Speakers

The Lúmenari are ocean-dwelling and bioluminescent. Their primary mode of communication is light-based — complex patterns produced across their bodies, precise enough to convey subtle meaning and nuanced emotional state simultaneously. When the Furlings made contact they discovered that Lúmenari light-communication was, in certain respects, more sophisticated than the Furling equivalent. The formal diplomatic exchange required us to develop translation tools capable of reading something our biology could not produce.

They are photosynthetic in part, deriving some energy directly from light. This shapes their culture's relationship to darkness, depth, and enclosure in ways that are difficult to translate but present in almost everything they make. Lúmenari art involves light as a medium in itself — not as illumination, but as material.

Their worldview is deeply ecological. They understand their ocean as a living system of which they are a part, not an environment they inhabit. This perspective was philosophically instructive for the Furlings in ways that influenced our approach to planetary ecology for generations. At the archive seal they were a stable coastal-world civilisation.

## <span class="furling-pin-inline">Aethen</span> — the Turned-Around

The Aethen are terrestrial, and their story is the one we discuss most carefully when we talk about what uplift is for.

Before Furling contact they had come close to destroying themselves through sustained internal conflict. The damage to their civilisation, their ecosystems, and their population was severe. The Furlings observed for a full generation before making contact — long enough to see what had happened and to see what the surviving Aethen chose to do about it.

What they chose was honest reckoning. Not revision of history, not reassignment of blame to those who could not argue back, but a sustained, painful, generation-spanning process of acknowledging what had happened and deciding, as a species, what they intended to become instead. The Furlings made contact when that process had produced genuine change rather than the performance of change.

The uplift programme was the most extended of any species — spanning multiple generations. What the Aethen became after that process is considered by many Furling scholars to be the most remarkable transformation in the archive's record. A species that had nearly destroyed itself through internal war became one of the most committed advocates of cooperative civilisation in the Kigal Galaxy. At the archive seal they were thriving across multiple worlds, active participants in the alliance network that connected dozens of species.
