# <span class="furling-pin">Omavel Thren</span> | Healer and Medical Educator

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 61–128 · Mira, Omavel Settlement · Medicine, Medical Philosophy, Cross-Species Practice

## Life and Work

Omavel Thren trained as a healer during one of the periods of heaviest cross-species medical activity in Furling history: the Era 60–80 expansion of the visitor programme, when Nibiru was receiving more species from more diverse biologies than at any previous point. The medical challenges this created were considerable. Furling medicine had developed over thousands of thara'keth with Furling biology as its reference point; applying its principles to species whose physiology was fundamentally different required both adaptability and a willingness to acknowledge the limits of existing knowledge.

Omavel was, by most accounts, exceptionally well-suited to both requirements. They had a temperament that found genuine interest in uncertainty rather than anxiety, and an approach to patients of any species that began, always, with the question of what the patient was experiencing rather than what the healer expected to find. This approach produced better outcomes in cross-species practice than the more systematic approaches of their contemporaries, and Omavel spent considerable effort in the middle period of their career trying to articulate why — an effort that eventually produced the foundational medical philosophy record that still stands as the visitor-level introduction to this section.

They practised in Mira's central medical district for forty thara'keth before relocating to Nibiru in Era 99, where they worked both as a healer and as the primary educator for the medical training programme that prepared Furling healers for work with visiting species. Their teaching was, by the testimony of many students across many generations, transformative in a specific way: Omavel had the ability to make a student feel that the patient in front of them was the most important person in the galaxy, and that this was simply a fact, not an attitude to be cultivated.

They died in Era 128, at sixty-seven thara'keth, of a cardiac event. Several of their students noted, in the archive's personal records section, that they found it difficult to reconcile the healer who had taught them to tend every life with the fact that his own had ended without warning. Omavel would likely have found this exactly correct.
