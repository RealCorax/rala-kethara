# <span class="furling-pin">Reva Naleth</span> | Composer, Archivist, and Portal Guide

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 107–141 · Skela'omara, Mira · Music, Archive Education, Portal Technology

## Life and Work

Reva Naleth was many things to the people who knew them, and the archive holds records of several of those things, in different branches, for different reasons. To the Vara'kela, they were a composer of significance. To the Meva'shala, they were the person who wrote the clearest visitor guide to portal travel ever produced. To the individuals who travelled alongside them during the exodus, they were something harder to reduce to a category.

Reva grew up in Skela'omara, a mid-sized city on Mira's western continent, in a household that treated music and practical knowledge as equally serious pursuits. Their parent-cohort included an atmospheric engineer and two musicians, and Reva absorbed both traditions without much apparent tension between them. By their eighteenth thara'keth they had already composed several works for community ceremonies and completed the first stage of a formal education in archival technology, which at the time required a working knowledge of crystal-lattice systems, portal addressing, and multi-species communication interfaces.

The combination turned out to be unusual enough to be useful. Reva spent Era 125–135 working as an education contributor to the Rala'Kethara, developing visitor-facing materials for the practical knowledge branch. Their approach to this work was shaped by their experience as a performer: audiences, they believed, understand things they can feel before they understand things they are told. The portal guide produced in Era 143 reflects this directly — it begins not with technical specifications but with a memory of what the first crossing felt like.

In their parallel life as a composer, Reva produced three works of lasting significance. The last of these, *Vetha'mai Nibiru* — Life-Greetings, Nibiru — was composed and performed in the city's main performance hall in Era 140, with four hundred Furlings present. It was understood at the time to be a farewell of a particular kind: not a grief-song, but something more precise. Reva's own description was that they wanted to say "we were here; this was real; come back."

Reva did not survive the exodus. They died in transit before reaching the Milky Way. The timing of their death, and the circumstances, are recorded in personal logs held at Student access. The archive record notes only: *died in transit, Era 141, age 34 thara'keth.*

The portal guide remains. The music remains, held in crystal in the Vara'kela. Both were written with the knowledge that Reva might not be there when they were needed, and both were written to be useful anyway.

> [!arc-note] On the Portal Guide's Date
> The portal guide is listed as Era 143, which is two thara'keth after Reva's death. The record was compiled posthumously by their archival colleagues from notes and teaching materials Reva had produced over many thara'keth of visitor education work. The voice in the record is entirely Reva's — the colleagues who assembled it made this explicit in their editorial note, held at Student access.
