# <span class="furling-pin">Vel Shala-Omaven</span> | Educator and Philosopher of Teaching

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 79–143 · Mira, then multiple worlds · Education Philosophy, Uplift Programme, Cross-Species Teaching

## Life and Work

Vel Shala-Omaven is perhaps the most widely read contributor to the Rala'Kethara's teaching section, and one of the most openly uncomfortable with that distinction. They spent a significant portion of their later career insisting, in lectures, personal correspondence, and archive notes, that the philosophy of teaching was not a subject that benefited from having authorities, and that the influence of any single thinker on how a civilisation understood education was probably a symptom of something that needed examining.

This was not false modesty. Vel had arrived, through decades of work across multiple worlds and species, at a genuine conviction that the most dangerous teaching was the kind people trusted completely. Their record for this section ends with an honest accounting of what went wrong with the Athel'kin uplift, and the inclusion of that accounting was their own insistence. Several colleagues on the archive education committee felt it was beyond the scope of a visitor-level record. Vel's position was that the failure was exactly the scope: if you were going to teach people the principles of teaching, you were obligated to show them what those principles had failed to prevent.

Vel trained initially on Mira in educational theory, then spent fifteen thara'keth travelling as part of the contact teams that worked with newly encountered species during the mid-era expansion. This period, by their own account, was the most formative of their career: seeing the gap between how Furling teachers understood themselves and how they were experienced by the species they worked with produced a permanent discomfort that they channelled into their writing.

They settled at Nibiru in Era 112, working in the archive's education division and teaching visiting scholars who had reached sufficient access levels to engage with pedagogical theory. They were still working when the exodus began. They chose to be among the last to leave, ensuring that the education division's records were complete and properly indexed before they went. Their fate after departure from Nibiru is not recorded.

## On Reading Their Work

Vel Shala-Omaven wrote to be disagreed with. Their records consistently present positions in a form that invites challenge rather than acceptance. Students who worked with them report that expressing agreement without argument was the one response that genuinely frustrated Vel. "Tell me why you think I am right," they reportedly said on multiple occasions. "That is much harder than telling me why I am wrong, and much more useful."
