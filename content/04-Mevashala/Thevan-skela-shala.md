# <span class="furling-pin">Thevan'skela'shala</span> | How We Learned to Cross the Dark

> *The first time I dialled a portal to a world I had never seen, I stood at the threshold for longer than I should admit. Not from fear, exactly. From the understanding that on the other side of that doorway was a distance that would take a lifetime to cross by any other means, and I was about to cross it in a single step. That never entirely stopped feeling like something worth pausing for.*
> — Field note, compiler unknown, Era 67

## What a Portal Is

A Furling portal is a doorway between two fixed points in space. Step through one side and emerge from the other — regardless of the distance between them. The journey takes no more time than crossing a room. Distance is irrelevant. What matters is that both ends exist, both are powered, and you know the address of where you are going.

This is not travel. It is arrival.

A Furling portal is a standing structure — roughly circular, grown from bio-organic material. No two are identical. They share a family resemblance: organic curves, a faintly luminescent surface, the quiet hum of a system in readiness. Each carries the character of the world it was grown on. The dialling mechanism is mounted to the right side of the portal frame, integrated into the structure itself rather than placed separately.

## How the Network Began

We did not begin here.

The first Furling portals were short-range, built during the reconstruction after Era 12, connecting two points on the same world or between a world and its nearest moon. They were urgent tools for a people rebuilding after near-extinction. They worked. They were not enough.

The problem was range. The physics of connecting two close points could not simply be extended outward. We were still working on this when, in Era 14, a Furling deep-range survey vessel encountered an Alteran seed ship in the outer reaches of the Kigal Galaxy.

The Alterans had solved the problem we were working on. Their network spanned galaxies. They shared their mathematics with us — the seven-point addressing system, the way of describing a position in space with enough precision that a wormhole can find it across any distance. We did not build a copy of their network. We built our own, using our materials and our methods, extended to interstellar range by their addressing principles. The Furling portal network in the Kigal Galaxy is the result of that collaboration. Something neither species would have built alone.

## How to Dial

At the centre of the dialling mechanism is a key socket. Every Furling portal requires a key to dial outward from. The key activates the portal's power draw and reveals the dialling rings.

**Inserting the key** makes the inner ring of twelve symbols visible on the dialling face.

**Pressing the first side button** projects a middle ring of eleven further symbols holographically around the first.

**Pressing the second side button** projects an outer ring of twelve more symbols beyond that.

All three rings together present the complete thirty-five symbols of the Furling portal network for the Kigal Galaxy.

To dial: rotate the key so its tip points to each destination symbol in sequence, pressing the key inward to lock each one. After six destination symbols are entered, pull the key outward. If the address is valid and the destination portal is active, the wormhole opens.

Those carrying a <span class="furling-pin-inline">rala'vetha'skal</span> — a wrist device — can dial remotely without a physical key. The device stores addresses in crystal memory and transmits a dialling sequence to any portal within range.

## The Address

Every portal address is seven symbols: six to describe the destination, one to identify the point of origin.

The six destination symbols triangulate a position in space using stellar reference points distributed across the Kigal Galaxy — the same addressing principle developed with the Alterans. The origin symbol identifies which portal is dialling.

The thirty-five symbols used in Furling addresses are called the <span class="furling-pin-inline">Vara'thara'nal</span> — the Star-Cycle-Marks. Each corresponds to a recognisable stellar formation visible from the core of the Kigal Galaxy, and each has a short spoken sound drawn from the roots of our own language. An address spoken aloud is not simply a sequence of navigation codes. It carries meaning.

A Furling who knows an address will often pause, when they first learn it, to notice what it says.

The complete symbol reference is recorded in the companion document to this record. See [[Rala-skela-thael|Rala'skela'thael — the Vara'thara'nal]].

## The Network Today

At the time this record was last updated, the network comprised several hundred active portals across the Kigal Galaxy — on Furling-settled worlds, the worlds of species we worked alongside, and at waypoint installations in deep space built only to extend the network's reach.

The network was not disabled when we left. It was left running.

Portals drawing power from local geothermal or stellar sources will still be active. Portals that depended on our maintenance may have gone dark. We do not know which portals remain open. That knowledge requires someone to travel the network and look.

If you are reading this and you are not Furling: the network is yours to use. The addresses in this archive were recorded for exactly this reason. We left them here because we intended for someone to find them.
