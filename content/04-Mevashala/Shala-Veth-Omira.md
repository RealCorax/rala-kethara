# <span class="furling-pin">Shala Veth-Omira</span> | Architect and Teacher of Building

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 51–119 · Mira, then Nibiru · Architectural Philosophy, Bio-Organic Construction Theory

## Life and Work

Shala Veth-Omira was not a builder in the practical sense. They never personally cultivated a wall or placed a crystal integration. What they were was a thinker about buildings — about what buildings are for, what they do to the people inside them, what it means to make a space that participates in a life rather than simply containing it. This was considered, in their early career, a somewhat impractical specialisation. By Era 80, when their influence on a generation of working architects had become undeniable, the assessment had shifted.

They trained initially as a conventional architect, which gave them the technical foundation their later philosophical work required. The pivot came in Era 55, when they were assigned to a project designing visiting quarters for a species whose sensory experience of space was significantly different from Furling norms — a species that navigated primarily through acoustic resonance rather than vision, for whom the sound of a space was more defining than its appearance. Designing for this species required Shala to think, for the first time, about what a building actually is to the being inside it, rather than what it looks like from outside.

The answer they reached, and spent the next sixty thara'keth developing, was that a building is primarily an experience rather than an object — and that designing for experience, rather than for appearance or structural correctness, required a fundamentally different starting position. Their published lectures from Era 65–85 are considered the theoretical foundation of Furling architectural philosophy in the late era.

The student who most famously implemented these ideas was Shela Vareth, who designed Nibiru itself. The record Shala contributed to the archive in Era 88 was written explicitly as a companion to Nibiru's construction: an explanation of the principles that the city embodies, intended for the visitors who would one day walk through it.

Shala died in Era 119, in Nibiru, at sixty-eight thara'keth.
