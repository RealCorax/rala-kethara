# <span class="furling-pin">Shaleth Omavar</span> | Scholar of Applied Philosophy

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 73–136 · Mira, Miravel Plateau Region · Applied Philosophy, Technology Ethics

## Life and Work

Shaleth Omavar was born in Era 73 in the Miravel Plateau settlements — the same region that had produced many of the architects involved in Nibiru's construction two generations before. They showed, from an early age, an interest that sat awkwardly between the practical and the theoretical: not quite satisfied by pure philosophy, not quite satisfied by engineering, happiest in the space between the two where a question like "does a doorway change the person who walks through it" had to be taken seriously by both sides.

They studied at the Shala'kela institutes on Mira for forty thara'keth, producing work that was, by their own description, considered "inconveniently persistent" by their colleagues. Their central argument — that every designed object teaches its user something about what matters, and that this teaching is as much the engineer's responsibility as the object's function — was not controversial in principle. Applied to actual engineering decisions, it generated significant debate for most of their working life.

In Era 118, Shaleth spent two thara'keth as a visiting contributor to the Rala'Kethara, compiling what became the foundational visitor-level record for section 04.01. The record draws together arguments developed across four decades of teaching and practical consultation with engineering teams across multiple worlds. It is, in Shaleth's own words, "an attempt to say clearly something that Furlings have always known but rarely put down."

They returned to Mira in Era 120 and continued teaching until Era 134, when increasing mobility difficulties led to a transition to archival consultation work. They died in Era 136 at 63 thara'keth — a relatively early death, attributed to a systemic condition that had troubled them since early adulthood. Students who attended their final teaching sessions report that they remained entirely themselves until the end: curious, rigorous, and constitutionally incapable of letting an interesting question pass without worrying at it.

## Selected Works

- *Vel'thevan'shala* — the foundational philosophy record for this branch (Era 118)
- *Thevan vel'oma* — an extended essay on intention-responsive design (Era 97)
- *Rala vel'thevan* — a collected volume of lectures on tool ethics across sixty thara'keth of teaching (Era 129)

## A Note on Their Teaching Style

Colleagues and students consistently describe Shaleth as a teacher who believed the best questions were the ones that made you slightly uncomfortable. Their archive record reflects this: it does not reassure the reader that Furling technology is good. It explains what Furling technology was designed to do, and then asks the reader to decide whether those choices were sound. Shaleth considered this the only honest way to teach anything.
