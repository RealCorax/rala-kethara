# <span class="furling-pin">Thevan'vetha'shala</span> | Making With Living Things

> *My students always want to know whether a living wall can feel pain. I have been asked this question across forty thara'keth of teaching. My answer has not changed: you are asking the right question, and the answer is complicated, and that is exactly why it matters.*
> — Vel Omathis, Era 131

>> This record covers the foundational principles of bio-organic technology: what it is, how it works, and what it requires of those who use it. Specific applications — architecture, medicine, city systems — are covered in their own sections within this branch.

## What We Mean When We Say Living

<span class="furling-pin-inline">Thevan'vetha</span> is not a category of materials. It is a category of relationships.

Every tool, structure, or system built on bio-organic principles is in an ongoing relationship with its environment. It draws from that environment, returns to it, responds to changes in it. A bio-organic wall breathes. It metabolises. It has, at some level, needs — light, trace minerals, water in the case of coral-mycelium constructions, specific atmospheric compositions for others. These needs are not obstacles. They are the mechanism through which the system maintains itself.

The foundational material in most of our constructed bio-organic technology is a hybrid we call <span class="furling-pin-inline">keth'vetha'mira</span> — roughly "eternal-life-ground," though the formal engineering term is more precise. It was developed over many generations beginning in Era 23, combining structural elements of deep-crystal mineral formations with living mycelial networks capable of penetrating and integrating with almost any substrate. The resulting composite is stronger than stone under most load conditions, completely self-repairing for minor damage, and capable of slow adaptive reshaping over long periods when the environmental conditions around it change.

Nibiru's walls are largely made from this material. The structures you are moving through are four-and-a-half thousand thara'keth old and have sustained no significant structural failure in that time. The mycelial networks are still active.

## Crystal-Biology Interface

The second major component of most Furling technology is the crystal-lattice system, which is covered in its own record. What belongs here is the relationship between crystal and living tissue, because that relationship is where our most sophisticated technology lives.

Crystals, in our engineering framework, function as both structural elements and information carriers. Living tissue responds to crystal-carried signals in ways that can be guided and refined across generations of development. The junction between the two is the foundation of our diagnostic medical systems, our memory storage, our intent-responsive activation, and several dozen other applications.

The short version: a crystal can carry a signal of extraordinary precision. Living tissue can receive, interpret, and respond to that signal in ways that no constructed mechanism can match for nuance. Engineering at this junction is difficult — the signal has to be calibrated for the specific biology receiving it, which means every major application requires custom adaptation. The wrist devices worn by Furling travellers are calibrated to the individual. They will not function well for anyone else without recalibration.

## What Bio-Organic Technology Requires

The honest accounting of bio-organic technology includes what it asks of its users and its builders.

It requires patience. Living systems grow at the rate they grow. You cannot rush a coral-mycelium wall. You can guide it, encourage it, create conditions that accelerate certain processes, but the fundamental timescale is biological, not mechanical. Our cities took generations to build not because we lacked the workforce but because the buildings needed time to become what they were.

It requires ongoing relationship. A bio-organic system that is genuinely neglected will, eventually, fail — not suddenly, but gradually, as the living components find less and less to work with. Nibiru has been managing itself on minimal resources for a very long time. The systems are resilient. They are not inexhaustible. If this city is going to be used, it will need tending.

It requires, in the strongest version of this principle, care in the sense of genuine attention paid. Our technology does not work well for people who are indifferent to it. The intent-responsive components in particular perform best for users who approach them as collaborators rather than instruments. This is not a limitation we regret. It is a feature of the design.

> [!arc-note] A Note on Repair
> Several systems in Nibiru have undergone significant strain across the thara'keth since the city was last actively maintained. The atmospheric and geothermal systems are stable. The agricultural district requires attention. If you intend to restore full function to any system, this archive contains the relevant guidance — begin with the Thevan'oma'shala records, accessible at Citizen level, which document the city's infrastructure in full.
