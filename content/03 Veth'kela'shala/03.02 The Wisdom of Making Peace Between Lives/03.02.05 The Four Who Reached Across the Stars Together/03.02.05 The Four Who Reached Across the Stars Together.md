# 03.02.05 The Four Who Reached Across the Stars Together | Vel'oma'keth

*Compiled by Vel'thara Oriskan, Era 71. Revised by Oma'vel Therishal, Era 103.*

There were four of them. Across two galaxies and more technological capability than any single species could have developed alone, four peoples found each other and chose to know each other. They called themselves nothing in particular -- no formal name was ever agreed upon. The term Four Great Races is the archive's convention, not their own.

What they shared was not a single purpose. They did not agree on everything. They were not always easy together. They were four different minds who had each reached a place where they could genuinely meet another mind as an equal, and they were honest enough to do it.

## How the Alliance Formed

The Furlings and the Alteran found each other first. The Furlings had discovered an Alteran seed ship in the Kigal Galaxy -- a vessel sent ahead of any crew, designed to seed habitable worlds with the conditions for life, or to leave records, or simply to mark that the Alteran had been here. The Furlings, characteristically, did not try to claim it. They tried to return it.

The Alteran contact that followed was slow and careful and became, over many eras, a genuine intellectual partnership. The portal network you use in this galaxy is the product of that friendship -- Furling and Alteran minds working together to extend what neither could have built alone.

The Asgard and the Nox were known to the Alteran before the Furlings met them. Each contact was different. With the Asgard, there was immediate mutual recognition -- two peoples who had thought carefully about their responsibilities toward less developed civilisations. With the Nox, there was something harder to describe, something that required more patience from both sides before the Furlings understood what the Nox were offering and what they were not.

## What the Alliance Was

The Four Great Races did not form a government. They did not share a military. They did not merge their civilisations. What they did was simpler and, in some ways, harder: they talked to each other honestly, across vast differences in knowledge and capability and approach, and they listened.

They met at Heliopolis, a meeting place that was understood to belong to all four equally. The records of those meetings are the most extraordinary documents in this archive outside the personal records of those who were present. Four entirely different minds, shaped by entirely different histories, trying to understand what they shared.

What they shared was this: a belief that the universe contains minds worth knowing, that those minds deserve honesty and respect, and that the possession of great capability carries responsibility toward those who have less.

These are not complicated ideas. They are not universally held.

## What Happened to the Alliance

The alliance did not end. It dispersed. The Alteran, over time, mostly ascended. The Asgard turned inward to address their biological crisis. The Nox withdrew further from galactic affairs by choice. The Furlings faced the Keth'alis War and eventually left the Kigal Galaxy.

The meetings at Heliopolis stopped. But the records remain. The portal links between galaxies still function. And somewhere, possibly, those minds continue -- different in form, perhaps, from what they were, but continuous.

The Furlings found the alliance to be one of the most important things that happened to them. Not for the technology exchange -- though that was transformative. For the experience of being genuinely known by another mind. Of finding that across all that difference, understanding was possible.

They would want you to know it is possible.
