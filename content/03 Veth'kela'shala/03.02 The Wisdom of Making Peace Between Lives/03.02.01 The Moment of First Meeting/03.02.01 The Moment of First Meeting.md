# 03.02.01 The Moment of First Meeting | Vel'oma'veth

*Compiled by Oma'vel Therishal, Era 88. Drawing on the full first contact archive spanning Eras 14 to 143.*

The moment of first confirmed contact between two peoples who did not know each other existed is the most consequential moment in the history of either. Everything that follows is shaped by it. The Furlings learned this early and took it seriously.

This record documents the principles the Furlings developed for first contact and the general pattern of how those contacts proceeded. Full records of individual first contacts are held at Student access in [[03.02.02 How We Helped Others Grow]] and related records.

## The Four Principles of First Contact

Over many eras and many encounters, the Furlings distilled their approach to first contact into four principles. These are not rules from above -- they emerged from observing what worked and what caused harm.

The first principle is to observe before approaching. A people should be studied carefully from a respectful distance before any contact is attempted. This is not surveillance for advantage -- it is the basic courtesy of understanding who someone is before you speak to them. The length of observation varies enormously by circumstances, but the Furlings never shortened it for impatience.

The second principle is to approach clearly and without concealment. When contact is made, it is made openly. The Furlings are who they are, coming from where they come from, for the reasons they have. There is no deception in first contact. A relationship built on concealment is not a relationship.

The third principle is to give what can be received. Knowledge, technology, medicine -- all of these were offered freely, but calibrated to what the receiving species could actually use and integrate without harm. A gift that overwhelms is not a gift. The long history of uplift in this archive reflects the Furlings' careful attention to this principle. It also reflects the one time they misjudged it. That record is held in this archive as a lesson, not a confession.

The fourth principle is to remain. Contact is not a single moment. A first meeting that leads to no ongoing relationship is not first contact -- it is a sighting. The Furlings committed to sustained presence with every people they formally contacted. This was costly in time and personnel. It was also what made the difference between a gesture and a friendship.

## What First Contact Actually Felt Like

The formal principles say one thing. The personal records of those who made first contact say another.

From a personal log held at [[03.05.02 Those Who Were There and Remembered]], the recorder's name withheld at their request:

*We came out of the forest edge and they were there, on the other side of the river, and they saw us at the same moment we saw them. Nobody moved for a very long time. I have been trained for this. I know the protocols. And still I stood there thinking: they have never seen anything like me before. They are thinking the same thing. We are both, right now, the most extraordinary thing the other has ever seen.*

*That is what first contact is.*
