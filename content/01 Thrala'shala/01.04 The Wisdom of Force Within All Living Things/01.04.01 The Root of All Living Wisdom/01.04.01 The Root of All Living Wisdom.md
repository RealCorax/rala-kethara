# 01.04.01 The Root of All Living Wisdom | Vel'vetha'shala

*Compiled by Vel'thara Kelomis, Era 44. Revised following contact with the four uplifted species by Shala'vetha Oriskan, Era 103. This record is held open to all visitors.*

What is alive and what is not? The question seems easy until you look carefully at the edges. A flame consumes fuel and grows and responds to wind. A crystal grows by adding material to its surface in ordered patterns. A virus carries information that allows it to reproduce but does nothing at all outside a living cell. These things challenge simple definitions.

The working definition adopted by this archive, after considerable debate across many eras:

A living thing is a self-organising system that maintains its internal order against the tendency of matter toward disorder, draws in matter and energy from its surroundings to do so, responds to changes in those surroundings, and carries within itself the means to produce more of itself.

This definition includes all life known to the Furlings across the Kigal Galaxy and all life encountered through contact with the other Great Races. It may require revision when applied to forms of life not yet encountered. That revision is anticipated and welcomed. The definition is a working tool, not a final truth.

## What All Known Life Shares

When the Furlings reached other worlds and encountered other living things, they expected variety. They found it. But underneath the variety they found something unexpected: a deep convergence at the level of chemistry and information.

All known life is based on carbon. Carbon forms stable bonds with more other elements than any other known element, producing the molecular complexity that self-organisation requires. No life based on a different central element has yet been confirmed, though the records of the unknown held in [[07 Nai'shala]] contain several observations that cannot yet be explained.

All known life uses water as its primary working medium. Water's unusual properties -- its stability across a wide temperature range, its ability to dissolve an enormous variety of compounds, its particular behaviour at its freezing point -- make it uniquely suited to the chemistry of life. Worlds without liquid water have not yet been found to harbour life, though this remains an open question.

All known life encodes heritable information in molecular form. The specific molecules vary -- the Furlings use one system, the Shal'kora use a variant, the Lumenari use a third -- but the principle is universal. Information is stored physically, copied when organisms reproduce, and subject to gradual variation across generations. That variation, interacting with the pressures of survival, produces the diversity of life on every inhabited world.

Whether these shared features represent the only possible basis for life, or merely the most common, is a question this archive holds open. See [[07.05 Life That Does Not Fit What We Know]].

## Life on Mira

All life on Mira traces to a single common ancestor. This is known because all Miran organisms share the same molecular information system -- the same fundamental encoding chemistry, the same translation machinery, the same basic energy currency. The full tree of Miran life -- all four million named lineages -- is documented in the complete biology records accessible to Students and above.

Mira's life is remarkable not only in its diversity but in its age. The oldest recognisable microbial traces in Mira's geological record are approximately 7,000 tael'kora old. Complex multicellular life emerged approximately 700 tael'kora ago. The line that eventually became the Furlings diverged from other large-bodied animals approximately 14 million thara'keth ago. On the timescale of Mira's life, the Furlings as a species are extraordinarily recent.

## Life on Other Worlds

The four species the Furlings worked most closely with -- the Shal'kora, the Vethari, the Lumenari, and the Aethen -- each evolved independently, on different worlds, from different ancestors, in different environments. Their bodies look different. Their societies are organised differently. Their histories are their own.

And yet at the chemical level, the family resemblance is unmistakable. The same four molecular bases. The same basic metabolic pathways. The same fundamental logic. Life, in the Kigal Galaxy, is variations on a theme that must predate any of the worlds it now inhabits.

How life first arose -- on Mira or anywhere -- is a question the archive holds in [[07.03 Where Wisdom Has Not Yet Found Its Ground]]. The chemistry of the first living thing from non-living precursors is not yet fully understood. The Furlings worked on it for many eras. The question remains open.
