# 01.05.02 How Worlds Learn to Hold Life | Mira'vel'shala

*Compiled by Varathal, Era 14. Planetary surveys by Shalaveth and the Vara'skela Survey Team, Eras 111 to 118. This record is held open to all visitors. For the scientific and cultural record of Mira specifically, see also [[02.01.01 Where Our Life First Rooted]].*

A world does not simply exist. It forms, slowly, from the leftover material of its star's birth -- gas and dust and rock colliding and accreting over millions of thara'keth until stable orbits emerge. Whether it will be capable of holding life depends on a convergence of factors: how far it sits from its star, how that star behaves, whether it holds water, what its atmosphere is made of, whether anything shields it from the worst of the debris that still travels the system.

Mira holds all of these conditions. The Vara'skela system that surrounds it shaped that fact.

## Vareth

Vareth is the innermost planet. Airless, tidally influenced by Vara so that it rotates very slowly relative to its orbit, its surface is exposed metallic and crystal rock. Furling explorers reached it early -- its crystal and metal deposits were among the first off-world resource sources in Furling history. It cannot support life as any known species requires it.

## Thrael

Thrael is the second planet, hot and volcanically active with a thin sulphur-rich atmosphere. It may once have been more temperate. What it is now is a reference point for what can go wrong when a world's atmospheric chemistry tips past a certain threshold and cannot recover. Its study contributed significantly to Furling understanding of planetary stewardship. See [[01.05.03 The Breath That Wraps Each World]].

Mira's active tectonics continuously cycle minerals through the surface, maintaining the chemical conditions that sustain complex life. The two moons -- Mirathal and Varakris -- drive tidal forces that have shaped Mira's geology and contributed to evolutionary pressure over billions of thara'keth.

### Moons of Mira

| Moon | Type | Radius | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mirathal | Iron-rich rocky | approximately 1.6 x 10^6 shael | Primary tidal driver; contributes to tectonics |
| Varakris | Silicate crystal | approximately 0.9 x 10^6 shael | Significant cultural and visual importance |

Varakris has little tidal effect but considerable cultural weight. Its crystalline surface reflects Vara's light with a warm shimmer visible to the unaided eye from Mira's surface. It appears in Furling art, poetry, and ceremony across all eras. It is the first thing many Furling children learn to find in the night sky.

## Velakris Belt

The inner asteroid belt, at approximately 1.39 skela'var, is composed of iron-rich and crystal-laden bodies. It represents material that did not accrete into a planet during the system's formation. It has served as a resource source and contributes to the occasional meteor influx on the inner planets.

## Naiveth

Naiveth is a cold super-Earth with a thick atmosphere and an ice-covered surface. Below the ice, a subsurface ocean is considered probable based on tidal heating models. Whether life exists in that ocean remains unconfirmed. The question is held open in [[07.05 Life That Does Not Fit What We Know]].

## Thalavar

Thalavar is a gas giant at 5.19 skela'var with a very strong magnetic field. Its gravitational influence shields the inner system from the majority of objects that would otherwise impact Mira at catastrophic frequency. This shielding function is one of the key factors enabling the long-term stability of Miran life. Systems without such a shielding body tend to show higher rates of large-body impact on their inner worlds.

Thalavar has three notable moons: Krisvela (ice-crystal, with geysers), Mirashal (rocky, thin atmosphere), and Velanai (probable subsurface ocean beneath ice shell).

## Velathara

The outer debris disk beyond approximately 22 skela'var contains icy bodies and comets. These deliver volatiles to the inner system over very long timescales and contribute to Mira's climate over tael'kora cycles.
