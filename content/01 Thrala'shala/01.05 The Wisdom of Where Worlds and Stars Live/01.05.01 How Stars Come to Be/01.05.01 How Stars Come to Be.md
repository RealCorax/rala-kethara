# 01.05.01 How Stars Come to Be | Vara'vel'shala

*Compiled by Varathal, Era 14. Expanded following cosmological timescale work by Omavel, Era 91. Final revision by Shalaveth, Era 118. This record is held open to all visitors.*

A star is not a fire. This distinction took the Furlings many eras to establish, and it matters. A fire consumes its fuel and goes out. A star fuses matter into new matter, releasing energy in the process, and can sustain this for millions or billions of thara'keth depending on its mass. The sun above Mira -- Vara -- has been burning for approximately 4 tael'kora. It will continue for at least 6 tael'kora more, possibly 9.

Understanding what Vara is and how long it will last was one of the most important scientific findings in Furling history. It answered a question the reconstruction era needed answered more than almost any other: how long do we have?

## How Stars Form

Stars form from clouds of gas and dust scattered through galaxies. The gas is predominantly hydrogen. When a region of such a cloud becomes dense enough, gravity pulls it inward. As it collapses it heats. When the core reaches sufficient temperature and pressure, hydrogen nuclei begin fusing together into helium. The energy released halts the collapse. The star has ignited.

The mass of a star at formation determines almost everything about its subsequent life: its temperature, its colour, its brightness, how long it will remain stable, and how it will end.

## Stellar Classification

The Furling stellar classification system was developed by Varathal in Era 14. Vara itself is the reference standard. All stellar properties throughout this archive are expressed relative to Vara, which is defined as 1.0 in mass, radius, and luminosity.

Stars are classified by surface temperature and size. The main classes, from coolest to hottest:

M-type stars are the most common in the Kigal Galaxy -- small, cool, red to orange-red, and extremely long-lived. Most stars you cannot see with the unaided eye from Mira's surface are M-type.

K-type stars are slightly larger and warmer. Vara is a K4 main sequence star -- orange-gold in colour, stable, long-lived. K-type stars are considered among the most hospitable for inhabited worlds.

G-type stars are what humans orbit in the Milky Way -- somewhat hotter and brighter than Vara, yellow-white in colour, moderately long-lived.

F, A, B, and O-type stars become progressively hotter, brighter, and shorter-lived. The most massive and hottest stars may live only a few million thara'keth before ending violently.

The name Vara derives from the root *var* meaning star and light. It is the oldest name in the Furling language -- it predates writing, predates formal speech, predates any understanding of what a star actually is. The name was not chosen. It simply accumulated, across generations, from the simple recognition that this thing above was bright and warm.

The informal name Skela'el'vara -- roughly "our home's light" -- is used in poetry, ceremony, and everyday conversation when Vara is meant as a personal presence rather than an astronomical object.

## How Stars End

A star's ending depends on its mass. Stars like Vara -- moderate K-type -- will eventually exhaust their core hydrogen supply, expand into red giants shedding their outer layers, and leave behind a dense cooling remnant. This process lies many tael'kora in the future for Vara. It is slow, and the ending is not violent.

More massive stars end differently and more dramatically. The records of stellar endings and their effects on surrounding systems are held at [[01.05.07 The Homes Beyond All Homes We Know]].
