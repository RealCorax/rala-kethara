# 01.02.01 What the Ground Holds in Its Strength | Mira'vel'thral

*Compiled by Vel'thara Mevashis, Era 31. This record is held open to all visitors as the foundation of physical understanding.*

A stone released from the hand falls. A thrown stone follows a curved path before it falls. A stone resting on the ground does not move unless something moves it. These observations are so familiar that they can seem too obvious to study. The Furlings studied them anyway, and what they found underneath the obvious was a structure of extraordinary precision and power.

Mechanics is the study of how forces act on objects -- how they cause motion, change motion, and bring motion to rest. It was the first branch of physics to reach formal precision in the Furling tradition, and it remains the foundation on which all other physical understanding is built.

## The Three Laws of Motion

Formalised by the author of this record in Era 31, these three statements describe the behaviour of all objects at the scales of everyday life. They have been tested in every environment the Furlings have ever inhabited, including the vacuum between worlds, and they have not failed.

The first law states that any object continues in its current state -- at rest or moving at constant speed in a constant direction -- unless a force acts upon it. Rest is not the natural state of things and motion is not the natural state of things. Constant unchanged state is the natural state of things. Change requires cause.

The second law states that when a force acts on an object, the change in that object's motion depends on both the size of the force and the mass of the object. A larger force produces more change. A more massive object requires more force to produce the same change. This relationship is precise and measurable.

The third law states that whenever one object exerts a force on another, the second object exerts an equal force back on the first, in the opposite direction. Forces do not exist alone. Every force has a counterpart. The stone resting on the ground pushes the ground downward with its weight. The ground pushes the stone upward with the same force. The stone does not sink because these forces are equal.

## What These Laws Allow

These three laws, combined with the mathematics of [[01.01 The Shape of All Things]], allow the prediction of where any object will be at any future time, given knowledge of where it is now and what forces are acting on it. This is the basis of all Furling engineering -- the construction of structures, the design of vessels, the management of the forces within the city of Nibiru itself.

For those visiting from worlds where this understanding is not yet formal: the laws are not Furling inventions. They were observed in stone and water and sky long before the Furlings named them. Any careful observer of the physical world is already using them intuitively. This record simply names what is already known.

## Limits

These three laws describe everyday scales accurately. At very high velocities -- approaching the speed at which light travels -- corrections are needed. At the scale of the smallest particles of matter, different rules apply. Those records are held at [[01.02.06 What Holds the Smallest Things Together]] and [[01.02.08 All Forces Understood as One Living Thing]].
