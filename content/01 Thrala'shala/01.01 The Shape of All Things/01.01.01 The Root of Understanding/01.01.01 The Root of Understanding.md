# 01.01.01 The Root of Understanding | Vel'thara'shala

*Compiled by Vel'thara Norimesh, Era 23. Expanded by Shala'vel Oriskan, Era 88. This record is held open to all visitors as a foundation for everything else in this branch.*

Before there was writing, there was counting. Before counting, there was pattern recognition -- the instinct to notice that two moons are different from three moons, that a herd yesterday had more in it than a herd today. The impulse that eventually became mathematics is older than the Furlings. It is older than language. It may be the oldest thing about any mind.

What mathematics became -- in the hands of those who studied it carefully over many thousands of thara'keth -- is something considerably more than counting. It became the study of structure itself: the properties that things can share with each other regardless of what those things are made of.

## What Mathematics Is

A number is not a physical thing. The number three is not the same as three stones or three thara'keth, though it is present in both. It is the abstract property they share -- a pattern that can be recognised, named, combined with other patterns, and reasoned about independently of any particular instance.

This abstraction is mathematics. The patterns exist whether or not any mind notices them. A Furling mathematician does not invent a theorem -- they discover it. The relationship between the sides of a right triangle held before anyone measured it, before anyone thought about triangles at all, before there were minds to think with.

That is what makes mathematics different from all other knowledge held in this archive. History records what happened. Biology describes what life does. Mathematics describes what must be, given certain starting conditions. Its truths are not contingent on how the universe turns out to be -- they are necessary.

## How Proof Works

The central tool of mathematics is proof -- the demonstration that a relationship holds not merely in observed cases but in all possible cases, by necessity of structure.

A Furling farmer who counts the harvest in one thara'keth and finds the same count as the thara'keth before has made an observation. A mathematician who demonstrates that the sum of any two even numbers is always even has made a proof. The farmer's observation could in principle be wrong next thara'keth. The proof cannot.

Proof requires: starting assumptions that are accepted as given (called axioms), and a chain of steps each of which follows necessarily from what came before. No hidden jumps. No appeals to what seems obvious. Every step must hold by the logic of the structure itself.

The standards for what counts as a valid proof were refined over many eras. Records from before Era 40 are annotated in this archive to note where modern standards would require additional justification. This is not a criticism of those early scholars -- they were working at the frontier of what was understood. It is simply honesty about the history of the discipline.

## Numbers and the Zero

The Furling number system is base-10, standardised in Era 45 for consistency across worlds. Before that, different regions and worlds used different bases -- most commonly base-7, rooted in the seven bodies of the Vara'skela system.

The concept of zero -- *nora*, from the root *nor*, meaning to hold with nothing held -- was formalised by the author of this record in Era 23. It had existed in practice before then: archive records from Era 9 use a placeholder symbol for an absent quantity. What Norimesh established was that zero is not merely the absence of number but a number in its own right, with its own mathematical properties. This change was not universally welcomed at the time. It is now considered one of the most important insights in the history of the discipline.

The full number system, including the time units derived from it, is documented in [[01.01.07 Making Shape Useful]] and in [[02.07.04 Numbers Mathematics and Measurement]].

## For Those Who Are New to This

If the concepts above are unfamiliar, that is not a barrier to this archive. The records here build on each other, and there is no requirement that a visitor understand all of mathematics before reading records in other branches. The measurement standards at [[01.01.07 Making Shape Useful]] are written to be used practically by anyone. The biological records in [[01.04 The Wisdom of Force Within All Living Things]] do not require mathematical training to read.
