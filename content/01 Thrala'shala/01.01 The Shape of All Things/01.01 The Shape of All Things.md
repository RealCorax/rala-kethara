# 01.01 The Shape of All Things | Thael'shala

*Section compiled by Vel'thara Norimesh, Era 23 onward. Expanded by Thara'vel Kelomis, Era 78. Final revision by Oma'shala Veriskan, Era 139.*

Mathematics is the study of structure. Not any particular structure -- not the shape of a stone or the arc of a river -- but the properties that structures can share with each other regardless of what they are made of. Three stones and three thara'keth share something. The thing they share is not made of stone or time. It is a pattern. Mathematics is the study of patterns.

This makes it unlike all other branches of knowledge held in this archive. Physics describes how the universe actually behaves. Chemistry describes what matter actually does. Biology describes how life actually works. Mathematics describes how things must be, if they are to be consistent with themselves. A mathematical truth, once demonstrated, cannot be otherwise. That is its particular nature and its particular value.

The Furling mathematical tradition spans the full 150,000 thara'keth of our recorded history. It began in counting and measurement. It grew, over many eras, into something far more abstract -- the study of structure itself, independent of any physical application. By the later eras, Furling mathematicians were proving theorems about entities that had no known physical counterpart, simply because the structure was interesting and the proof was possible.

Some of those abstractions later proved to describe physical reality with uncanny precision. Others remain beautiful and useless in equal measure. Both are held here.
