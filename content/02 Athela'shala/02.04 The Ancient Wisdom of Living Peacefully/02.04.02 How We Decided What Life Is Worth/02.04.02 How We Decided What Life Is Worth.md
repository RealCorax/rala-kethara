# 02.04.02 How We Decided What Life Is Worth | Ethics of Life

*Compiled by Vel'thara Oriskan, Era 83.*

Furling ethics is values-based, not rule-based. A behaviour is evaluated not by whether it breaks a specific prohibition but by whether it aligns with the Five Principles. Does this action strengthen the Whole, protect the vulnerable, share knowledge or labour, maintain trust, and support children and the future?

This method produces a system flexible enough to handle genuinely novel situations while remaining anchored to principles stable enough to hold across 150,000 thara'keth.

## Positions on Specific Questions

Over millennia the Furlings developed consistent positions on questions that many species find difficult.

Euthanasia is supported. The second principle -- Compassion is Strength -- is understood to include the compassionate ending of suffering that cannot be relieved. Voluntary, guided by healers, deliberate.

Abortion is not supported. Life is considered sacred from its beginning. Where a developing life faces illness or impairment, Furling medicine addresses this through genetic correction and intervention. This position is not imposed on other species. It reflects the Furlings' own values about when life begins and what is owed to it.

Suicide is not supported, but it is treated with grief rather than judgement. A life lost this way is understood as a failure of the community -- a person who died because the Whole around them could not hold them well enough. The question asked is always: what did we miss?

Killing is the gravest violation. Even in wartime -- and the Furlings fought two major wars -- killing was held as something to be done only when necessary and mourned, never celebrated.

Knowledge suppression is one of the most serious ethical breaches. The third principle makes the deliberate withholding of knowledge from those who need it an act of harm.

## What This Means for Justice

Furling justice is restorative. Punishment does not strengthen the Whole. Healing does. The goal of any justice process is repair -- of the harm, of the trust, and where possible of the person who caused the harm. Exile is the last resort, considered a tragedy even when necessary.
