# 02.02.01 Before the Cycles Had Names | Pre-History and Proto-Furling Eras

*Compiled by Thara'shala Kelisometh, Era 131. Drawing on archaeological records by Thevanara, Era 31, and linguistic reconstruction by Vel'thara Oriskan, Era 79.*

The era before consistent written language is reconstructed from archaeological evidence, fossil records, geological work, and comparative linguistic analysis that allows partial reconstruction of early spoken forms. No complete firsthand accounts exist. What we know, we infer.

The proto-Furling peoples lived in small family units, moving with seasons and resources. Evidence of shelter construction, fire cultivation, and early tool use is present in the record from approximately 200,000 thara'keth before the present era. The earliest evidence of deliberate food cultivation appears approximately 80,000 thara'keth ago.

## Early Social Structure

Initial social units were family-scale. As populations grew and resources concentrated, these units aggregated into larger tribes. Tribal identity became dominant over family identity within a few thousand thara'keth of sufficient population density. The tribal period is the foundation of much of the conflict that defined the early eras.

## The Velamira Observation Tradition

Before writing, before formal science, there were watchers. The Velamira tradition -- pre-language systematic observation of the sky -- left physical records in stone carvings that predate any known writing system. These carvings tracked Vara's movement, the orbital cycles of Mira's two moons, and seasonal patterns. The impulse to understand preceded the tools to do so formally.

The stone carvings of Velamira are among the oldest surviving Furling artifacts. Some survive intact on Mira to this day.
