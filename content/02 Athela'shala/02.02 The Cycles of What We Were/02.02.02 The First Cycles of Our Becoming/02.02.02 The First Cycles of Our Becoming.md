# 02.02.02 The First Cycles of Our Becoming | The Early Eras

*Compiled by Thara'shala Kelisometh, Era 131.*

The period from the first consistent written records through to the catastrophe of Era 12 spans approximately 11,000 thara'keth -- eleven thousand years of slow, often violent development.

The earliest Furling governance was tribal: single leaders holding authority over bounded territories. This produced stability within tribes and constant conflict between them. Resource competition drove most of the inter-tribal warfare of this period.

What consistently failed across these eras: any structure that allowed power to concentrate in too few hands for too long. Single leaders who were wise produced successors who were not. Hereditary systems produced eventual incompetence. Meritocratic systems were corrupted by those who defined the merit criteria.

What began to work: distributed decision-making with no permanent individual power. The earliest proto-Councils emerged before Era 12 -- uncoordinated, culturally distinct, but pointing toward the same answer independently in multiple regions of Mira. The idea that three voices guided by shared principles would resist corruption better than one had no single inventor. It was arrived at separately by people who had watched what happened when one voice held too much.

By Era 11 there is evidence of literature -- stories told not for survival but for meaning. The transition had already begun before the catastrophe that would make it necessary.
