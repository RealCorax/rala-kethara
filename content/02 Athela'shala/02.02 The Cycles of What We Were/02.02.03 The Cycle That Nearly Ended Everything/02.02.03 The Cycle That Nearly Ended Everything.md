# 02.02.03 The Cycle That Nearly Ended Everything | Era 12 and the Great War

*Compiled by Thara'shala Kelisometh, Era 131.*

The details of what caused the Great War in Era 12 are largely lost. The war was planet-wide, involved the most destructive weapons Furlings had developed to that point, and nearly ended the species entirely.

Population before Era 12: estimated in the hundreds of millions.
Population after Era 12: fewer than one million confirmed survivors.

The causes were territorial and resource-based -- the same pattern that had produced smaller conflicts throughout the early eras, now scaled to catastrophic consequence. There was no single aggressor. There was an escalation that no one stopped until it was almost too late.

## Tharúven Kora'thesh

The figure who halted the escalation and began reconstruction emerged from the war as a survivor who understood with clarity that the species would not survive another such conflict. He convinced the remaining population -- scattered, grieving, exhausted -- that unity was not idealism but survival.

His argument was simple: *Tharun'vel shora.* Hold whole.

## The Korath Veshun -- The Original Five Principles

In the years immediately following the war, Tharúven Kora'thesh created the original version of what would later become the Kora'thesh Veshúnar. The original, in the proto-Furling language of Era 12:

1. *Tharun'vel shora* -- Hold Whole.
2. *Veshar kelun* -- Tend Weak.
3. *Shora veshun tal* -- Share Path.
4. *Korath noren* -- Break no Bond.
5. *Thalen shora'vel* -- Raise Young Together.

Only one original tablet survives -- cracked, incomplete, held in a sealed vault on Mira. The text survived because hundreds of copies were made in the centuries following Era 12.

## The Reconstruction

The era following the war was the most generative in Furling history -- not despite the devastation but because of it. With fewer than a million survivors and the full knowledge of what unchecked conflict produces, the Furlings rebuilt not merely their population and infrastructure but their understanding of what a civilisation should be.

The archive, the Five Principles, the Council of 3, the communal child-rearing traditions, the practice of knowledge freely shared -- all of it traces back to decisions made in the reconstruction era by people who had seen what the alternative produced.
