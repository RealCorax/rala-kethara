# 02.06.03 Words Carried Across the Distance Between Us | Correspondence Collections

*Compiled by Thara'shala Kelisometh, Era 119.*

A letter is a form of faith. You write it believing there is someone on the other end who will receive it, and who will care.

The correspondence collections hold surviving letters, messages, and personal communications from across Furling history -- between family members separated by colonisation, between colleagues on different worlds, between Council members during periods of crisis, between individuals who were in love.

Letters tell you what other records cannot. Official documents record decisions. Journals record thoughts. Letters record relationships -- the texture of how two people spoke to each other across distance and time.

## The Exodus Letters

Among the most requested collections: letters written by Furlings during the final thara'keth before the exodus, many addressed to people they would not be able to take with them, or to the unknown future readers of this archive.

From Thevathal Kelisometh, three years before the exodus, to her sister on Mira:
*I know you think we are wrong to go. I cannot argue with you -- not because you are wrong, but because I am no longer certain what right looks like from here. What I know is this: Nibiru is finished. It is ready. Whatever comes next, someone will find it. I need to believe that someone will find it.*
