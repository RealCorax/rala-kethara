# 02.03.02 The Family at the Heart of Peace | Family Structure and the Greater-Family System

*Compiled by Oma'vel Therishal, Era 88.*

Furlings mate for life. The choice of partner is entirely free -- no family, council, or community authority has any formal role in the decision. The bond is considered sacred under the fourth principle: Trust is Sacred.

With lifespans extending to 400 to 500 thara'keth, a Furling partnership can span centuries. Long-lived couples often describe the experience as a kind of continuous becoming -- they are not the same people they were when they chose each other, and neither is their partner, and this is considered one of the deepest gifts of a long life.

## Children and the Greater-Family

Children are raised by their biological parents for the first 10 thara'keth, accompanying them to their work and attending local mixed-age group training. But Furling children also belong to the greater-family -- the extended community of adults who share responsibility for their wellbeing and education.

The fifth principle -- Every Child is Ours -- is not metaphor. It is the literal operating principle of Furling child-rearing. A child spending time with the greater-family is not separated from their parents. It is a normal, celebrated part of Furling childhood.

## Birth and Death

Birth is celebrated communally. All children born on a given day in an area are honoured together, not individually. The celebration belongs to the community, not only to the parents.

Death is also celebrated rather than mourned in isolation. The community gathers. Stories are told. The life is honoured. Grief is shared rather than held privately.

## Multi-Generational Households

With extended lifespans up to 500 thara'keth, great-great-great-grandparents may be alive simultaneously with newborns within the same greater-family. The archive contains accounts of Furlings who knew five living generations of their own line. This is considered a profound gift. Elders who have lived four centuries carry knowledge that cannot be found in any written record.
