# 02.05.02 How We Shaped the Spaces We Lived In | Architecture and Housing

*Compiled by Thara'vel Omashal, Era 94.*

The Furlings did not build buildings. They grew them. The distinction matters. What is grown has a relationship with its environment. What is built is imposed on it.

The primary building material -- a coral-mycelium composite -- is a living engineered organism that grows according to guidance and then hardens to stone-like durability while retaining the organic warmth and curve of its living origins. A significant structure may require decades of careful cultivation. This is not considered a disadvantage. Furling culture values things designed to outlast their builders.

There are no sharp corners in mature Furling architecture. Sharp corners concentrate stress at a single point. An organic curve distributes it across the whole surface. The architecture embodies the same principle as the culture: the Whole holds better than any single point.

The city of Nibiru is the fullest expression of these principles. It was grown over many decades. The buildings you see have been standing, without maintenance, for 5,000 thara'keth. Some wall surfaces are still slowly self-repairing. For the architectural records specific to this city, see [[04.03.01 Why We Build the Way We Do]].
