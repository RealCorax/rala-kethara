# 02.05.05 The Ceremonies That Held Us Together | Ceremonies Festivals and Communal Rituals

*Compiled by Thara'vel Omashal, Era 94.*

A ceremony does not create meaning. It makes meaning visible. That visibility is what allows meaning to be shared.

## Birth -- The Naming Circle

When a child is born, the naming ceremony takes place within the first seven days. The child's parents choose a name. The greater-family gathers. The child is carried through a circle of adults, each placing a hand briefly on the child's head and offering a single word -- a quality they commit to modelling for that child. Patience. Curiosity. Steadiness. Joy. The record of who was present and what word each person gave accompanies the child's formal archive entry.

## Coming of Age -- The Choosing Day

At 18 thara'keth, a cohort of young adults from the area gather for the Choosing Day ceremony. Each person states publicly the field they are choosing to dedicate their initial adult years to. The community responds with the names of people already working in that field who have agreed to serve as mentors. Every young adult leaves the ceremony with at least one name: a person who is already doing the thing they want to do, and who has agreed to help them.

## Death -- The Memory Feast

Death ceremonies are communal celebrations of a life completed. The format: stories, shared meals, music the person loved, objects that belonged to them passed around the circle. At the end of the gathering, a crystal memory-vessel is sealed -- it contains every story told that day, every piece of music played, the voices of everyone who spoke. The vessel is placed in the archive. No Furling is wholly lost while their vessel remains.

## The Great Festivals

The most significant annual festival is Oma'thara -- Peace Day -- commemorating the reconstruction after Era 12. On this day all work stops. The communities gather. The Kora'thesh Veshúnar is read aloud. Children ask questions and elders answer them.
