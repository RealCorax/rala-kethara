# 02.07.05 The Living Voice — Idiom and Poetic Forms | Idiom and Poetry

*Compiled by Vel'thara Oriskan, Era 79.*

Furling proverbs rarely instruct. They observe. A Furling saying the river does not ask which way is down is not telling you what to do. They are reminding you that some things have a natural direction.

## Selected Proverbs

On knowledge: Knowledge isolated is knowledge lost. All wisdom connects.

On community: No Furling built anything alone, even in an empty room.

On time: A long life is not wisdom. It is the opportunity for wisdom.

On difficulty: The Whole has held worse than this.

On peace: Peace is not the absence of conflict. It is the presence of enough trust to resolve conflict.

## The Poetic Forms

The Vel'shala is the primary lyric form -- three stanzas of three lines, with the third line of each stanza echoing the first line of the next. The form creates a continuous flowing sense of each ending becoming a beginning.

The Keth'rala is the great narrative form, oral in origin, designed to be performed as well as read. The greatest surviving Keth'rala holds the complete mythological and historical narrative of the Furling people in a single continuous arc.

The Oma'vel is the quietest form -- a single extended breath, six to eight words, designed to hold a complete truth in minimum language.

Three examples of Oma'vel:
*Vetha'nor oma'len* -- Life holds even in stillness.
*Shala meva'nor* -- Wisdom is a gift that holds.
*Rala'keth lora* -- The eternal voice flows.
