# 02.07.01 Where Our Voice First Rooted | Linguistic Origins

*Compiled by Vel'thara Oriskan, Era 79.*

Before the Furlings had writing, before they had discrete words in the sense we now understand them, they sang. The linguistic root of the entire language sits in a pre-verbal tonal communication system that was closer to music than speech. The language still carries this. Furling spoken aloud by a trained speaker is not simply said -- it is performed, with pitch and rhythm as much a part of meaning as the words themselves.

The oldest recoverable language, Proto-Furling, was tonal and highly contextual. It had a limited consonant inventory -- the harsh consonants present in many other species' languages were absent from the beginning. The Great War of Era 12 destroyed an incalculable quantity of linguistic material. Regional dialects, entire vocabulary families, and several complete writing systems were lost.

The language that emerged from reconstruction was not the language that entered the war. It was a simplified, unified convergence -- a common tongue chosen from the surviving variants because the survivors understood they needed to share a language if they were going to survive as a civilisation. This post-war convergence became Classical Furling, and then, through natural development, Modern High Furling.
