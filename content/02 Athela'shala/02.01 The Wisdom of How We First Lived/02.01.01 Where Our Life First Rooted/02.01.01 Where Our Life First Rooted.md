# 02.01.01 Where Our Life First Rooted | Evolutionary Origins and Fossil Record

*Compiled by Vel'thara Kelomis, Era 44. Drawing on geological records by Thevanara, Era 31.*

The fossil record on Mira tells a story that begins long before the word Furling existed to name anything. The species that would eventually become the Furlings evolved over many millions of thara'keth on the third planet of the Vara'skela system -- a cool temperate world with high crystal mineral content, two moons generating significant tidal activity, and a stable orange star that did not punish life with radiation or violent flare cycles.

These conditions shaped everything that followed. The creatures that eventually became us were not inevitable. But Mira was generous, and generosity tends to produce complexity.

## The Pre-Sapient Line

Furling evolutionary biologists identified three primary branches of hominid ancestor before the emergence of clear sapience.

The Vel'thara line -- small, forest-dwelling, highly social, with strong indicators of cooperative behaviour and early tool use. This line is the direct ancestor of modern Furlings.

The Mira'thara line -- larger, plains-adapted, physically stronger. This line went extinct approximately 12 million thara'keth ago, likely outcompeted as the Vel'thara line developed language and coordination.

The Nai'thara line -- coastal and riverine adapted, with evidence of early fish cultivation. Merged genetically with the Vel'thara line before the emergence of full sapience.

The genetic contribution of all three is visible in the modern Furling genome -- the cooperative instinct of the Vel'thara, the environmental adaptability of the Nai'thara, and traces of the Mira'thara's physical resilience.

## The Emergence of Sapience

The fossil and archaeological record places full sapience -- defined as language use, abstract reasoning, and deliberate teaching of non-survival knowledge -- at approximately 200,000 thara'keth before the present era. The emergence was gradual. There was no single threshold. There was simply, over thousands of thara'keth, a species that increasingly chose to understand rather than merely to survive.

## A Note on Creation Traditions

The Furlings hold several creation traditions about their origins. These are not contradicted by the fossil record. They are a different kind of knowledge -- cultural and meaning-bearing rather than empirical. Both are preserved in this archive.
