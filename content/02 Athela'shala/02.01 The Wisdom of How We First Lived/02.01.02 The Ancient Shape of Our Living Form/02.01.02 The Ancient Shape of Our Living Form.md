# 02.01.02 The Ancient Shape of Our Living Form | Furling Physiology and Anatomy

*Compiled by Vel'thara Kelomis, Era 44.*

## General Form

The Furling body is humanoid, bilaterally symmetrical, and adapted to the cool temperate conditions of Mira.

Height ranges from 4 to 5 feet in adulthood. Furlings stand shorter than many species they have encountered. This was occasionally a source of humour. The Furlings found it funnier than most.

Build is slender and proportional, conveying grace rather than strength. The musculature is functional and capable but not prominently defined.

Skin tones run through warm earth ranges -- amber, bronze, and terracotta -- with subtle natural grain patterns visible in certain lighting. The grain shifts slightly across a lifetime and varies between individuals. No two Furlings have the same pattern.

Eyes are large and expressive, spanning blues and greens with occasional grey-green in older individuals. The size relative to the face gives an impression of attentiveness that many species found immediately trustworthy.

Ears are small and lightly pointed, set close to the head. Hearing range is somewhat broader than human equivalent, particularly at higher frequencies.

Facial structure is gentle and rounded, with slight elegant ridges along the forehead and cheekbones. The overall impression is calm and approachable.

Hands have four fingers plus an opposable thumb, with elongated graceful proportions. Fine motor control is exceptional. Furling hands were built for precise work.

Hair grows in earth tone ranges -- browns, auburns, deep chestnut. Most Furlings maintain it at shoulder length or longer by preference.

Movement is naturally unhurried and graceful. A Furling at rest is genuinely still -- not tense, simply at rest.

## Internal Systems

Standard carbon-based biochemistry. Oxygen-dependent respiration. One taelketh (approximately 1.94 hours) of sleep per mira'thara is sufficient for full cognitive restoration. The Furling lifespan is naturally up to 200 thara'keth, extended to 400 or more through scientific means developed from approximately Era 100 onward.

A faint bioluminescent capacity exists along the hands and forearms -- a vestigial trait from a pre-sapient ancestor. Non-functional in any practical sense. Furling artists have made considerable use of it.
