# <span class="furling-pin">Thara'vel Keth-Oun</span> | Survey Observer and Athel'kin Contact Lead

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 88–134 · Vara'skela research stations, then field work · Long-Range Observation, First Contact, Cultural Documentation

## Life and Work

Thara'vel Keth-Oun spent thirty thara'keth as a survey observer before the Athel'kin contact — long enough, they noted in personal records, to have developed very specific ideas about what made a species worth extended attention. They had documented thirty-seven species in that period, produced survey reports running to several hundred thousand entries, and become known within the observation corps for reports that contained, alongside the standard scientific observations, a quality of genuine curiosity about the beings they were watching.

The Athel'kin assignment began in Era 110 as a standard long-range observation: a newly detected civilisation in the mid-galaxy region, assessed as pre-industrial, no immediate contact protocol indicated. Within three months of beginning observation, Thara'vel had filed an unusually personal preliminary note — the passage reproduced in the Vara'athel'veth record is taken from it — that was, by all accounts, read with more attention than preliminary notes typically received.

The forty thara'keth of observation that followed produced the most detailed pre-contact documentation of any species in the Furling archive, partly because Thara'vel had the time and the instinct to look carefully, and partly because what they were watching was, as they wrote repeatedly, simply extraordinary. The survey team's final assessment report, which recommended contact, was largely drafted by Thara'vel. The personal view expressed in that report — that if any species merited consideration for the Vetha'meva, it was this one — was noted in the Council record and contributed, in ways Thara'vel never entirely made peace with, to the eventual decision.

Thara'vel was part of the initial contact team and remained closely involved with the Athel'kin documentation project for the next fifteen thara'keth. They compiled the original versions of both the creative works archive and the spiritual tradition record, and supervised the ongoing documentation through Era 125. By that point the relationship between the Furlings and the Keth'alis — as some of the Athel'kin had begun to call themselves — was already deteriorating. Thara'vel's final contact report, filed in Era 126, is held at Student access. It is not, by their own description, a comfortable document to read.

Thara'vel died in Era 134, on Mira, of natural causes. They were sixty-six thara'keth old. They did not see the war.

## On Their Sense of Responsibility

Personal correspondence preserved in the archive makes clear that Thara'vel spent considerable time, in their later years, thinking about what their recommendation had contributed to. Not to the point of self-destruction — they were a disciplined thinker — but with genuine ongoing attention to the question of whether they had seen what they said they saw, or whether they had seen what they wanted to see.

Their conclusion, recorded in a personal journal entry from Era 130, was careful: *"I saw what was there. I described it honestly. The decision was made by the Council on the basis of what I and others described. I cannot now make myself responsible for the Council's decision, or the outcome of it, in ways that would require me to have been dishonest about what I observed. I was not. What I was, is someone who saw something beautiful and said so. The beauty was real. What came after is a different question."*
