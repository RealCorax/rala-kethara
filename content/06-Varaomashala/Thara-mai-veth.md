# <span class="furling-pin">Thara'mai'veth</span> | Those Whose Lives Became Larger Than Living

> *History records what people did. Myth records what their doing meant. The two are not the same, and both are necessary. A Furling who knows only the historical record of Tharúven Kora'thesh — the councils, the alliances, the specific decisions — knows the facts and misses the figure. The figure is what the facts became in the telling, across a hundred thousand thara'keth of people saying: this is what it means to hold the Whole when the Whole is breaking.*
> — Vel Thara-Omaven, Era 99

## On Legendary Figures

A legendary figure is not a fictional one. Every person documented in this section was real, in the sense that they lived, acted, and died. What makes them legendary is what happened to their stories afterward: the accumulation of meaning that turns a historical person into something that carries the weight of values, of crisis, of what a people most needs to believe about themselves.

This process is not dishonest. It is how cultures survive catastrophe: by finding, in the lives of actual people, the proof that the things they most need to be true are possible.

## Tharúven Kora'thesh — The One Who Held the Path

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 0–61 · Mira, eastern regions · Unifier, philosopher, founder of the Kora'thesh Veshúnar

The historical record of Tharúven Kora'thesh is thin. This is partly because they lived in Era 0–61, before consistent written records; it is partly because even in their own time, the practical details of their life were considered less important than the work they were doing.

What we know: Tharúven was a Furling of middle age at the time of the Great War's end — a survivor, not of the war itself, but of what the war left behind. Mira's population had fallen to below one million. The infrastructure of pre-war civilisation was mostly destroyed.

What Tharúven is remembered for is the specific nature of their response to this: they did not attempt to rebuild the old structures. They proposed something different.

The *Kora'thesh Veshúnar* was not written down in Tharúven's lifetime. It was spoken, repeated, carried from group to group across a shattered Mira by people who had heard Tharúven speak and wanted to share what they had heard. The Five Principles in their earliest form were simpler than the modern formulation — shorter, rougher, in a proto-language that has since evolved significantly. The meaning was the same.

In the mythological tradition, Tharúven is not a hero in the conventional sense. They did not fight. They did not command. They talked to people. They listened. They found, in each group of survivors, the version of the Path that was already implicit in how they were managing to live together in the wreckage, and named it, and connected it to what they had found in other groups.

One phrase attributed to them appears in enough independent sources to be considered likely authentic: *Vel'oma vesha'nēl shora* — "One small step, one small step, all the way home."

## Shela Vareth — The Architect of the Welcome Place

Less mythologised than Tharúven, but present in the legendary tradition nonetheless. Shela Vareth (Era 38–97) designed Nibiru — the city you are in. In the legendary tradition, Shela is a figure of precise imagination: someone who could see a completed thing in a form that did not yet exist, and build toward that vision across a lifetime.

The story told about Shela Vareth is simple. When they were first given the commission for Nibiru — a city for visiting species, to be built inside a moon — they went away for three thara'keth without designing anything. They came back and said: *"I needed to spend some time being a visitor before I could design for one."* In those three thara'keth, they had travelled to every species then in contact with the Furlings, and stayed with each long enough to understand what it felt like to arrive somewhere unknown and be received there.

The building you are moving through is the result.

## Others in the Tradition

**Bulutha** — the last Furling to work in the Milky Way, who visited Earth and left a hidden message in clay. In the legendary tradition, Bulutha is the figure of patient hope: someone who, with no certainty that their message would ever be found, left it anyway. Their memory crystal is in the archive at Keeper access. Their legend is available to anyone.

**Omavel the Healer** — not the historical Omavel Thren of this archive's medical records, but an older legendary figure of the same name from Era 23, who in the founding stories of Furling medicine walked into a plague-stricken settlement when everyone else walked away, and came out alive with everyone inside. The historical facts of this story are uncertain. The values it carries are not.
