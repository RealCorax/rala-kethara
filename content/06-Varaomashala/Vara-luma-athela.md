# <span class="furling-pin">Vara'luma'athela</span> | The Music

> *Furling music includes registers humans cannot hear. This is not a flaw in the recordings or a translation problem. It is simply true. The full piece is not accessible to human ears without technology. What you can hear is still the music. It is still worth hearing. But knowing this — that you are hearing something that has a dimension you cannot access — seems important to say at the start.*
> — Shala Reva-Vel, Era 138

## What Furling Music Is

We hear more than most species. The Furling audible range extends significantly beyond human limits in both directions. This shaped our music from its earliest forms: compositions that move through registers beyond the range of other species, harmonics that interact at frequencies only Furling ears resolve, rhythmic structures built on the breath cycles of beings who speak from stillness.

The result is a tradition that, when translated for other species, is genuinely present but incompletely accessible. The recordings in this archive are complete. What a Furling hears and what a human hears from the same recording are different amounts of the same thing.

What survives the difference: the emotional register. The quality of space in Furling music — the use of silence, the pace of development, the patience of a tradition that does not believe music needs to fill every moment — crosses species lines well. Visitors who spend time with the recordings often describe the experience as unusual, then settling, then difficult to leave.

## The Three Featured Composers

The visitor collection includes representative works from three composers, chosen to show the breadth of the tradition across its long history.

---

**Thelo Varamis** — Era ~61, ceremonial choral tradition

Born in the generation after the Great War, when Mira was still scarred and the question of what would come next was not settled. Thelo's work carries the shape of grief without becoming grief — the vara'thara form, a single repeating melodic phrase that changes meaning through context rather than variation. Their masterwork *Korath Vel* was a five-hour unaccompanied choral piece for the first great post-unification public ceremony. No instruments. Breath as rhythm. Community made audible.

Their music is spare, resonant, patient. Listen to it in a large space if you can.

**Selethara Nai-Vel** — Era ~89, encounter and wonder

The first Furling composer to spend extended time in contact with other species' music before composing their own response to it. Selethara worked as a survey musician — a role they largely created — travelling with contact teams and documenting sonic traditions from newly encountered civilisations. Their compositions weave Furling structure around sounds and scales from other worlds.

*Rala'velun Kethavar* — "Signal-Eternal Across the Dark" — is their most performed work: a piece for solo voice with handpan, harp, and deep ambient resonance, written after their first encounter with a species whose entire language was music. There is a held silence in the middle of the piece, marked in the score with an ellipsis. Selethara never wrote what it means. Most performers hold it for longer each time they play it.

**Reva Naleth** — Era 107–141, late era, leave-taking

Reva's music is the most immediately accessible to most visiting species — warm, melodic, structured around familiar harmonic relationships rather than the more complex Furling extended range. They were also a practical thinker who compiled the archive's portal user guide, and there is something in their music that reflects that quality: it wants to be useful, not just beautiful.

Their final work, *Vetha'mai Nibiru* — "Life-Greetings, Nibiru" — was performed in this city's main performance hall in Era 140, for four hundred Furlings who understood what was coming. It is not a farewell song. Reva said directly that she did not want to write one. What she wrote instead says: *we were here, this was real, come back.* The recording is in the archive. It is the last piece of Furling music composed in Nibiru.

Reva died in transit before reaching the Milky Way. She was thirty-four thara'keth old.
