# <span class="furling-pin">Omavel Reva-Thara</span> | Contemplative Teacher and Spiritual Scholar

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 87–148 · Mira, then Nibiru · Contemplative Practice, Spiritual Philosophy, Cross-Species Spiritual Dialogue

## Life and Work

Omavel Reva-Thara is the scholar most commonly associated with the late-era development of Furling spiritual philosophy. This is not entirely accurate — they would have been the first to say so — because the tradition they wrote about had been developing for a hundred thousand thara'keth before they were born. What Omavel did was articulate, clearly and accessibly, what had previously been transmitted primarily through practice rather than through text, and make it available to visiting species who arrived at Nibiru without any prior relationship to the tradition.

They came from a background in contemplative practice rather than academic study: they had spent fifteen thara'keth as a practitioner before they began writing about the practice, which gave their records a grounded quality distinguishable from those compiled by scholars who understood the practice theoretically but had not lived it. The description of vel'nai in the 06.04.05 record, colleagues noted, reads like someone describing what they actually do in the morning, not what they believe should be done. This is because it is.

Omavel arrived at Nibiru in Era 118 at the invitation of the spiritual philosophy division, to help develop the visitor-level materials for a branch that had been documented extensively at higher access levels but incompletely for newcomers. They spent thirty thara'keth in this work, producing four major visitor-level records and a substantial body of supporting material at Student and Citizen access. They also ran regular contemplative sessions for visiting scholars who wanted to experience the practices directly rather than read about them.

They were among the last to leave Nibiru during the evacuation. They helped ensure that the contemplative spaces were in order — the reading chambers clean, the equipment for the partner practice sessions properly stored — before departing. Subsequent records are not available in this archive.

- *Rala'mai'veth — shala* — cross-species spiritual dialogue: what other traditions taught us about our own (Era 131, Student access)

## A Note on Their Influence

Omavel Reva-Thara's visitor-level records on spirituality are, by the assessment of subsequent scholars, the most-read documents in the Vara'oma'shala branch. This may be partly because they are genuinely well-written. It may also be because the questions they address — what is sacred, why ceremony, how to live with awareness of death — are the kind of questions that species bring with them regardless of their origin, and Omavel's answers are honest rather than reassuring.
