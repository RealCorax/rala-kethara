# <span class="furling-pin">Vara'athel'veth</span> | The Light They Made Before the Silence

> *Their cities were not built — they were composed. Their public spaces contained works of art of a quality I have not seen matched in thirty thara'keth of survey work. And they knew they were going to die soon. Every individual we observed lived approximately 35–38 thara'keth. They knew this. And yet what they made contained no resignation, no bitterness, no evidence that the shortness of life had diminished their investment in it. I find this extraordinary.*
> — Thara'vel Keth-Oun, Survey Report, Era 110

>> This section of the archive holds the complete creative record of the Athel'kin before their transformation. We have preserved it in full. It requires particular care in presentation, and we ask that you bring particular care in reception. What you are reading is the work of people who no longer exist in the form that made it.

## Who the Athel'kin Were

Before contact, before the Vetha'meva, before everything that followed — the Athel'kin were a peaceful, nomadic people organised into five Houses, with a natural lifespan of thirty to forty thara'keth.

This is the fact that shaped their entire creative tradition. Thirty to forty thara'keth. It is not long enough to see a full generation grow to adulthood and raise their own children. Not long enough to complete most of the projects that a longer-lived species would consider substantial. The Athel'kin knew this. They had always known it. Teaching children was the highest cultural value across all five Houses not because they were especially devoted to education, but because every elder understood that when they died, everything they knew would die with them unless they had given it away completely.

The creative work that came from this is unlike anything else in the archive.

## The Art

Athel'kin visual art is immediate. This is the only word that accurately describes the quality that distinguished it from every other tradition in the archive. Where Furling art uses patience — long development, layered meaning, forms that reveal themselves across time — Athel'kin art is fully present in the first moment you encounter it. It has to be. The artist making it knew they might not be alive when it was finished if they didn't work quickly enough; the person receiving it might not be alive to see a slow build-up pay off.

The materials were what was local: clay, wood, plant-fibre pigments, stone worked with simple tools. What was done with these materials is extraordinary. Athel'kin carvings in the archive collection show a command of spatial composition that Furling art scholars spent decades trying to articulate. The best explanation, reached eventually, is that the Athel'kin understood negative space — the empty areas in and around a form — as a primary element of the work rather than an absence of it. A carved figure is also the shape of the air around it. Both matter.

Their colour use was expressive rather than representational and was tied to emotional and social meaning that our translators have partially reconstructed: certain combinations mark grief, others celebration, others the specific mood of a piece of music being referenced visually. Athel'kin art and Athel'kin music were not separate traditions. They talked to each other constantly.

## The Music

The Athel'kin had no equivalent of Furling music's extended range. Their instruments were percussive, resonant, and remarkable for their use of overlapping rhythmic cycles — patterns that were individually simple but created complex emergent structures when layered.

Their vocal tradition was the heart of the music. Every House had song-keepers whose function was precisely what the word describes: to hold the accumulated music of their House in memory and voice, to teach it to the next generation, to add to it carefully. A song-keeper's repertoire was the House's complete musical history — erasing a song-keeper was understood as equivalent to burning a library.

One song is held in the archive's featured collection, documented by our observers during the contact period. It is a House Geirroh (Sky People) song called, approximately, *"The Name We Give the Morning."* It is sung at first light, collectively, by the whole House. The melody is simple. The harmonics that emerge when many voices sing it together in a natural space are not.

## The Literature

The Athel'kin writing system was developed late relative to their other cultural achievements — only a few generations before Furling contact. Prior to writing, all their literature was oral, carried by memory-keepers analogous to the song-keepers but for narrative.

What exists in the archive is a mixture: written records from the contact period, and oral transcriptions made by Furling observers from memory-keeper recitation. The oral tradition has a different quality — the repetitions, the listener-address, the endings that are pauses rather than conclusions — but the written works are already more developed than their short history of writing would suggest. A species that has been composing for a long time, even without writing, learns the forms thoroughly before ever putting them down.

The most striking quality of Athel'kin literature is its relationship with impermanence. Not preoccupation — relationship. A human or Furling reader encountering this literature might expect, given the short lifespan, a certain urgency or melancholy. What is actually present is something more careful: an acknowledgement that things end, and a refusal to treat that acknowledgement as a reason to love them less. The closest Furling philosophical tradition is the Quiet Path afterlife tradition. The Athel'kin did not need a philosophical tradition for this. It was simply how they wrote.

## A Note on What Comes After

This section covers the creative work of the Athel'kin before their transformation. The Keth'alis — what the Athel'kin became — are a different matter entirely, documented elsewhere in the archive at higher access levels.

We have made a deliberate choice in how this section is organised: the pre-transformation work is here, at Visitor level, visible to anyone who enters this branch. We made this choice because the Athel'kin who made these works deserve to be known as who they were. The Keth'alis are not what they made. They are what was done to what they made.

The music, the art, the literature — this was the Athel'kin. Not what came after. This.
