# <span class="furling-pin">Vel Thara-Omaven</span> | Mythologist and Oral Tradition Scholar

> [!person-portrait]
> Era 61–128 · Mira, various communities · Oral Tradition, Mythology, Comparative Cultural Studies

## Life and Work

Vel Thara-Omaven was sixty-one thara'keth old when they died, which in the pre-lifespan-extension era would have been an unremarkable age. In the period when they lived — Era 61 to Era 128 — Furlings of their generation could expect to reach four hundred or more. Vel died of a condition that resisted treatment, at an age they described in personal correspondence as "early but not tragic." The archive holds their complete works and several collections of personal writing. They are, by most assessments, one of the clearest thinkers in the mythology section.

Vel came to mythology from an unusual direction: they were trained as a linguist, specifically in the oral tradition reconstruction methods that allowed scholars to work backward from early written texts to probable oral originals. This gave them a tool that many mythology scholars lacked — the ability to assess when a written version of a story had been shaped by the constraints of writing itself, and to recover, approximately, what the spoken form might have carried that the written form had lost.

The mythology records they compiled for the Vara'oma'shala branch were produced across a period of twenty thara'keth, during which Vel travelled extensively among communities that still maintained strong oral tradition practices, collecting variants of the major mythological cycles and comparing them to archive records. The creation myth records in particular draw on this fieldwork: the four traditions presented are not abstract summaries but reconstructions based on living practice as Vel observed it.

Their approach to the material was consistent: take it seriously as what it is, not as a failed attempt at something else. The myths are not primitive science. They are something that science is not and does not need to replace.
