>> The Furling language contains approximately 4,200 documented root words, from which all compound terms in the archive are formed. This reference holds every root, common compound, grammatical particle, and phrase recorded across the archive's history. It is searchable by sound, by meaning, and by category.

## On Using This Reference

The dictionary is organised by root rather than by alphabetical sequence, because the roots are the architecture of the language. A Furling word is rarely arbitrary it is a combination of roots that carries the meaning of its parts. Knowing the roots allows you to read new words rather than memorise them.

The wrist device translation function draws from this same vocabulary database. If a word appears in the archive that your device cannot translate, it is either a very old form or a specialist technical term held at higher access levels.

The living voice reference opens in a dedicated viewer. Use the search field to find any word by its Furling sound or its meaning. Filter by category to browse related terms. The font toggle in the viewer cycles between romanised and Furling-script display.
