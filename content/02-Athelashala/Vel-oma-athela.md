# <span class="furling-pin">Vel'oma'athela</span> | Social Foundations and the Kora'thesh Veshúnar

> *"We are sometimes asked what holds us together — what, in the absence of a state church or a ruling family or a compulsory ideology, keeps a civilisation coherent across thousands of worlds and one hundred and thirty-four thousand thara'keth. The answer is not complicated. It is simply that we agreed, a very long time ago, on what mattered. And we keep agreeing."*

>> This record introduces the social foundations of Furling civilisation: the values and structures that underlie everything else in this branch. The Five Principles themselves are documented in full in the companion record. This page addresses what they are, where they came from, and what it means that a people would make values rather than law the foundation of their society.

## What the Principles Are Not

The <span class="furling-pin-inline">Kora'thesh Veshúnar</span> is not a legal code. It is not a constitution. It is not a religious text, though some Furlings have always read it with something close to reverence. It does not compel specific behaviours. There is no authority that enforces it. A Furling who acts against the Principles does not face legal sanction from the principles themselves — they face the much more present reality of what their community thinks of them.

This distinction is important. Most species that have asked us about our governance assume that values must be backed by enforcement to be effective. Our experience across one hundred and thirty-four thousand thara'keth is that the opposite is often true: values backed only by enforcement produce compliance. Values held genuinely produce something else.

## Where They Came From

The Five Principles in their modern form trace directly to the reconstruction period following the Great War of Era 12. That war killed a significant proportion of the Furling population. The survivors, surveying the scale of what had happened, faced a specific question: what had gone wrong, and how could they prevent it from happening again?

Tharúven Kora'thesh, whose unification effort in the reconstruction is documented in the history section, understood that new laws and new governance structures were necessary but not sufficient. The communities that had fought each other had all had laws. They had had governance structures. What they had lacked was a shared account of what those laws and structures were for.

The original <span class="furling-pin-inline">Korath Veshun</span> that Kora'thesh helped articulate in the early reconstruction was simpler than the modern version — five short phrases in Proto-Furling, closer to commitments than to principles. They were not presented as commandments. They were presented as observations: these are the things that the survivors, looking back at what had happened, agreed were the things that mattered.

## How Values Become Identity

The question historians return to is why the Principles held. Laws can be repealed. Constitutions can be amended. Religious texts can be reinterpreted. The Kora'thesh Veshúnar has been revised twice across its history, and it has held through one hundred and thirty-four thousand thara'keth of a civilisation that expanded across thousands of worlds.

Our understanding is that the Principles held because they were transmitted not as external rules but as genuine identity. Every Furling child, from their earliest kinara years, encounters the Principles through story, through the behaviour of the adults around them, through the way the greater-family makes its small decisions. By the time a Furling is old enough to think critically about the Principles, they are not evaluating an ideology. They are examining something that feels like themselves.

This is not indoctrination. The Principles are discussed, questioned, and debated throughout a Furling life. The archive itself holds the records of those debates. What the transmission achieves is not unthinking acceptance but rather genuine ownership: the sense that these values belong to you because you have made them yours.
