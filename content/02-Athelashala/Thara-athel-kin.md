# <span class="furling-pin">Thara'athel'kin</span> | The Athel'kin — Contact and the Life-Gift

> *"They were extraordinary. I want that on record before everything else that follows. Whatever the Athel'kin became, what they were when we found them was extraordinary."*

>> This record covers the Furling contact with the Athel'kin and the gift of the Vetha'meva. What happened afterward — the discovery and the war that followed — is covered in the records below this one. The full account of the Keth'alis is held at Deep Circle access. This record covers what visitors need to understand: who the Athel'kin were, what we gave them, and the nature of what arose from that gift.

## Who the Athel'kin Were

The Athel'kin were a small, short-lived species native to their homeworld. Their natural lifespan was 30 to 40 thara'keth — brief, by our standards, but they used it with remarkable intensity. They were artists by temperament and deep organisational commitment: their civilisation was structured around the creation and sharing of creative works. Music, visual art, narrative, ceremony — these were not leisure activities but the central organising purpose of their society.

What made the Athel'kin remarkable was the quality of attention they brought to their short lives. Because they knew they had so little time, they wasted almost none of it. A piece of music composed knowing it would be the last the composer completed had a quality that is difficult to describe in language but unmistakeable when encountered. The archive holds extensive records of Athel'kin creative work from this period at Student access.

They were peaceful. Genuinely, constitutionally peaceful — not from philosophical choice in the way our peace is a choice built on the memory of the Great War, but from something that seemed closer to nature. In the forty thara'keth of observation before formal contact, not a single armed conflict was recorded among them.

## The Vetha'meva

After formal contact and an extended period of relationship-building, a proposal was brought before the Council of 3: to offer the Athel'kin a genetic therapy that could extend their natural lifespan to 300 to 400 thara'keth. The therapy was called <span class="furling-pin-inline">Vetha'meva</span> — the Life-Gift.

The debate within the Council was substantive and honest. The case for offering it was straightforward: these were beings of extraordinary capacity whose lives were so short that much of their potential was simply lost before it could develop. The case against was the standard Furling caution about intervention: we do not know all the consequences of changing a species' biological relationship with time. We cannot know.

The debate produced the following record, which is often cited:

*"The team evaluating this proposal includes members who have watched the Athel'kin for forty thara'keth. They have watched them create things that will outlast them. They have watched them say goodbye to their children. The question is not whether the Vetha'meva is safe. We believe it is safe. The question is whether we have the right to change the relationship between a people and their own mortality. The answer we have arrived at is: yes, if they choose it freely, with full understanding of what is known and what is not known."*

The Athel'kin were approached. The offer was explained — what it was, what it would do, what was known and what was not known. The Athel'kin accepted. The therapy was administered.

Initial results were everything the Furlings had hoped. Athel'kin living ten times longer than their parents had began developing in ways that seemed to confirm the gift was what it was intended to be: more time for extraordinary minds to do what they did.

## The Proverb

Among us, the consequences of the Vetha'meva are recalled in a single line: *"Even stars, in their generosity, can burn the worlds they warm."*

What followed is documented in the records below. What the Athel'kin became, and what we were required to do in response, belongs to the hardest chapters of our history. It is recorded here not to diminish what the Athel'kin were before, but because an honest archive does not look away from what its own actions set in motion.
