# <span class="furling-pin">Veth'thara'athela</span> | Biological Development and Life Stages

> *"A Furling child at ten thara'keth and a Furling elder at four hundred are recognisably the same people, which surprises most visiting species when I describe it. We change, of course. But we do not change the way species with short lives change — frantically, in great lurching leaps. We drift. And across centuries of drifting, we come to know ourselves very well."*

>> This record covers the biological stages of a Furling life: from birth through the five recognised life stages to natural death. The extended lifespan achieved from Era 100 onward is addressed separately in the companion record on longevity research.

## The Five Stages

Furling biological development does not divide neatly into the sharp demarcations that some species experience. The stages are recognised as useful frameworks rather than absolute categories.

**Kinara** spans from birth to approximately ten thara'keth. The kinara period is characterised by rapid development, high learning plasticity, and an almost total integration into the greater-family structure. Kinara are not considered responsible for their actions in the way adults are — not because we consider them lesser, but because we understand that full moral agency requires a biological development of the prefrontal and empathic systems that is genuinely not complete until later. Kinara belong to everyone. Every adult in a greater-family considers themselves responsible for every child in it.

**Grown Child** spans from ten to seventeen thara'keth. This stage is the period of formal education and early specialisation. The Grown Child begins contributing small amounts of community duty, usually physical tasks in the farm quarters or civic maintenance — not as labour extraction but as the beginning of learning what it means to participate. The biological changes of this period are gradual and involve the continued development of the empathic and relational neural systems that define adult Furling consciousness.

**Adult** begins at eighteen thara'keth, marked by the <span class="furling-pin-inline">vel'shora</span> ceremony. The vel'shora marks are applied; the Furling is considered fully responsible for their actions and fully entitled to participate in community decisions. Adult biology is stable for a long period — a Furling in their first century of adulthood is functionally not ageing in any meaningful sense.

**Grown Adult** is the informal term for a Furling between roughly one hundred and two hundred thara'keth, where accumulated experience begins to manifest in physical ways that others can read. The face softens in particular ways. The vel'shora marks deepen slightly in colour. The voice, already unhurried, becomes something else: not slow, but settled.

**Senior** designates the final period before natural death, which occurs at roughly two hundred thara'keth without medical extension. A Senior Furling is not diminished. Cognitive function remains high. Sensory acuity decreases somewhat, particularly hearing at the upper ranges. What changes is priority: most Seniors shift their community contribution heavily toward teaching, record-keeping, and the kind of long-view counsel that only centuries of experience can give.

## Extended Lifespan

From approximately Era 100, Furling medicine developed the means to extend a natural lifespan of two hundred thara'keth to four hundred or five hundred. This is not a uniform process: individual Furlings make individual choices about whether and how much to extend, and the extension requires ongoing medical engagement rather than a single intervention.

The result is that multi-generational overlap within a single greater-family became the norm. Six generations knowing each other simultaneously is not unusual. The philosophical implications of this — what it means to know your great-great-great-grandchildren while you are still biologically active and contributing — are explored in the philosophy sections of this branch.

We note here, and we ask that it be understood clearly: the extended lifespan the Furlings achieved is a product of science applied to our own biology. It involved no other species, no harvesting of anything from any living being, and no compromise of the values that hold our civilisation together. We name this distinction not to lecture, but because it is important that the record be unambiguous.
