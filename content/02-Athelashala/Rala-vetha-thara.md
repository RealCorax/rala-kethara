# <span class="furling-pin">Rala'vetha'thara</span> | The Verb System — Four Classes and Complete Tables

> *"The four classes are not a filing system. They are a philosophy. When you ask what class a verb belongs to, you are asking what kind of thing that action is. Not how it works grammatically. What kind of event it is in the world."*

>> This record is a companion to the complete grammar. It covers the four verb classes, their conjugation tables, and the eight irregular verbs that predate the class system entirely. Read the grammar record first if you are new to the language.

## Why Four Classes

Furling verbs are organised into four classes based on the nature of the relationship they describe. This is not arbitrary. It reflects the Furling understanding that different kinds of events have fundamentally different qualities of being.

A speaker knows from the verb ending what kind of action is taking place, even before understanding the specific word. The class is information about the nature of the event, carried in the sound itself.

## Class 1 — Velelan Class

*Verbs of being and state.* The subject simply exists in a condition. Nothing is done to anything. These are intransitive, inward verbs.

| Form | Ending | Example: *skelan* (to belong) |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **-elan** | *skelan* |
| Present | **-en** | *skelen* |
| Past (settled) | **-enma** | *skelenma* |
| Future (turning) | **-envai** | *skelenvai* |
| Negated | skar + present | *skar skelen* |

Common Class 1 verbs include: *skelan* (to belong), *velelan* (to be alive as a sustained state), *omēlan* (to be at peace), *kethelan* (to endure through time), *skar'skelan* (to be lost, without belonging).

## Class 2 — Nalithar Class

*Verbs of flow and transfer.* Something moves from one being or place to another. These are the relational verbs, the most used class in the language, because Furling thought is organised around relationships.

| Form | Ending | Example: *mevithar* (to give) |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **-ithar** | *mevithar* |
| Present | **-ith** | *mevith* |
| Past (settled) | **-ithma** | *mevithma* |
| Future (turning) | **-ithai** | *mevithai* |
| Negated | skar + present | *skar mevith* |

Note: Class 2 future uses *-ithai* rather than the expected *-ithvai*. This is the only class-level irregularity in the system.

Common Class 2 verbs include: *mevithar* (to give), *ralithar* (to speak toward), *shavithar* (to teach), *renithar* (to help), *melovithar* (to receive), *shalovithar* (to share so both hold it).

## Class 3 — Theovan Class

*Verbs of making and changing.* Deliberate acts that alter the state of something. Always transitive — something is acted upon. These are the verbs of creation, construction, repair, and transformation.

| Form | Ending | Example: *skelovan* (to build) |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **-ovan** | *skelovan* |
| Present | **-ov** | *skelov* |
| Past (settled) | **-ovma** | *skelovma* |
| Future (turning) | **-ovai** | *skelovai* |
| Negated | skar + present | *skar skelov* |

Common Class 3 verbs include: *skelovan* (to build), *vethovan* (to activate, bring to life), *venorovovan* (to restore, return to wholeness), *shalovovan* (to record, make permanent), *theovovan* (to create deliberately).

## Class 4 — Hēlelan Class

*Verbs of sensing and knowing.* Mental and sensory states — things that happen inside. The long vowel ē marks these as internal and experiential. They are held slightly longer when spoken, because the events they describe take place within rather than between.

| Form | Ending | Example: *shālēlar* (to know) |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **-ēlar** | *shālēlar* |
| Present | **-ēl** | *shālēl* |
| Past (settled) | **-ēlma** | *shālēlma* |
| Future (turning) | **-ēlvai** | *shālēlvai* |
| Negated | skar + present | *skar shālēl* |

Common Class 4 verbs include: *shālēlar* (to know), *veshēlar* (to see), *helēlar* (to feel), *milēlar* (to remember), *omēlar* (to choose), *vethēlar* (to hope), *helovairēlar* (to grieve), *omalovēlar* (to forgive).

## The Eight Irregular Verbs

The oldest, most fundamental verbs resist regularisation. There are exactly eight. They predate the class system and must be learned individually.

| Infinitive | Meaning | Present | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Velar** | To live, to be alive | vel | velma | velvai |
| **Renar** | To help, to be in helping-connection | ren | renma | renvai |
| **Nalar** | To flow (physical) | nal | nalma | nalvai |
| **Mevar** | To give, to offer | mev | mevma | mevai |
| **Thevar** | To create, to bring into being | thev | thevma | thevai |
| **Thalar** | To guard, to hold safe | thal | thalma | thalvai |
| **Valar** | To shine, to illuminate | val | valma | valvai |
| **Ralar** | To speak, to say | ral | ralma | ralvai |

Each of these verbs is irregular for the same reason: it describes something so fundamental that it existed before the system that would have organised it. Life, help, flow, giving, creation, protection, light, speech. The class system was built around these verbs, not the other way around. They are the ground the system stands on.

> [!arc-note]
> Adverbs are formed by compounding a quality-root directly to the verb root with an apostrophe, following the verb. In everyday speech this compound often shortens and the apostrophe becomes a dash, marking the adverb as fused. The meaning is unchanged.
