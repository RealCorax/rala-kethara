# <span class="furling-pin">Rala'shala'athela</span> | Modern High Furling Complete Grammar

*"I have spent forty thara'keth studying our language and I am still not certain I speak it well. What I am certain of is this: the grammar is not a set of rules to memorise. It is a way of seeing. Once you see it, you cannot unsee it, and you will find it in everything."*

>> This record covers the full grammar of Modern High Furling as spoken and written in Eras 121 to 150. It is the primary reference for all documents in this archive. If you are learning the language for the first time, begin with the sentence structure section, then move to the verb system, then return to the details as you need them.

## How the Language Sees the World

Most languages begin with an actor, a subject who does something to an object. In those languages the world is made of separate things pushing against each other.

Furling does not see the world this way. A Furling sentence begins with a relationship, the connection between two things, and asks: what is the nature of what flows between them?

*"I see you"* becomes *"seeing flows from me toward you."* The star does not warm the world. Warmth flows from <span class="furling-pin-inline">Vara</span> toward <span class="furling-pin-inline">Mira</span>. This is not poetry. This is grammar. The basic sentence order is: relationship, source, destination, circumstance.

The pronoun <span class="furling-pin-inline">no</span> (I) rarely opens a sentence. To begin with *no* is emphatic, almost insistent. In ordinary speech we begin with the relationship and let the self appear as a participant. This is not a rule. It is a habit rooted in the belief that the Whole is larger than any one self within it.

## The Flow Particle

<span class="furling-pin">La</span> is the heart of the language. It marks the direction of any relationship: from where, toward where, between whom.

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **la'no** | flowing from me |
| **la'lei** | flowing toward you |
| **la'maha** | flowing between us |
| **la'leis** | flowing toward all of you |
| **la'varo** | flowing toward or from them |
| **la'[name]** | flowing from or toward a named person or place |

In archive register, flow particles are always written in full. In educated speech they fuse into verb endings. In everyday speech they collapse to implication. The meaning does not change. Only how much the speaker makes it visible.

## Aspect, Not Tense

Furling does not mark tense. Time is understood as cyclical: events are patterns that recur, not points that pass forever. What the language marks instead is aspect, the quality of a moment within a cycle.

| Suffix | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *(none)* | Flowing | Present, alive inside the moment |
| **·ma** | Settled | Completed; the cycle closed, its mark remains |
| **·vai** | Turning | Approaching; the cycle moves toward this |

## Evidential Particles

Every statement in formal speech carries a particle marking how the speaker knows what they claim. This is not optional in archive register.

| Particle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **·shala** | Known from study, evidence, research |
| **·rala** | Known from testimony or record |
| **·vetha** | Known from direct lived experience |
| **·thala** | Held carefully; uncertain |

The particle <span class="furling-pin-inline">·thala</span> is never dropped in any register. Using it marks honesty, not weakness. A speaker who states something uncertain without ·thala is considered careless. The archive itself marks uncertainty this way throughout.

## Compound Formation

Words are built by joining roots. The pattern is always: first root is the subject, second root is the lens through which it is understood.

<span class="furling-pin-inline">Mira'shala</span> — earth through the lens of wisdom, which is geology. <span class="furling-pin-inline">Oma'vetha</span> — peace through the lens of life, which is active living peace rather than mere absence of conflict. Three-root compounds extend the same logic outward.

This pattern is fully productive. A speaker who knows the roots can decode any compound they have never seen before. The roots are not arbitrary sounds. They carry meaning in every context in which they appear.

## The Three Registers

We speak differently depending on where we are and who is listening.

Archive and ceremonial register writes everything in full: flow particles, evidential particles, complete compounds, the self entering late. This is the register of all documents in this archive and of Selavai's formal greetings.

Educated speech compresses: flow direction fuses into verb endings, evidentials shorten to single syllables, compounds may abbreviate. This is how most Furlings speak with colleagues and friends who share their background.

Everyday speech compresses further: flow direction is implied by context, evidentials appear only when flagging genuine uncertainty, compounds shorten freely. Speed, however, does not increase. Pace remains unhurried in all three registers. A speaker in a hurry is not speaking Furling. They are speaking their own anxiety with Furling words in it.
