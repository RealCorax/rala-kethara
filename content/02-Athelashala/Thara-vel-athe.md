# <span class="furling-pin">Thara'vel'athela</span> | Furling History — The Deep Time and the Founding

> *"We do not study our history because it is comfortable. We study it because it is ours — the beautiful and the shameful alike. A people who know only their victories know nothing about themselves."*

>> This record covers the earliest period of Furling civilisation, from prehistoric origins through the Great War of Era 12 and the Reconstruction that followed. The full historical record is extensive. This is the foundation from which everything else in our history grows.

## The Earliest Furlings

The earliest evidence of our presence on [[Mira]] comes from archaeological sites in the crystal-ridge territories of the Miravel continent. The oldest of these, Vel'shael-Nai, predates Era 0 by at least sixty thara'keth. Small communal groups living in natural crystal-cave formations, using bioluminescent crystal as both tool and light source.

We were not always what we became. The earliest Furlings were hunter-gatherers. The transition to cultivation came later, and more slowly than in some species we have since encountered. Some scholars interpret this as evidence that early agricultural knowledge was introduced from outside. Others hold that our agricultural development was simply at a different pace, and requires no outside explanation. Neither position has been definitively resolved, and both are preserved here as equally held scholarly interpretations.

## The Vel'athara

Across every regional oral tradition of Mira, a consistent account exists of two beings — always two — who arrived among the early Furlings during the pre-tribal or early tribal period. They are described as tall, luminous, calm. They taught: the cultivation of the river grain that became the basis of our agricultural system, the first formal structuring of spoken language, construction using shaped crystal, reading of the stars for seasonal navigation. And the concept of the greater-family — that those beyond your bloodline are still your family. This last teaching is considered by many historians to be the most significant, because it forms the philosophical root of everything that came after.

The <span class="furling-pin-inline">Vel'athara</span> are interpreted through three scholarly traditions that coexist in our civilisation. The Vel'athara Faithful hold them as real beings of genuine ancient power whose departure was not abandonment but withdrawal for reasons beyond our understanding. The Shala'vel tradition interprets them as advanced but mortal beings from a civilisation now lost, who helped us as we would later help others. The Mira'thara tradition is less concerned with what the Vel'athara were than with the specific places where the teaching occurred, which they hold as sacred in a material rather than mystical sense.

All three traditions are considered legitimate. The philosophical debate between them is one of the longest-running scholarly exchanges in our history.

## The Great War

The tribal period that followed the early settlement era was not inherently violent. The oldest oral records describe inter-tribal cooperation, shared harvest festivals, and formal exchange ceremonies. What ended this cooperation was population pressure and resource competition in a landscape that had always been finite.

The Great War reduced our population from several million to fewer than one million survivors — possibly as low as 800,000. It was planet-wide. It ended not through victory but through exhaustion: no surviving faction had the capacity or population to continue. Entire regions of Mira were rendered uninhabitable. The specific written records of the period did not survive the war. We know what happened in its broad shape. We do not know precisely why it escalated as it did, and we have had to live with that absence.

[[Archivist Shela Vorn-Mirath]] wrote of the scale of the loss:

*"Eight hundred and forty-seven thousand people. Every teacher who had ever taught. Every builder who had ever built. Every song that had only been carried in one person's memory. The Great War did not just kill people. It killed knowledge, and memory, and the social fabric that makes knowledge transmissible. Tharúven did not rebuild a civilisation. He rebuilt the possibility of one."*

## Tharúven Kora'thesh and the Reconstruction

[[Tharúven Kora'thesh]] was not a figure of pre-war significance. He held no tribal leadership position. He had been fighting since he was old enough to hold a weapon, not from ideology but because survival in his region required it. When the war ended through exhaustion, he was simply one of those who remained.

What he did in the years that followed should not have been possible. He travelled between surviving communities. He listened before he spoke. He proposed, rather than commanded. He refused to take authority for himself at every point at which it was offered to him. He directed that authority, always, toward building structures that would outlast any individual.

The result was the <span class="furling-pin-inline">Kora'thesh Veshúnar</span> — the principles that define what it means to be Furling — and the Council of 3 governance system that has guided our civilisation ever since. Tharúven died in Era 7, by natural causes, having spent his post-war life as an ordinary citizen. His grave on Mira is marked with a simple crystal-slate bearing the opening words of the Kora'thesh Veshúnar. By his explicit instruction, it bears no title.
