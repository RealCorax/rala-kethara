# <span class="furling-pin">Maha'skela'athela</span> | The Greater-Family — Community as Kin

> *"The greatest-family teaching was not invented by Tharúven. He found it in the oral traditions — it was one of the things the Vel'athara were said to have given us. He simply put it in writing and built a civilisation around it."*

>> This record covers the greater-family structure that organises Furling social and community life. It is the social implementation of the Kora'thesh Veshúnar's fifth principle: every child belongs to all of us.

## What the Greater-Family Is

The Furling biological family — two partners who have chosen each other for life, their children — is the foundation of daily personal life. But it is explicitly not the limit of responsibility or of belonging.

The greater-family is the extended community: neighbours, colleagues, those in the same area of work or study, the wider settlement. Every Furling belongs to a biological family and to a greater-family simultaneously. Obligations flow in both directions.

The practical expression of this is the community duty requirement: all Furlings contribute 100 thaeli per thara'keth to greater-family support, regardless of their field of work or their position within the community. What this contribution looks like varies by need and by individual: care of elderly members, support for families with young children, maintenance of shared spaces, teaching within the community. The form changes. The expectation does not.

## Children and the Whole

Children are understood as belonging to the Whole, not only to their biological parents. In the first ten thara'keth of a child's life, the primary carers are the biological parents — the child accompanies them to work, learns from them, is held by them. But the greater-family is present from the beginning.

Children also participate in mixed-age learning groups with others from the local community. Older children teach younger ones. The boundary between family and community, in a child's experience, is permeable from the beginning. This is intentional. A Furling who grows up understanding the whole community as kin does not have to learn this later, at the cost of having first learned something narrower.

Birth is celebrated communally. Death is celebrated communally. The significant moments of a life are not private events witnessed by a small circle. They are moments the Whole marks together.

## The Economy of the Whole

There is no currency in Furling society. Resources belong to the community in a real sense — not as an abstract principle but as a practical arrangement. Planets often specialise in particular productions: crystals from one world, particular food varieties from another, specific technologies from a third. The coordination of these specialisations is the work of the Councils of 3 and the greater-family networks they are embedded in.

Each Furling chooses their field of study and work. They are not assigned roles. The greater-family duty — the 100 thaeli per thara'keth of community contribution — is the common thread that runs through all choices. Whatever a Furling devotes their expertise to, they also tend the Whole.

## What This Requires

The greater-family structure requires something of every Furling that is not always easy: genuine attention to those beyond your immediate circle. Not performance of attention. Real attention. A Furling eating alone is gently noticed by the community. A Furling in difficulty is accompanied, not left to manage privately. The expectation of mutual awareness is continuous.

For visitors from species with different social structures, this can feel like a loss of privacy. Furlings understand this response and do not dismiss it. What we would offer in return is the observation that the sense of never being quite alone — not in difficulty, not in grief, not in confusion — is not a small thing. It is what the Whole offers in exchange for being tended.
