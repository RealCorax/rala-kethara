# <span class="furling-pin">Thara'keth'athela</span> | The Expansion Era — Worlds Beyond Mira

> *"The Kigal Galaxy, as we discovered it, was not empty. It was full of minds. That fact changed everything about who we decided to be."*

>> This record covers the long expansion era following the Reconstruction, during which Furlings spread across the Kigal Galaxy and developed the principles of contact and uplift that would define our civilisation. For individual species records, see Division 03.

## Reaching Outward

The development of portal technology and spacefaring capability came gradually in the eras following the Reconstruction. The survivors of the Great War had rebuilt something fragile and knew it. The early expansion was cautious — new worlds were observed for many thara'keth before settlement, and the lessons of what happens when a civilisation overextends without the cultural foundation to support it were vivid in living memory.

What the Furlings found, as they moved outward from Mira through the Kigal Galaxy, was that they were not alone. The galaxy was home to dozens of intelligent species at various stages of development — from pre-agricultural communities on single worlds to small multi-world civilisations with their own technology. We catalogued them all. We made contact with some. We observed others from a distance for generations without making our presence known. We protected several from threats they might not have survived.

## The Uplift Philosophy

The question of when and how to make contact with a developing species was among the most debated in Furling governance. The answer the Council of 3 developed was: slowly, and only after extensive observation.

A minimum of ten thara'keth of observation was required before any approach. A comprehensive survey using the Vel'thara Commission's detection methodology was required. A Council review was required. Where other species were already present in a system, an extended observation period before any consideration of contact was added.

This process was slow. Intentionally slow. The archive contains more than one memo from expansion-era Council officials arguing for expedited timelines. These were consistently declined.

Four species were formally uplifted during this period — given active support in developing their scientific and social infrastructure — after extended observation confirmed their readiness:

The <span class="furling-pin-inline">Shal'kora</span>: amphibious, communal by biology, with a sophisticated percussion tradition capable of transmitting complex meaning across water. Their mastery of water-based energy systems became one of their defining contributions to the alliance network.

The <span class="furling-pin-inline">Vethari</span>: long-lived by nature, predominantly solitary intellectuals whose written philosophical tradition was already extensive and sophisticated when we found them. What we offered them was something they had not had: contact with other minds. The encounter transformed their work.

The <span class="furling-pin-inline">Lúmenari</span>: ocean-dwelling, bioluminescent, their primary communication a light-based language produced across their bodies. Their understanding of light communication was, in certain respects, more sophisticated than our own when contact was made.

The <span class="furling-pin-inline">Aethen</span>: the most extended uplift programme of this period, spanning multiple generations. A species that had nearly destroyed itself through internal conflict, and chose unity. What the Aethen became after that process is considered by many scholars to be the most remarkable transformation we ever witnessed.

## The Era 56 Conflict

Not all contact in the expansion era was peaceful. The Chorathi — an insectoid species with caste-structured society organised around harmonic-communicating Resonants — were already a multi-world civilisation when we encountered them. Their expansion trajectory was moving directly into regions the Furlings had recently settled or placed under protective alliance.

Initial contact was correct on both sides. What became apparent over time was that the Chorathi held a philosophical position incompatible with the Kora'thesh Veshúnar: that resource-rich space belongs to whoever is strong enough to hold it. Low-level conflict escalated into the sustained engagement historians designate the Era 56 War.

The war was costly. It concluded through negotiated boundary agreement rather than decisive military victory. The Council record from Era 56 states plainly:

*"A peace built on proof of strength is a peace that requires continuous maintenance."*

This assessment proved accurate. The boundary held, but required continuous diplomatic work. What happened to the Chorathi under later pressures is recorded in Division 03 and in the conflict records of Division 05.
