# <span class="furling-pin">Kora'thesh Veshúnar</span> | The Five Principles — The Path That Holds Us Together

> *"These are not laws. You cannot be arrested for violating them. You cannot be tried for them. What you can be is a Furling who has violated them — and that is a different kind of accountability, one that lives in you rather than in any court."*

>> This record presents the Kora'thesh Veshúnar in its current form, with brief context for each principle. The historical versions and the philosophical debates surrounding each principle are held at Student and Citizen access.

## What the Kora'thesh Veshúnar Is

The <span class="furling-pin">Kora'thesh Veshúnar</span> — The Path That Holds Us Together — is the foundational document of Furling civilisation. [[Tharúven Kora'thesh]] created it in the period immediately following the Great War of Era 12, when fewer than one million Furlings remained alive on Mira.

It is not law. It does not specify punishments. It does not create a governing body. It defines what it means to be Furling. Every Furling child learns it. It is inscribed above the entrance to every formal archive. It is recited at every Council session. It has been revised three times across the full span of our history as our understanding deepened — but its core has not changed, because its core is not a policy. It is an identity.

## The Five Principles

**1. Vetha'shar kelúnen**
*Tend what is living, for life is the only currency.*

Every being in your care — sick, young, old, afraid — is your responsibility. Compassion is not sentiment. It is the act of keeping life whole. In practice: care for the injured, the young, and the elderly; offer guidance without judgment; prioritise healing over punishment.

**2. Vel'nar thal'shora**
*What sustains the whole must never be withheld.*

Power — whether energy, food, medicine, or knowledge — belongs to everyone. Hoarding what others need to survive is the first step toward everything we have seen go wrong when this principle is abandoned. In practice: contribute to the community, teach what you know, share resources rather than accumulate them.

**3. Shala'nar lora'veth**
*Let knowledge flow to those who need it.*

Knowledge withheld is a wound. What you know, you share. What you build, you teach. The archive exists because of this principle — not as a vault, but as a river. In practice: contribute to archives, mentor younger Furlings, share discoveries openly, teach with genuine care for the learner's understanding.

**4. Nai'ven shora'veth**
*Understand the whole before you act within it.*

The community, the ecosystem, the city — all are one system. No action exists in isolation. Before you build, research. Before you speak, listen. Before you judge, observe. The principle does not demand certainty before action — it demands genuine effort toward understanding. In practice: take time to understand the full context of what you are acting within; be honest about what you do not yet know.

**5. Ketha'nal vel'ithar**
*What is broken between living things must be made whole.*

Justice is the completion of the other four. When life is tended, resources are shared, knowledge flows, and understanding is sought — and harm still occurs — justice is what restores the whole. It comes last because it only exists when everything else has been tried.
