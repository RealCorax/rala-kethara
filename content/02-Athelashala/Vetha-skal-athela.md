# <span class="furling-pin">Vetha'skal'athela</span> | Furling Biology — Appearance and the Living Form

> *"I am occasionally asked by visiting species to describe what we look like. I find the question harder than it should be — not because the answer is complex, but because I keep wanting to describe how we move rather than what we are. The two are not separable."*

>> This record covers the physical form, senses, and biological characteristics of the Furling people. It is written as a genuine account, not a species catalogue. For the deeper biology — genetics, cellular structure, the mechanisms of the vel'shora marks — see the companion records at Student access.

## The Form

We are small by the standards of many species we have encountered. Most of us stand between 1.1 and 1.4 shael in height — compact and slender in build, proportioned in a way that is often described by other species as elegant, though we do not experience ourselves that way. Elegance is not something you notice from the inside.

Our skin is predominantly blue and green in tone — deep teal through to pale aquamarine, with individual variation across the full range between. The skin has a subtle natural pattern at the surface, visible in close light: a fine grain, like the texture of water over stone. Our eyes are large relative to our face and expressive — variations of blue and green, typically a shade or two brighter than the surrounding skin. Our ears come to a slight point. The facial features are rounded and the overall impression, we are told, is of something gentle and attentive.

We have four fingers and a thumb on each hand. The fingers are long and capable of fine manipulation — the result of an evolutionary history that relied heavily on working with small crystal formations.

## The Vel'shora

The bioluminescent marks that appear on every adult Furling are not decoration. They are considered a visible record of the self — who a Furling is at the level that cannot easily be spoken.

The <span class="furling-pin-inline">vel'shora</span> are applied at the coming-of-age ceremony at 18 thara'keth, by a practitioner called a <span class="furling-pin-inline">vel'thara</span>. The vel'thara spends considerable time in conversation with the young Furling — not taking notes or making assessments, but listening. The design emerges from what the vel'thara understands of the person they have been with. Before application, the Furling sees the proposed design and may say if something is wrong. Usually they do not change much. Usually the vel'thara has seen something true.

The marks glow softly. They are most visible in low light, subtle in daylight. Their colour is typically a brighter or complementary shade of the individual's skin tone. Placement varies: most commonly the forehead, cheekbones, jaw, neck, and upper arms. Some designs flow further. There is no rule about size or extent.

To another Furling, the vel'shora communicate regional origin, something of who the person is, and the era they came of age in. They do not communicate status, achievement, or rank. A Council member's marks look exactly as personal and specific as anyone else's.

The marks are permanent and living. They change almost imperceptibly over a Furling's lifetime — so slowly that only the Furling themselves, looking back across centuries, may notice the drift. This is considered natural. The self changes. The marks follow.

## The Senses

Our hearing extends further than most species we have encountered, both lower and higher in frequency. Sounds that other species cannot detect are part of our ordinary sensory world. Furling music includes registers that are simply inaudible to many listeners — this is why translated recordings of our music sometimes feel incomplete. The full piece is not accessible without technology.

Our vision is not stronger than most, but we resolve colour transitions with greater precision. A spectrum that appears smooth to another species shows visible gradations to us. Our visual art is consequently very dense in colour detail in ways that do not always translate to other eyes.

We are significantly more sensitive to bright light than most species. Nibiru's artificial sun is calibrated to levels comfortable for us — considerably gentler than the daylight of some worlds.

## Lifespan

Our natural lifespan runs to approximately 200 thara'keth. Through scientific extension developed around Era 100, this range expanded to 400 to 500 thara'keth. We are not immortal. The desire for immortality — and what it cost to pursue it — is one of the great tragedies recorded in this archive. We note that our extended lifespan is a product of science, not of harvesting anything from any other being. These are different things, and the difference matters completely.

Multi-generational overlap is the norm in Furling families. It is not unusual for five living generations to know each other.
