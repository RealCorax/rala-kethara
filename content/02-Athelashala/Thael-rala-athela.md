# <span class="furling-pin">Thael'rala'athela</span> | Numbers, Measurement and the Furling System of Units

> *"We count in tens because our crystals grow in tens. This is not a coincidence or a convenience. The number system and the material world that produced it are the same thing, looked at from different angles."*

>> This record covers the Furling number system, the pattern-meanings carried by each number, scaling conventions, and the standard units of measurement anchored to Mira and Vara. The full measurement reference is a companion document in Division 01.

## The Number System

Furling numbers are base-10. This is not arbitrary. The crystal kris'thral, from which the first measurement standards were derived, grows in a lattice structure with perfect three-axis symmetry at base-10 increments. The number system grew from the material that made it possible to measure anything consistently.

Each number carries a pattern-meaning alongside its quantity. These meanings are not merely poetic — they inform how the numbers are used in naming, in philosophy, and in the archive's own organisational structure.

Large numbers use the scaling roots: *vora* <span class="furling-pin">100</span>, *kora* <span class="furling-pin">1000</span>, *lhaketh* <span class="furling-pin">1'000'000'000</span>. Very large numbers use the prefix *lhā* (vast, of great scale) combined with the appropriate scaling root.

## Measurement Units

All standard Furling units are anchored to physical constants of Mira and Vara. This was a deliberate choice of the measurement commission in Era ~47: rather than abstract standards that could be lost or disputed, the units are recoverable from observation of the home system.

**Length:**
The base unit is the *shael* — defined as the length of a standardised kris'thral crystal shard under precisely specified conditions. A shael is approximately equivalent to a human forearm's length.

**Mass:**
The base unit is the *thal'keth* — defined as the mass of a standardised kris'thral shard. The crystal's unusual density and stability made it ideal for a mass reference.

**Time:**
The *tael* is the base time unit — derived from the resonance frequency of kris'thral under standard conditions. A tael is a very short unit, approximately comparable to a human second. Larger time units build from the tael:
- *Thaeli* — approximately eleven tael (a practical working unit for short tasks)
- *Mira'thara* — one complete rotation of Mira on its axis (the standard short-cycle unit)
- *Thara'keth* — one complete orbit of Mira around Vara (the standard long-cycle unit)

**Temperature:**
The *lath* scale is anchored to Mira: 0 lath is the freezing point of deep ocean water on Mira's surface; 100 lath is Mira's global average surface temperature. The scale extends below 0 and above 100 for extreme conditions.

**Stellar Measurement:**
For stellar and astronomical values, Vara itself serves as the reference point. Stellar weight, reach, and light-output are each expressed as a ratio against Vara's own value of 1.0. The full stellar classification system is held in Division 01.

## Numbers in Sentences

Numbers in Furling grammar are adjectives and follow the noun they modify. *Kinaris thara* — four children. The pattern-meaning of the number is always available to a listener as a resonance within the quantity: four children is a first-completion of children, a cycle's worth.

This is one of the places where the language's refusal to separate practical meaning from deeper meaning is most visible. We count. And in counting, we also say something about the nature of what we count.
