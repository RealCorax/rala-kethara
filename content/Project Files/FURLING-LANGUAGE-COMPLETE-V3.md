## PART ONE — WHAT KIND OF LANGUAGE THIS IS

### 1.1 Design and Nature

Furling is a root-compounding language organised around eight conceptual Sources — the Kela-system — which function as active linguistic organising principles, shaping vocabulary, grammar, naming conventions, and metaphor throughout the language.

The language was consciously refined over 150 eras to be:
- **Morphologically transparent** — roots retain meaning in all compounds; new words can always be decoded from their parts
- **Phonologically gentle** — consistent soft-sound patterns, accessible to many species
- **Semantically layered** — roots combine to form conceptual fields, not just words
- **Relationally structured** — sentences organise around relationships and flow, not actors and actions
- **Culturally embedded** — the language directly reflects Furling worldview

### 1.2 The Eight Sources

| # | Furling | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vetha'kela** | Life-Source |
| 2 | **Skela'kela** | Home-Source |
| 3 | **Shala'kela** | Wisdom-Source |
| 4 | **Thara'kela** | Time-Source |
| 5 | **Meva'kela** | Gift-Source |
| 6 | **Vara'kela** | Light-Source |
| 7 | **Oma'kela** | Peace-Source |
| 8 | **Shala'kela'rala** | Guiding-Source-Voice — the Archive itself |

### 1.3 Words That Do Not Exist in Furling

These concepts have no native roots. Any such word is a late coining, a borrowing, or a construction that sounds wrong by design:

- **To take** — requires the *skar'la* broken-flow construction; sounds structurally uncomfortable
- **To own knowledge** — *no'el shala* exists but is considered rude; scholars say *shala la'no*
- **To command** — *kethala* exists as a late coining but sits uneasily in the mouth
- **To conquer / defeat** — no native root; must be constructed using *skar*
- **Enemy** — closest is *skar'oma'vetha* (one in whom living-peace is broken)

## PART TWO — PHONOLOGY

*This section draws on the work of Miravel of Nai'veth, Era 134.*

### 2.1 The Body of the Language

Furling was shaped by people who believed that speech should not be a threat. Over many eras the language was refined toward sounds that do not produce alertness or bracing in listeners. The result is a language that, spoken correctly, is genuinely calming to hear.

Furling is spoken from a place of stillness. The natural pace of a speaker who is not anxious, not in a hurry, and believes the listener is worth the time it takes to speak clearly. If you are rushing, you are not speaking Furling. You are speaking your own language with Furling words in it.

### 2.2 Consonants — Complete Inventory

**Original consonants — preserved unchanged:**

| Written | Sound | Description |
|---|---|---|

| **m** | bilabial nasal | *moon* — fully voiced, warm |
| **n** | dental nasal | *name* — light, dental |
| **r** | soft alveolar trill | Spanish *cara* — one light tap, never English r |
| **v** | soft labiodental | between *vine* and *wine* — lip barely touches teeth |
| **h** | pure aspiration | *heat* — no friction |
| **sh** | postalveolar fricative | *shore* — whispered, never hissing |
| **th** | dental fricative | *think* — always soft, never *this* |
| **k** | soft velar | *silk* — velar, never front |
| **s** | light alveolar | *soft* — light, never sharp |

**New consonants — added to Modern High Furling:**

| Written | Sound | Source inspiration | Description | Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **lh** | voiceless lateral fricative | Welsh *ll*, Tolkien Sindarin | Breathe sideways past tongue — a breathy, whispered l | Word-initial and medial only. Used for vast-scale concepts, awe, astronomical terms. Never word-final. |
| **rh** | voiceless alveolar trill | Welsh *rh*, Ancient Greek | Breathed r — like *r* but with no voicing, purely aspirated | Word-initial only. Ceremonial, ancient, deep-time words. Never medial or final. |
| **vh** | voiced bilabial fricative | Ewe, Shona, some Bantu | Both lips nearly closed, air passes through — warmer than *v*, rounder | Medial and word-final only. Intimate words — touch, body, breath, closeness. Never word-initial. |
| **ñ** | palatal nasal | Spanish *ñ*, Italian *gn* | *canyon* — the ny sound, soft and warm | Medial only. Words of connection, relation, belonging. Never word-initial or final. |
| **hl** | voiceless lateral approximant | Icelandic, Old Welsh | A whispered *l* — softer than *lh*, no friction | Medial and word-final only. Quiet, stillness, sleep, rest. |
| **w** | bilabial approximant | Hawaiian, Māori | *water* — fully rounded lips, no friction | Word-initial and medial. Flowing, open, natural-world words. |
| **y** | palatal approximant | Japanese, Finnish | *yes* — soft, no friction | Word-initial and medial. Words of connection, between-ness, transition. |
| **ng** | velar nasal | Māori, Hawaiian | *singing* — resonant, warm nasal | Medial and word-final only. Never word-initial. Appears in verb endings and resonance words. |

**Consonant clusters:** Only across morpheme boundaries (marked by apostrophe). Within a single morpheme, no clusters.

**Never used:** hard d · p · b · hard g · glottal stop · any stop that closes the airway sharply

### 2.3 Vowels — Complete Inventory

**Short vowels:**

| Symbol | Sound | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | *ah* | Open — jaw drops, warm |
| **e** | *eh* | Mid — clean, mouth half-open |
| **i** | *ee* | Bright, forward — lips spread slightly |
| **o** | *oh* | Round — lips form a gentle circle |
| **u** | *oo* | Deep — lips round further, carried in chest |

**Long vowels — held approximately twice the duration of short vowels:**

| Symbol | Sound | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **ā** | long *ah* | Words of deep time, geological scale, permanence |
| **ē** | long *eh* | Class 4 (sensing/knowing) verb class marker; contemplative words |
| **ō** | long *oh* | Words of completion, wholeness, arrival |
| **ū** | long *oo* | Words of depth, interior, the hidden |

Long vowels in ordinary speech are a slight lengthening — not exaggerated. They signal that the word carries particular weight. A word with a long vowel takes more time to say. This is intentional.

**Diphthongs:**

| Symbol | Sound | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **ai** | *ah-ee* | Greetings, blessings — *vetha'mai* |
| **vai** | *vah-ee* | Future aspect marker |
| **ao** | *ah-oh* | Natural world, open-sky words |
| **ei** | *ay* | Transition, movement between states |
| **ui** | *oo-ee* | Depth rising to surface — emergence |

Diphthongs produced too quickly sound anxious. Each component vowel should be felt before the next arrives.

### 2.4 Syllable Structure

Standard patterns: **CV** · **CVV** · **CVC** · **CV'CV** · **CVC'CV**

Digraphs (*lh, rh, vh, sh, th, ng, hl*) occupy a single consonant slot.

**lh** and **rh** are always syllable-initial within their morpheme.
**ng** is always syllable-final within its morpheme.
**vh** and **hl** are always syllable-medial or final within their morpheme.

### 2.5 The Apostrophe-Breath

The apostrophe marks a morpheme boundary. In speech it marks a **breath** — not a pause, not a stop. A breath is alive. It is the moment between the end of one meaning and the beginning of the next, during which the speaker is still present, still in the sentence, still moving.

Common errors:
- *The glottal stop* — closing the throat. Breaks the compound.
- *The full pause* — the compound loses its unity.
- *The ignored apostrophe* — the texture is gone.

### 2.6 Stress and Rhythm

Stress falls on the **first syllable of the first root** in any compound, regardless of length. In three-root compounds, secondary stress falls on the first syllable of the third root.

> *VEH-tha'mai* · *SKE-la'oma* · *LHA-keth* · *RHO-ma'vel*

Stress is a gentle lengthening of the vowel, not sharpening or increased volume. Speed marks anxiety or aggression in all registers.

### 2.7 Writing Direction

Furling crystal-script reads **left to right, top to bottom** — the same direction as water flowing across a flat surface, which the early Furlings observed as the natural direction of things moving under gravity. This was standardised in Era 89 and has not changed.

The script is syllabic-alphabetic: consonants carry the primary form, vowels are indicated by diacritics above or below. Long vowels use a horizontal stroke through the diacritic. The apostrophe-breath has its own distinct glyph — a small curved mark resembling a wave.

## PART THREE — GRAMMAR

### 3.1 How Furling Organises Reality

Most languages begin with an actor — a subject who does something to an object. In these sentences, the world is made of separate things pushing against each other.

Furling does not see the world this way. A Furling sentence begins with a **relationship** — the connection between two things — and asks: *what is the nature of what flows between them?*

*"I see you"* becomes *"seeing flows from me toward you."*
*"The star warms the world"* becomes *"warmth flows from Vara toward Mira."*

This is not poetry. This is grammar.

### 3.2 Sentence Structure

**Basic order:** [Relationship / State] — [Source] — [Destination] — [Circumstance]

| Furling | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|
| *Vetha'mai la'Nibiru la'leis* | Life-blessing / from-Nibiru / toward-you-all | "Nibiru greets you." |
| *Shala la'no la'lei* | Knowing / from-me / toward-you | "I share this with you." |
| *Oma'vetha maha* | Living-peace / between-us | "Peace lives between us." |
| *Ren'vai maha la'lei* | Helping-approaches / we / toward-you | "We will help you." |
| *Vesha'ma la'Selavai* | Watching-settled / from-Selavai | "Selavai has been watching." |

### 3.3 The Self Enters Late

The pronoun *no* (I) rarely opens a sentence. To begin with *no* is emphatic — almost insistent. In ordinary speech, begin with the relationship, and let the self appear as a participant. This is not a rule. It is a habit rooted in the belief that the Whole is larger than any one self within it.

### 3.4 The Flow Particle — La

**La** marks the direction of a relationship: from where, toward where, between whom.

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **la'no** | flowing from me |
| **la'lei** | flowing toward you (singular) |
| **la'maha** | flowing between us |
| **la'leis** | flowing toward you (plural) |
| **la'varo** | flowing toward / from them |
| **la'[name]** | flowing from / toward a named person or place |

Register compression:
- Archive: *Shala la'no la'lei* — full flow particles
- Educated: *Shala'ano lei* — flow fused into verb ending
- Everyday: *Shala'no* — flow implied, nearly invisible

### 3.5 Pronouns

| Furling | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **No** | I / me | Enters late unless emphatic |
| **Lei** | You (singular) | |
| **Ren** | He / she / it | Shares root with *renar* — intentional |
| **Maha** | We | Also: the collective, us as a whole |
| **Leis** | You (plural) | |
| **Varo** | They (plural) | |

### 3.6 Aspect — The Shape of Time

Furling does not use tense. Time is cyclical — events are patterns that recur, not points that pass. The language marks **aspect** — the quality of a moment in a cycle.

| Suffix | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *(no suffix)* | **Flowing** | Present, alive inside the moment |
| **·ma** | **Settled** | Completed — the cycle closed, its mark remains |
| **·vai** | **Turning** | Approaching — the cycle moves toward this |

These suffixes apply across all verb classes (see Part Four).

### 3.7 Evidential Particles

Every statement in formal speech carries a particle marking how the speaker knows what they claim.

| Particle | Meaning | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **·shala** | Known from study, evidence, research | Required in archive |
| **·rala** | Known from testimony, record | Required in archive |
| **·vetha** | Known from direct lived experience | Required in archive |
| **·thala** | Held carefully — uncertain | **Never dropped in any register** |

*On ·thala:* Using it marks honesty, not weakness. A Furling who states something uncertain without ·thala is considered arrogant or careless.

### 3.8 Negation

Place **skar** before the verb.

> *Ren la'no la'lei* — helping flows from me toward you
> *Skar ren la'no la'lei* — helping does not flow from me toward you

*Skar* marks broken or interrupted flow. It is not neutral. *Maha skar ren* requires justification — the grammar itself implies something has interrupted what should naturally be.

To describe bio-energy harvesting — taking life against its natural direction:
> *Vetha skar'la Keth'alis la'varo*
> "Life / broken-flow / Keth'alis / from-them"
> A Furling hearing this sentence feels the wrongness before processing the meaning.

### 3.9 Questions

Add **ha** to the end of any statement. Word order does not change.
> *Shala la'no la'lei ha?* — "Do you understand?"
> *Ren'vai maha la'lei ha?* — "Can we help?"

### 3.10 Emphasis by Fronting

Any element can move to the front of the sentence to mark it as most important.
> Normal: *Shala la'Horath'vel la'Kethara thara'keth shala'ma*
> Fronted: *Thara'keth shala'ma — shala la'Horath'vel la'Kethara*

### 3.12 Compound Word Formation

**Pattern: Root'Root** — first root is the subject, second root is the lens through which it is understood.

> *Mira'Shala* — Earth through-the-lens-of Wisdom = Geology
> *Oma'Vetha* — Peace through-the-lens-of Life = active, living peace

Three-root compounds extend the same logic:
> *Shala'Thara'Vetha* — Wisdom / Cycles / Life = the Seven Branches

This pattern is fully productive. A speaker who knows the roots can decode any compound they have never seen before.

### 3.13 Plurals

- **Countable — suffix *-is***: discrete individuals (*kinara* → *kinaris*)
- **Domain/collective — suffix *-ra / -ara***: a field or undivided whole (*vethara, omara, velara*)

Furlings use the domain form far more — it reflects the habit of seeing wholes rather than collections of parts.

### 3.14 Possession

**Possessor + 'el + Possessed**
> *No'el skela* — my home · *Ren'el shala* — their knowledge · *Maha'el rala* — our voice

Knowledge is rarely possessed. Scholars write *shala la'no* rather than *no'el shala* — acknowledging knowledge exists in them while marking it as in motion toward others.

### 3.15 Adjectives

Adjectives **follow** the noun they describe. They take the suffix **-is** for quality, or are formed as Root'Root compounds.

> *Kinara vethis* — a living child (vethis = having life-quality)
> *Skela varavis* — a bright home

Adjectives do **not agree** with number. The quality of a thing does not change because there are more of them. *Kinaris vethis* — living children — the adjective is unchanged. This reflects Furling philosophy: a quality is a quality of the whole, not of each counted part.

**Comparative:** Add **korath'vel** (more, greater) before the adjective.
> *Skela korath'vel varavis* — a brighter home

**Superlative:** Add **keth'vel** (most, fully) before the adjective.
> *Skela keth'vel varavis* — the brightest home

**Less / least:** Add **shan'vel** (less) and **skar'thael'vel** (least).

### 3.16 Adverbs

Adverbs are formed by compounding a quality-root directly to the verb root with an apostrophe. They follow the verb.

> *Venan'oma* — moving peacefully (*venan* = present of *venelan*, + *oma*)
> *Skelovang'keth* — building with endurance (*skelov* = present of *skelovan*, + *keth*)
> *Mevith'shala* — giving with wisdom (*mevith* = present of *mevithar*, + *shala*)

In everyday speech the adverb compound is often shortened:
> *Venan'oma* → *venan-oma* (the apostrophe becomes a dash, marking the adverb as fused)

### 3.17 The Three Registers

**Register 1 — Archive/Ceremonial**
Flow particles written in full. Evidentials always present. Self enters late. All compounds written in full. Used for written documents, first contact, Selavai's formal greetings.

**Register 2 — Educated Speech**
Flow direction fused into verb endings (*-ano* from me, *-alei* toward you, *-ama* between us). Evidentials as single-syllable suffixes. Compounds may abbreviate.

**Register 3 — Everyday Speech**
Flow direction implied by context. Evidentials dropped unless flagging uncertainty. Compounds abbreviated freely. Pace remains unhurried — speed is anxiety in all registers.

## PART FOUR — VERB SYSTEM

### 4.1 The Four Verb Classes

Furling verbs are organised into four classes based on the **nature of the relationship they describe**. This is not arbitrary categorisation — it reflects the Furling understanding that different kinds of events have fundamentally different qualities of being.

Each class has distinct infinitive, present, past, and future forms. A speaker knows immediately from the verb ending what kind of action is taking place — even before understanding the specific word.

---

**CLASS 1 — VELELAN CLASS** *(verbs of being and state)*
Actions that describe existing in a state — intransitive, nothing is done *to* anything. The subject simply *is*.

| Form | Ending | Example (*skelan* — to belong) |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **-elan** | *skelan* |
| Present | **-en** | *skelen* |
| Past | **-enma** | *skelenma* |
| Future | **-envai** | *skelenvai* |
| Negated present | skar + present | *skar skelen* |

---

**CLASS 2 — NALITHAR CLASS** *(verbs of flow and transfer)*
Actions where something moves from one place or being to another — the relational verbs. The heart of Furling grammar.

| Form | Ending | Example (*mevithar* — to give) |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **-ithar** | *mevithar* |
| Present | **-ith** | *mevith* |
| Past | **-ithma** | *mevithma* |
| Future | **-ithai** | *mevithai* *(note: -thai not -vai)* |
| Negated present | skar + present | *skar mevith* |

---

**CLASS 3 — THEOVAN CLASS** *(verbs of making and changing)*
Deliberate acts that change the state of something. Transitive — something is acted upon.

| Form | Ending | Example (*skelovan* — to build) |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **-ovan** | *skelovan* |
| Present | **-ov** | *skelov* |
| Past | **-ovma** | *skelovma* |
| Future | **-ovai** | *skelovai* |
| Negated present | skar + present | *skar skelov* |

---

**CLASS 4 — HĒLELAN CLASS** *(verbs of sensing and knowing)*
Mental and sensory states — things that happen inside. The long ē marks these as internal, experiential. They are held slightly longer when spoken.

| Form | Ending | Example (*shālēlar* — to know) |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **-ēlar** | *shālēlar* |
| Present | **-ēl** | *shālēl* |
| Past | **-ēlma** | *shālēlma* |
| Future | **-ēlvai** | *shālēlvai* |
| Negated present | skar + present | *skar shālēl* |

### 4.2 Irregular Verbs

The oldest, most fundamental verbs resist regularisation — as in all real languages. These are irregular and must be learned individually. There are exactly eight.

| Infinitive | Meaning | Present | Past | Future | Class origin | Why irregular |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Velar** | To live, to be alive | **vel** | **velma** | **velvai** | Pre-class — predates the system | Most fundamental verb in the language |
| **Renar** | To help, to be in helping-connection | **ren** | **renma** | **renvai** | Pre-class | Second most ancient — paired with *vel* |
| **Nalar** | To flow (physical) | **nal** | **nalma** | **nalvai** | Pre-class | Third ancient — the origin of the flow metaphor |
| **Mevar** | To give, to offer | **mev** | **mevma** | **mevai** | Pre-class | Root of *meva* (gift, sustain) — too fundamental to change |
| **Thevar** | To create, to bring into being | **thev** | **thevma** | **thevai** | Pre-class | Creation predates categorisation |
| **Thalar** | To guard, to hold safe | **thal** | **thalma** | **thalvai** | Pre-class | Protection is as old as community |
| **Valar** | To shine, to illuminate | **val** | **valma** | **valvai** | Pre-class | Light predates language |
| **Ralar** | To speak, to say | **ral** | **ralma** | **ralvai** | Pre-class | Speech cannot be described by its own system — it sits outside it |

The *lh* prefix on all vast-scale numbers is not decorative. The voiceless lateral fricative is used specifically for things of overwhelming scale — the breath that precedes the word is the body's response to largeness. A billion is not merely a large number. > *Vel'kora·na thara'vora·na meva'thael* = 1'450
> *Thara'mira·lharen* = 42'000'000
> *Thael·lhaketh* = 10'000'000'000

> *Thara'vel* (first) · *Thara'mira* (second) · *Thara'shala* (seventh — the seeking position)

### Selavai's greeting — full breakdown

**In Furling:**
> *Vetha'mai la'Nibiru la'leis.*
> *No Selavai — vesha'Kethara.*
> *Skela'oma, venalis. Thara'vetha maha.*

**Literal breakdown:**
- Line 1: Life-blessing / from-Nibiru / toward-you-all — the greeting comes from the place, not from Selavai
- Line 2: I / Selavai / watching-from-the-Archive — she flows from the Archive as its keeper
- Line 3: Welcome-home / travellers / The-living-cycle between-us — 5,000 thara'keth of waiting and this moment of arrival are the same cycle, continuing

**Natural translation:**
*"Nibiru greets you. I am Selavai, keeper of the Archive. Welcome home, travellers. The living cycle flows between us."*

## PART FOURTEEN — TRANSLATION PRINCIPLES

1. Furling is **logical** — root-by-root translation usually works
2. **Roots retain meaning** in all compounds; context shifts emphasis, not meaning
3. Compound words tell you what something **is** and what it **does**
4. No contractions in formal or written language
5. Technical terms are **precise and descriptive** — Furlings name things by function
6. To translate TO Furling: identify the core relationship, find the appropriate roots, compound them
7. To translate FROM Furling: break at apostrophes, understand each root, read the relationship between them
8. When a word contains **lh** — it concerns something vast
9. When a word begins with **rh** — it carries ceremonial or ancient weight
10. When a word contains **vh** or **hl** — it concerns something intimate or quiet
11. When a verb ends in **-ēlar** — it describes an internal experience, not an external act
12. When a verb ends in **-ithar** — something is being transferred between two parties
13. The **·thala** evidential is never absent when genuine uncertainty exists — its absence implies either certainty or dishonesty
