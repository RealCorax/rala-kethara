## ARTIST 1 — Thelo Varamis
**Era:** ~61 (~68,000 years ago)
**Known as:** The Voice That Rebuilds
**Origin:** Miravel, Homeworld Mira
**Tradition:** Ceremonial Choral
**Archive Ref:** Vara'luma'athela 001-THV

**Bio:** Born in the generation immediately after the Great War of Era 12, when Furling population had crashed below a million. Mira was scarred, cities in rubble. Thelo learned to vocalise by listening to community grief-ceremonies, and by their eighteenth thara'keth was composing multi-movement works lasting hours. Their radical idea: music should carry the shape of grief without becoming grief. They introduced the vara'thara form — a single repeating melodic phrase that changes meaning through context rather than variation. Their most famous work, Korath Vel, was a five-hour unaccompanied choir piece for the first post-unification public ceremony. They never left Mira. Music was spare, resonant, built entirely around the Furling voice — breath as the primary instrument, harmony as the community made audible. Considered the origin point of all Furling ceremonial music.

---

### Work 1.1 — Korath Vel ("The Path Holds")
**Type:** Instrumental
**Context:** The masterwork. Five-hour ceremonial piece for the first great public gathering after unification.

**Styles:** `choral, drone, space ambient, polyphony, gregorian chant`

**Prompt:** Ancient voiceless choir, breath as rhythm, no percussion, no melody in the conventional sense — only a single slowly shifting harmonic mass that breathes in and out over long cycles. The texture is deep, resonant, cathedral-vast but not gothic. The space feels alive and patient. Multiple voices sustaining the same tonal cluster, occasionally resolving into unison and then drifting apart again. No urgency. No drive. Time has been replaced by breath.

---

### Work 1.2 — Vel'tharema ("The Path We Carried Together")
**Type:** Instrumental
**Context:** Smaller, more intimate — composed for community grief-ceremonies rather than the great public gathering.

**Styles:** `choral, dark ambient, meditation, chamber ensemble, polyphony`

**Prompt:** Two or three voices humming wordless harmony in a small stone space. Not sad — present. The texture is close and warm, low register dominant. Long held notes overlap slightly as one ends and another begins, like people taking turns to breathe. Occasionally one voice drops to near-silence while the others sustain. No rhythm. No progression in the Western sense — only a texture that persists and gradually settles into something quieter than it began.

---

### Work 1.3 — Shora'vel — Vethis Mira ("Hold Whole — Living Ground")
**Type:** With Lyrics
**Context:** One of Thelo's earliest composed works. Used to mark the end of grief-ceremonies when the community returned to daily life. The mood lifts — not into joy, but into something steadier. Ground. The emotional core: *we are still here, and that is enough to begin.*

**Styles:** `choral, drone, polyphony, space ambient`

**Prompt:** Ancient ceremonial vocal piece. Slow, deep-breathing choir. No percussion. The singing has the quality of stone and patience — not soaring, not weeping. The harmonic texture is modal and open. Vowel-heavy language that sounds like it was made to be held in the body. The final phrase should resolve into a unison hum and then silence.

**Lyrics (Furling):**

## ARTIST 2 — Selethara Oun-Mivelka
**Era:** ~105 (~40,000 years ago)
**Known as:** The Wanderer's Signal
**Origin:** Kethavar Prime (colonised world, Expansion Era)
**Tradition:** Expansionist / Ecological
**Archive Ref:** Vara'luma'athela 2204-SOM

**Bio:** Born on a settler world only four generations old, with no formal music tradition. Kethavar Prime was geologically volatile with dominant lifeforms that produced bioluminescent sound-response patterns — the world appeared to sing back when sound was introduced into certain cave systems. Selethara spent her childhood in those caves, largely self-directed. By her twenties she was composing in dialogue with a living planet, using a dual-line score: one line for the composed melody, one for the recorded world-response — treating them as equal voices. Where Thelo composed for interior experience, Selethara composed for encounter. Her works are conversations between unlike things. She developed layered structures with many simultaneous independent melodic threads that occasionally, briefly, aligned — the effect overwhelming because of how long they'd been apart. First major Furling composer to incorporate non-Furling influence, spending 40 thara'keth studying Shal'kora percussion traditions. Quoted: "The galaxy makes sound whether we listen or not. Our music is the proof that we were here and we listened."

---

### Work 2.1 — Vel'keth Mivelka ("Signal from the Deep Place")
**Type:** Instrumental
**Context:** The masterwork. 80-minute cave-composition. The dual-score structure — composed melody alongside recorded planetary sound-response — makes this categorically unlike anything produced on Mira. The most alien-sounding work in the Furling archive.

**Styles:** `space ambient, experimental ambient, organic ambient, drone, theremin, handpan`

**Prompt:** A composition that sounds like it was made inside a living planet. Deep geological bass tones underlie everything — not bass guitar, but the felt resonance of enormous stone spaces. Above this, thin bright tones move independently of each other like bioluminescent organisms — sometimes briefly aligning in a moment of startling consonance, then drifting apart again. The theremin provides the alien-organic quality: not used melodically but as texture, a wavering presence. Handpan gives periodic metallic resonance that sounds found rather than composed. No time signature. No drums. Vast.

---

### Work 2.2 — Lhaketh'oma Vethis ("The Vast Peace of Living Things")
**Type:** Instrumental
**Context:** Mid-period work composed after the Shal'kora expeditions. First Furling piece to incorporate cross-species percussion influence. The Shal'kora heartbeat pulse enters here — not as a driving beat, but as something felt beneath, like planetary rotation.

**Styles:** `organic ambient, experimental ambient, drone, handpan, space ambient, tabla`

**Prompt:** Two separate musical worlds in conversation: a slow persistent handpan and tabla pulse — not a beat, more like a heartbeat you can feel in the floor — and over it a wash of sustained tonal voices, both instrumental and vocal-textural. The percussion is not driving the music; it is simply present the way a planet's rotation is present. The melodic layer moves independently, indifferent to the pulse below it, until moments where they briefly share a breath and it feels significant. Warm but vast. Alive but not urgent.

---

### Work 2.3 — Rala'velun Kethavar ("The Signal I Am Sending")
**Type:** With Lyrics
**Context:** A rare vocal work — Selethara composed very few with text. Written on the return journey from her third expedition. The emotional core: *I am sending this into the dark. I don't know if anyone will receive it. I'm sending it anyway.* The ellipsis marks a held silence — ambient continues, no singing.

**Styles:** `space ambient, experimental ambient, organic ambient, sung poetry, drone`

**Prompt:** Sparse vocal piece — a single voice, unhurried and without vibrato, suspended over a deep ambient drone. The voice does not perform emotion; it simply carries sound, the way a crystal carries light. Vowel-heavy alien language. Periods of silence between phrases that are as present as the sound. The ambient layer breathes very slowly beneath, never competing. The feeling is of someone transmitting into the dark, patient, with no expectation of immediate reply.

**Lyrics (Furling):**

```
Mira la'nēlar

...

Oun la'skar thema

Oun la'skar thema
Velun, la'mai
```

**Translation:**

## ARTIST 3 — Reva Shola-Thareth
**Era:** ~141 (~6,000 years ago)
**Known as:** The Last Light
**Origin:** Thareth Station, inner rim orbital (no planet)
**Tradition:** Song · Intimate · Present
**Archive Ref:** Vara'luma'athela 8841-RST · also filed: Vara'thara'athela (Historical Art)

**Bio:** Born on an orbital station — no planet underfoot, no soil, no sky. Part of the generation that watched the Keth'alis threat move from rumour to horror. By reputation extraordinarily technically skilled, having studied under three masters across three worlds, absorbing the full range of Furling musical tradition. Her own journals describe her earlier work as "too careful. Music for those who already have everything." The Keth'alis crisis changed her entirely. In the final 20 thara'keth before the exodus she produced a body of work unlike anything else in the archive — raw, urgent, alive with unresolved love for things being lost. Her Shala'Thara cycle is 47 short pieces each named for a specific place that would be abandoned. They are not mournful — they are present. She deliberately moved toward accessibility in her final period, embedding familiar melodic phrases from earlier tradition under her own compositions so frightened, displaced Furlings had something known to hold. Her final work, Vetha'mai Nibiru, was performed in Nibiru's main hall before the sealing, 400 Furlings present. She died in transit before reaching the Milky Way. Her music survived because she sent archive copies to Nibiru before departure.

---

### Work 3.1 — Thara'keth Thareth ("Station Time / Eternal Time")
**Type:** Instrumental
**Context:** An early work, before the crisis. Reva grew up with no planet underfoot and this piece reflects that — music reaching toward a physical world she never had. Her most abstract piece, from before everything changed.

**Styles:** `new age, ambient, post-minimalism, chamber ensemble, synthesizer`

**Prompt:** A chamber piece with a feeling of searching for solid ground. Sparse strings and sustained tones, gently interlocking. There is something slightly weightless about it — not floating, but without the gravity of planetary music. The melodic phrases feel like someone looking out a window at a very long view. Not melancholic — curious. The texture is clean, precise, with small silences that feel like the gaps between stars. Gradually the piece finds a quiet resting point that it holds for a long time before releasing.

---

### Work 3.2 — Skela'oma, Nibiru ("Welcome Home, Nibiru")
**Type:** Instrumental
**Context:** From the Shala'Thara cycle — 47 pieces each named for a place. This is the Nibiru piece. Played on the day the evacuation began. It is not a farewell. It refuses to be a farewell.

**Styles:** `new age, chamber ensemble, ambient, harp, post-minimalism`

**Prompt:** Music that sounds like love refusing to become grief. Harp and strings, warm and present, with a recurring melodic phrase that is simple enough to feel like something you have always known. Not triumphant, not weeping — fiercely, quietly, fully here. The arrangement is sparse enough that each note has space around it. There is an urgency underneath the gentleness — not panic, but the quality of someone memorising a face. The piece should end with the main melody played once, very quietly, alone.

---

### Work 3.3 — Vetha'mai Nibiru ("Life-Greetings, Nibiru")
**Type:** With Lyrics
**Context:** The final work. Composed and performed in Nibiru's main performance hall before the city was sealed. 400 Furlings present. Reva died in transit before reaching the Milky Way. The emotional core: *not farewell. We were here. This was real. Come back.*

**Styles:** `ambient pop, chamber ensemble, art pop, sung poetry, harp`

**Prompt:** A vocal piece for solo voice with harp and sparse strings. Intimate, unhurried, present. The singer is not performing — they are simply speaking something true in musical form. Vowel-heavy alien language that sounds both ancient and immediate. The melody is simple and memorable — the kind of melody that feels like it was always there. No crescendo, no dramatic peak. The power is in the restraint. The final verse is quieter than the first, not louder.

## ARTIST 4 — Orvhen Luma-Shael
**Era:** ~92–147
**Known as:** The Light Between · informally among younger Furlings: Vel'luma ("The Light")
**Origin:** Shael'vora, mid-tier colony world — prosperous, educated, firmly Shala'vel in character
**Tradition:** Popular / ceremonial crossover · rhythm-led vocal composition
**Archive Ref:** Vara'luma'athela 4417-OLS

**Bio:** Born to a family of educators on Shael'vora — his father a language scholar, his mother a mathematics instructor. The household was everything the Golden Age Shala'vel tradition produced at its best: intellectually warm, deeply principled, and entirely confident that the Vel'athara were mythologised memory. Faith was not mocked in the Luma-Shael home. It simply wasn't there.

His musical ability was apparent before his coming-of-age. By sixteen he was performing publicly. By nineteen he was the most talked-about young musician on Shael'vora, his recordings circulating across three neighbouring systems. What distinguished him was not just quality but *character* — his music didn't perform at you. It pulled you in. Younger Furlings described his early work as feeling like being welcomed somewhere they hadn't known they wanted to go.

His early career was the most purely joyful period of his output — layered vocal harmonics over deep rhythmic structures, melodic lines that lodged in the mind for days, lyrics full of the specific pleasure of being alive and young in the most prosperous era Furling civilisation had ever known.

Somewhere in Era ~126 something happened. He cancelled one performance — the only cancellation of his career — and went silent for eight months. When he returned, the music was different. Still rhythmically driven, still warm, but searching in a way the early work wasn't. Lyrical fragments appeared that his Shala'vel listeners found baffling and his Vel'athara listeners found immediately recognisable — references to the ones who came before the record, to the teaching that arrived before the teacher had a name.

He didn't announce anything. Then in Era 129, in a conversation intended to be about compositional technique, he said without planning to that he had come to believe the Vel'athara were real. That he had always felt something in the music he couldn't account for rationally. The interview became the most widely read piece of writing in the Kigal Galaxy that year.

His family's response was private but its shape became known. His father published a measured academic response to the Vel'athara question that never mentioned his son by name but whose timing was unmistakable. The Vel'athara faithful embraced him with a completeness he found suffocating — he had not converted to become anyone's symbol. For several years he occupied the specific loneliness of someone claimed by two communities and fully belonging to neither.

The peace came slowly. His late period — the final decade of his life as the Keth'alis crisis built — produced his finest work. He reconciled with his father before his father's death in Era ~143. His final completed work, *Vel'athara La'mai*, contains in its closing section a quiet melodic quotation from Thelo Varamis's *Korath Vel* — unnoticed by most listeners for years. He completed the recording eleven days before his death. He did not take the longevity extension. When asked why, he said only: *"I think I want to know what my life is actually made of."*

---

### Work 4.1 — Shael'vora Luma ("Shael'vora Light")
**Type:** With Lyrics — Early Period
**Context:** From his second recording, Era ~112. Pure early joy — the sound of someone absolutely at home in being alive. No shadows yet. The emotional core: *being alive is extraordinary and I want you to feel that too.*

**Styles:** `art pop, ambient pop, indietronica, choral, handpan, downtempo`

**Prompt:** Layered vocal harmonics over a warm rhythmic pulse — not drums exactly, more like a heartbeat made musical. The kind of music that makes you move without deciding to. Alien language, vowel-heavy, the vocals stacked in ways that feel like a crowd celebrating. Melodic and immediately memorable. Joyful without being lightweight. Warm and deep — nothing sharp, nothing harsh. It should feel like being let into somewhere wonderful.

### Work 4.2 — Nai'vel'athara ("The Hidden Ancient Light")
**Type:** Instrumental — Middle Period
**Context:** Era ~129, the year after the interview. No lyrics — he said publicly he didn't yet have the words for what he was trying to say. The searching quality is fully present. Still danceable. Something underneath it younger Furlings could feel without being able to name.

**Styles:** `indietronica, ambient pop, downtempo, space ambient, handpan, experimental ambient`

**Prompt:** A rhythmic pulse that feels like searching rather than arriving — present and warm but with a quality of movement toward something not yet found. Layered melodic fragments that almost resolve and then open again into something new. The handpan gives alien resonance underneath the warmth. Moments where the rhythm drops to near-nothing and then returns, like someone catching their breath mid-sentence. Not sad — uncertain in a way that is still beautiful. The feeling of an important question asked in the most patient possible way.

### Work 4.3 — Vel'athara La'mai ("The Ancient Light Greets Us")
**Type:** With Lyrics — Late Period
**Context:** His final completed work, Era ~147, finished eleven days before his death. The peace has arrived. The searching is over. The emotional core: *I found the thing I was looking for. It was there the whole time. It is still here.* The closing section contains a quiet melodic quotation from Thelo Varamis's Korath Vel — unnoticed by most listeners for years.

**Styles:** `art pop, ambient pop, chamber ensemble, choral, sung poetry, harp, space ambient`

**Prompt:** A vocal piece that has come through something and arrived somewhere real. Warm, unhurried, with a gentle rhythmic quality — like breathing rather than dancing. Layered harmonics that feel like a community gathered quietly. The alien language has the quality of someone who has been working toward saying something for a long time and has finally found the words. Toward the end, an older and simpler melodic phrase enters — ancient-feeling, choral, as if it came from somewhere further back than this song. The voice holds it gently. No crescendo. It ends the way understanding ends — not with noise, but with quiet.

*The final* `la'mai` *carries in Furling the sense of an ongoing greeting — something still arriving, not yet complete. Orvhen's last recorded word is the same word Reva Shola-Thareth used to close Vetha'mai Nibiru. Whether this was intentional has never been confirmed.*

## Suno Production Notes

- **Non-Earth sound:** Use `drone` or `space ambient` as the base style — this removes the pulse that makes Earth music feel familiar. Add tonal instruments (harp, handpan, theremin, chamber ensemble) as colour on top.
- **Furling lyrics in Suno:** The vowel-heavy, soft-consonant structure suits AI vocal models well. If words are being garbled, add to prompt: "vowel-heavy alien language, no hard consonants, first syllable stress."
- **Repetition is intentional:** Furling song lyrics use repetition emotionally, not structurally — the same phrase sung twice means something different the second time. Don't cut repeats when entering lyrics into Suno.
- **Selethara's silence:** The ellipsis in Rala'velun Kethavar marks a held silence. Suggest in prompt ("a long held silence mid-piece where only ambient breath remains") or create in post-production.
- **Keep handpan to Selethara and Orvhen's middle period:** Reva's warmth should come from harp and strings only — preserves the sonic distinction between artists.
- **The la'mai connection:** Reva's Vetha'mai Nibiru and Orvhen's Vel'athara La'mai both end on the word *la'mai*. If you ever play these back to back in a session, that's a moment worth letting breathe.
- **Emotional arc across all four artists:** Thelo (grief into ground) → Selethara (wonder at encounter) → Orvhen (the long search for something true) → Reva (love refusing to become farewell). Four different eras. One civilisation.
