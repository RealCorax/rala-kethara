# FURLING PORTAL NETWORK — COMPLETE REFERENCE
*Covers: history, mechanics, the 35 symbols, archive document, and GM notes*

---

## PART ONE — HISTORY & ORIGIN

### The Two Stages

The Furlings did not invent portal travel in its current form. They arrived at it across two distinct stages, and both matter.

**Stage 1 — Short Range (Pre-Era 14)**

The earliest Furling portals predate interstellar travel entirely. Developed during the reconstruction period following Era 12, when the need to move people and materials quickly across a recovering world was urgent.

These portals were short-range — capable of connecting two points on the same planet, or between a planet and a nearby moon. They required significant power for modest distances. Dialling was simple: a rotating crystal dial with a small set of location symbols specific to that world.

They worked. They were not enough.

The fundamental problem was range. The physics of short-range portal connection could not simply be scaled outward. Connecting two points on Mira required one order of energy and precision. Connecting Mira to another star system required something qualitatively different — a way of describing not just *where* but *how far*, in a way the universe would recognise and honour. The Furlings were still working on this problem when they met the Alterans.

**Stage 2 — The Alteran Gift (Era 14)**

The Alterans had solved it. Their Stargate network — already spanning galaxies — was built on a system of seven-point addresses: six symbols to describe a location in three-dimensional space, one symbol to identify the point of origin. The mathematics described positions within a moving, expanding universe with extraordinary precision.

The Furlings studied this system deeply. What they built from it was not a copy. It was a fusion — Furling portal technology extended to interstellar range using Alteran addressing principles, redesigned around Furling materials, Furling aesthetics, and Furling intent-based activation.

The result was the network that still exists today.

### Relationship to the Stargate Network

Furling portals and Stargates are related but distinct technologies. They are compatible — a Furling portal can in principle connect to the Stargate network and vice versa — but they feel different, operate differently, and were built with different philosophies.

| | Furling Portals | Stargates |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Furling short-range tech + Alteran addressing | Alteran |
| Aesthetic | Bio-organic, grown, warm | Metallic, constructed, precise |
| Dialling | Key + rotating rings, or wrist device | DHD or manual |
| Address length | 7 symbols (6 destination + 1 origin) | 7 symbols (same principle) |
| Security | Key required to dial out | No key required |
| Network | Kigal Galaxy only | Multi-galaxy |

The Furlings understand the relationship clearly and document it in the archive. Whether the Alterans considered the Furling network a branch of their own, or something separate, is not recorded.

## PART TWO — HOW A PORTAL WORKS

### The Portal Itself

A Furling portal is a standing structure — roughly circular, grown from bio-organic material rather than cast or forged. No two portals are identical, because no two were grown in exactly the same conditions. They share a family resemblance: organic curves, faintly luminescent surface, the quiet hum of a system waiting. Each carries the character of the world it was grown on.

The dialling mechanism is mounted to the right side of the portal frame — integrated into the portal itself, not a separate pedestal.

### The Key

Every Furling portal requires a key to dial outward. The key is an ornate crystal-and-organic device shaped to fit the socket precisely.

**Inserting the key:**
- Activates the portal's power draw from its local source
- Reveals the **inner ring** — 12 symbols physically present on the dialling face

**First side button:**
- Projects a **middle ring** holographically — 11 more symbols appearing around the inner ring

**Second side button:**
- Projects an **outer ring** holographically — 12 more symbols appearing beyond the middle ring

All three rings together present the complete **35-symbol** Furling portal set for the Kigal Galaxy.

**To dial:**
1. Rotate the key so its tip points to each symbol in sequence
2. Press the key *inward* to lock each symbol
3. After all six destination symbols are entered, pull the key *outward*
4. If the address is valid and the destination portal is active, the wormhole engages

### The Address

Every portal address is **7 symbols**: 6 to describe the destination, 1 for the point of origin.

The six destination symbols triangulate a position in space using stellar reference points distributed across the Kigal Galaxy — the same addressing principle the Alterans developed, adapted to Furling use. The origin symbol identifies which portal is dialling.

A Furling who knows an address can reach any active portal in the network. The symbol combinations number in the hundreds of millions. Most produce nothing.

### The Wrist Device

Those carrying a Furling *rala'vetha'skal* (wrist device) can dial portals remotely without a physical key. The device stores addresses in crystal memory and transmits a dialling sequence to any portal within range.

Wrist devices can store a large number of addresses. Senior Furling travellers typically carried dozens. Bulutha's device, held in the Nibiru archive, contains several hundred known destinations across Kigal.

### Speaking an Address

A Furling dialling from memory speaks the address quietly under their breath while turning the key. Each symbol has a short sound — two to four characters — drawn from the roots of the Furling language. Six symbols spoken in sequence with the apostrophe-breath between them sounds almost like a short phrase.

The origin symbol is spoken last, after a longer breath:

> *vel'om'sha'lha'ren'eth — · — vai*
> *(address) — (breath) — (origin: Nibiru)*

### The Network Today

At the time the archive was last updated (~4,460 thara'keth ago), the network comprised several hundred active portals across the Kigal Galaxy — on Furling-settled worlds, allied species' worlds, and deep-space waypoint installations.

The network was **not disabled during the exodus**. The Furlings left it running.

- Portals powered by local geothermal or stellar sources will still be active
- Portals that depended on Furling maintenance may have gone dark
- There is no current record of which portals remain active
- That knowledge requires someone to go and look

## PART THREE — THE 35 SYMBOLS (VARA'THARA'NAL)

*The Star-Cycle-Marks. Each symbol corresponds to a recognisable stellar formation visible from the core of the Kigal Galaxy.*

The sounds are not arbitrary. Each is a fragment or compressed form of a Furling root word — meaning bleeds through every address. A Furling reading an address doesn't just see coordinates. They see echoes of meaning.

The three rings loosely track scale and remoteness: inner ring sounds are warm and familiar, outer ring sounds are vast and deep — consistent with how the language treats scale (lh- prefix for vastness, long vowels for depth).

### The Poetry of Addresses

Because the sounds echo real roots, spoken addresses carry flavour a Furling would register immediately.

**vel'om'sha'mir'eth'kel**
*life · peace · wisdom · ground · [life-eternal] · source*
Reads almost like a blessing. Probably an address for somewhere the Furlings considered deeply settled or sacred.

**ath'rha'keth'lha'oru'miru**
*ancient · ceremonial · eternal · vast · deep-peace · deep-world*
Every syllable points toward deep time and remoteness. This address would make a Furling pause before dialling.

**ren'vai'mev'sel'tha'ven**
*help · becoming · gift · keeper · time · living-flow*
Warm and purposeful. Feels like an address for somewhere active — a teaching world, a way station, somewhere people were *doing* something.

**lhor'keth'shel'oru'vhei'alo**
*vast-peace · eternal · wisdom-on-air · deep-peace · quiet-becoming · ancient-peace*
Three peace-echoes, two deep-time echoes. Meditative. Probably somewhere very remote. Possibly somewhere no one has dialled in thousands of thara'keth.

### Nibiru's Origin Symbol

**vai** (symbol 10) — *toward, becoming, future aspect*

Bulutha chose this deliberately. When you speak Nibiru's address, the last sound you make is *vai* — the future particle, the word that means *something is turning toward what it will be*. Every time anyone dials Nibiru, the address ends with hope.

### 04.01.06 — The Portal Network | How We Learned to Cross the Dark

*Branch 04 — Meva'shala · What We Have Built · Access: Visitor*

---

A Furling portal is a doorway between two fixed points in space. Step through one side, emerge from the other — regardless of the distance between them. The journey takes no more time than crossing a room. The distance is irrelevant. What matters is that both ends exist, both are powered, and you know the address of where you are going.

This is not travel. It is *arrival*.

---

**What a Portal Looks Like**

A Furling portal is a standing structure — roughly circular, grown from bio-organic material. No two are identical. They share a family resemblance: organic curves, a faintly luminescent surface, the quiet hum of a system waiting. Each carries the character of the world it was grown on.

The dialling mechanism is mounted to the right side of the portal frame, integrated into the structure itself. At its centre is a key socket.

**To dial a portal:**

Insert the key. The inner ring of twelve symbols becomes visible on the dialling face. Press the first side button and a second ring of eleven symbols appears holographically around the first. Press the second button and a third ring of twelve more appears beyond that. All three rings together present the complete thirty-five symbols of the Furling portal network.

Rotate the key to point at each destination symbol in sequence. Press the key inward to lock each one. After the sixth symbol is entered, pull the key outward. If the address is valid and the destination portal is active, the wormhole opens.

**The address is seven symbols:** six describing the destination, one identifying where you are. The seven-point system was developed in cooperation with the Alterans — we had the portals; they had the mathematics to reach across stars. The fusion of both is what you are standing in front of.

Those who carry a wrist device may dial remotely, without a key. The device holds many addresses in its crystal memory and can transmit a dialling sequence directly to any portal within range.

---

**How the Network Began**

We did not begin here.

The first Furling portals were short-range. Built during the reconstruction after Era 12, they connected two points on the same world, or between a world and its nearest moon. They were urgent tools for a people rebuilding after near-extinction. They worked. They were not enough.

The problem was range. The physics of connecting two close points could not simply be extended outward. We were still working on this when, in Era 14, a Furling deep-range survey vessel encountered an Alteran seed ship in the outer reaches of the Kigal Galaxy.

The Alterans had solved the problem we were working on. Their network spanned galaxies. They shared their mathematics with us — the seven-point addressing system, the way of describing a position in space with enough precision that a wormhole can find it across any distance.

We did not build a copy of their network. We built our own, using our materials and our methods, extended to interstellar range by their addressing principles. The Furling portal network in the Kigal Galaxy is the result of that collaboration — something neither species would have built alone.

---

**The Thirty-Five Symbols**

The symbols used in Furling portal addresses are called the *Vara'thara'nal* — the Star-Cycle-Marks. Each corresponds to a recognisable stellar formation visible from the core of the Kigal Galaxy. Together they form a map written in the sky itself.

The symbols are not arbitrary. Each sound that names a symbol is drawn from the roots of our own language. An address spoken aloud is not simply a sequence of navigation codes. It carries meaning — echoes of the words our language was built on. A Furling reading an address will often pause to note what it says.

The complete symbol reference is recorded in this archive with each symbol's sound, its stellar referent, and which ring of the portal dial it occupies.

*(See: 04.01.06a — The Vara'thara'nal — Symbol Index)*

---

**The Network Today**

At the time this record was last updated, the network comprised several hundred active portals across the Kigal Galaxy — on settled worlds, on the worlds of species we taught and worked alongside, and at waypoint installations in deep space that existed only to extend the network's reach.

The network was not closed when we left. It was left running.

Portals drawing power from local geothermal or stellar sources will still be active. Portals that depended on our maintenance may have gone dark in the time since. We do not know which portals remain open. That knowledge requires someone to travel the network and look.

If you are reading this and you are not Furling: the network is yours to use. The addresses in this archive are recorded for exactly this reason. We left them here because we intended for someone to find them.

---

**What This Archive Does Not Contain at Visitor Level**

The engineering principles behind portal construction, the mathematics of address calculation, and the technical specifications for portal installation are held at deeper access levels. What is here is sufficient to use the network — to understand what a portal is, to read an address, to dial a destination, to recognise the symbols.

Building a portal, or understanding the physics of why it works, requires a deeper study.

## PART FIVE — GM NOTES

### What Players Can Do at Visitor Level
- Read the above archive document
- See the 35 symbol sounds and their stellar referents in 04.01.06a
- Understand how to construct and speak an address
- Notice that spoken addresses carry meaning through the root system
- Begin to recognise symbols on portal dials they encounter

### What Is Locked (Student and Beyond)
- The mathematics of address calculation
- How to build or repair a portal
- The full catalogue of known portal destinations with coordinates
- Which portals are confirmed still active as of the exodus
- Details of the deep-space waypoint installations

### Wrist Device Addresses
Bulutha's wrist device (held in the Nibiru archive, accessible once players reach appropriate access level) contains several hundred known portal addresses across Kigal. This is a significant story resource — each address is a potential destination, and the address sounds will tell attentive players something about where they might be going before they arrive.

### The Nibiru Entry Portal
The portal on the surface of M4S-457 that leads *into* Nibiru is a special case — it is a single point-to-point connection, not part of the general network. It requires the Furling key and dials only one destination. Its address is not listed in the general network catalogue. This is how Nibiru remained hidden.

The Stargate *inside* Nibiru (Central Spire, Level 3) is part of the general network and can in principle dial any active portal in Kigal, or connect to the broader Stargate network — once the DHD is repaired.

### Address Design for New Locations
When creating portal addresses for campaign locations, use the 35 symbol sounds. Choosing sounds thoughtfully will give each address a subtle flavour:
- Teaching worlds or safe havens: warm inner-ring sounds (vel, om, sha, mir, ren, vai)
- Old or significant Furling sites: middle-ring ceremonial sounds (ath, rha, thal, kor)
- Remote, dangerous, or unknown destinations: outer-ring sounds (lha, lhor, keth, oru, miru)
- Mixed addresses feel like places with history — once well-known, now distant
