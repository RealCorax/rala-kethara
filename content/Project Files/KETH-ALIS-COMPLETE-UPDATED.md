## PART TWO — ORIGIN & HISTORY

### 2.1 The Athel'kin (Pre-Contact)

Before Furling contact the Athel'kin were a peaceful, nomadic, pre-industrial species organised into five Houses. Their natural lifespan was 30–40 years — intelligent and creative, but losing knowledge with every generation. Every cycle of elders dying took accumulated wisdom with it. They were perpetually racing against their own mortality.

Despite this, they were not primitive. By the time the Furlings found them they had developed sophisticated tools, basic metallurgy, early written language, trade networks between Houses, strong oral traditions, and a deep knowledge of medicine — particularly among the Ngo'verath (Forest People). They had simply hit the ceiling of what a short lifespan permits you to build.

Teaching children was the highest cultural value across all five Houses. Knowledge preservation was not just important — it was survival. This is the cultural bedrock that the transformation would eventually corrupt into something unrecognisable.

**The Five Houses:**

| House | Name | Character | Governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge Walkers | Rrozok | Direct, efficient, makers | Single strong leader |
| Water People | Ngahzoka | Ceremonial, memory keepers | Blood dynasty |
| Sky People | Geirroh | Open, curious, collective | Knowing Circle |
| Fire People | Zrohga | Practical, survival-focused | Earned leadership |
| Forest People | Ngo'verath | Quiet, observant, healers | Elder-voice (oldest speaks) |

The Ngo'verath were the smallest and most distinctive House — semi-nomadic where the others fully roamed, staying largely within a dense ancient forest they knew intimately. Other Houses told children's stories about them: *don't wander toward the still forest, the Ngo'verath will watch you from the trees.* Not violent stories. The Ngo'verath in those tales simply *knew things about you* before you spoke. They were unfailingly kind despite their reputation. They carried that quiet sadness with dignity.

The Original Three came from this House.

### 2.2 The Furling Contact and Golden Age (~7,000 to 5,200 years ago)

The Furlings made contact with all five Houses equally and began sharing knowledge freely, as was their nature. Within the first generation after contact, the Furlings offered genetic therapy to extend Athel'kin lifespans from 30–40 years to 300–400 years. The gift worked perfectly, with no initial side effects. The Athel'kin thrived.

What followed was approximately 2,000 years of genuine flourishing. With time to accumulate knowledge across extended lifespans, the Athel'kin advanced rapidly — technology, medicine, philosophy, space travel. The five Houses remained distinct but shared the bounty of Furling teaching. This was a real golden age, not a myth. The Athel'kin became a sophisticated, settled civilisation. They had cities. They had ships. They had 2,000 years of Furling-assisted education.

**Nibiru and the Welcome City:**
During this golden age, the Furlings periodically brought selected Athel'kin to Nibiru — the Furling welcome city hidden in an underground cavern on a moon in the Kigal Galaxy. Athel'kin visitors experienced the artificial sun, the seven knowledge districts, encounters with other species the Furlings were teaching, and access to the archive at Visitor level. They were shown the galaxy. They were treated as students and equals.

They were never told where Nibiru was. No coordinates were given. No gate address. This was standard Furling practice — knowledge flowed at the right pace, and the location of their central knowledge city was not information they shared with any species. The Athel'kin who visited knew only that they had been taken somewhere extraordinary and brought home again.

These experiences became cultural memory — stories told across generations. The teaching city of the Furlings. The place beneath a false sun where all species learned together. By the time the three were born, Nibiru was legend rather than living memory. Something their parents or grandparents might have experienced. Something that represented the height of what the Furling relationship had offered their species.

The tragedy is proportional to the height of what was lost.

### 2.3 The Discovery (~5,200 years ago)

**Important timeline note:** The three Keth'zara were born into an Athel'kin civilisation that already had extended lifespans from the Furling gift. They did not witness the original contact or the genetic therapy — they are products of the golden age, not its architects. They grew up in a world shaped by Furling teaching, with Nibiru as inherited legend.

Three individuals from the Ngo'verath House — scientists and healers, products of the golden age — began private experiments pushing beyond the Furling gift. They wanted more life. Not out of malice. Out of the same hunger that had always defined their species: *we are running out of time.* Even 300–400 years felt insufficient to them.

They began with animals. Bio-energy existed in living things — they could detect it, study it, eventually extract it. The effect when consumed was real but modest. Then came the critical moment: one of them — probably Vaketh, the scientist, or Selvaris, the physician — tested it on a sentient being. The effect was overwhelming by comparison. Sentient bio-energy is qualitatively different, richer, more complex. It didn't just extend life. It began to reverse aging.

They kept it completely secret while they refined the process and tested it on themselves. The dependency crept in before they understood what it was. By the time they recognised that stopping would mean rapid death, they had been consuming sentient bio-energy for long enough that reversal was impossible.

The Furlings later attempted to reverse the dependency. They failed completely. It was not a choice that could be undone.

### 2.4 The Spread (~5,200 to 5,100 years ago)

The three did not announce what the bio-energy was or where it came from. They presented it as a medical breakthrough — a treatment derived from deep Ngo'verath knowledge of living systems. This was entirely believable from them. They offered it selectively to leaders of other Houses, framed as a gift.

The House leaders who accepted it felt the effects and wanted more. The three now had leverage over the most powerful people in the species — and those people didn't yet know the source.

Word spread. Not the truth — just the effect. Extended life beyond the Furling gift. Potential immortality. Everyone wanted it. The three could not contain demand and eventually stopped trying. Distribution moved through existing House structures, but all roads led back to the three who controlled supply. Within a generation the dependency had spread across the species. House leaders who might have resisted were already dependent.

A significant movement grew organically around the three during this period — true believers who genuinely thought this was the solution to everything the Athel'kin had ever suffered. They were not manipulated into faith. They looked at what they saw and believed it sincerely. These early devotees would become the seed of what later grew into a formal religion.

### 2.5 The Furling Intervention and the War (~5,100 to 5,000 years ago)

The Furlings discovered what was happening. They confronted the three privately first, then publicly — demanding immediate cessation of all bio-energy harvesting and offering everything they had to find alternatives or reverse the dependency. The Furlings were not enemies in this moment. They were horrified friends trying to stop a catastrophe.

The three understood the situation exactly: if the Furlings succeeded, everyone who was dependent would die. Including them. They had a choice between submission and death, or war.

They chose war — but they were smart enough to know they couldn't start it alone. They went public. Not with shame, but with framing. *The Furlings gave us longer life and now they want to take it back. They are not our teachers. They are our jailers.*

It worked. Every Keth'alis with anything to lose had the same interest: keep the supply flowing. House leaders who might have challenged the three politically were already dependent and unified by the same existential fear. The war was not fought for the three. It was fought for survival. The three simply positioned themselves as the only people willing to fight for it.

**Why the Furlings lost despite their advantages:**

The Furlings had defence capability but their military was built around a fundamentally different threat model. After millennia of being genuinely beloved as teachers and helpers, they were not psychologically or doctrinally prepared for a war of extermination from within their own sphere of contact.

More critically: the Furlings could not bring themselves to fight back at full capacity. Decisive violence against a species they had taught and loved was almost impossible for them. By the time they understood this was not a conflict that could be resolved through dialogue, too much ground had already been lost.

The physical reality of ground combat was devastating. The Athel'kin stood 6–7 feet tall, muscular, with partial wolf-like anatomy — fast, capable of pouncing and leaping, closing distance faster than targeting systems could track. The Furlings stood 4–5 feet. Even with superior weapons technology, the physical disparity in early engagements was catastrophic.

The three almost certainly targeted Furling knowledge infrastructure first: archive worlds, teaching centres, the places where Furling technology was understood and developed. Cutting the Furlings off from their own accumulated knowledge so they couldn't adapt during the war is exactly the calculating move Vaketh or Draveth would have made.

**What the Furlings destroyed:**
When it became clear they had lost, the Furlings made a deliberate and disciplined choice. They destroyed their capital world and all foundational technology and research they could not evacuate. This was not panic — it was clear-eyed denial of their technology to their enemy. The Keth'alis have spent 5,000 years working with only what they captured in the field: reverse-engineered equipment, scattered research, weapons technology. The deep science, the foundational knowledge that would let them truly replicate Furling capability, was deliberately destroyed.

Nibiru survived only because no one knew where it was. It was never defended — it was simply abandoned in a location no one could find.

The war lasted approximately 200–300 years. Near-total Furling extermination. A small number escaped to the Milky Way or into deep hiding. The Athel'kin claimed the Kigal Galaxy entirely and renamed themselves Keth'alis — the Eternal Ones.

### 2.6 The Consolidation Period (~5,000 to 4,500 years ago)

Victory did not mean stability. The harvesting infrastructure didn't yet exist at scale, and millions of lower-class Keth'alis were dying of dependency collapse. The three were scrambling to divide the galaxy and get the system running while simultaneously consolidating their political position.

The old House power structures were still fresh in living memory. Several leaders — particularly those from houses that had considered themselves superior to the Ngo'verath — resented the three's authority. One of them moved against the three directly.

**The First Challenge — The Founding Myth of Keth'alis Power:**

A lord who had held real standing before the war calculated that the three were administrators who had got lucky — not rulers by right. He moved against the three through assassination rather than open challenge. The attempt came close. One or more of the three nearly died.

The correction was total. Him, his family, his inner circle, his lieutenants, everyone who had served him personally. His sector redistributed to someone loyal. His name removed from official record. Not just death — *unmaking*. He never existed.

This story is the foundational political myth of Keth'alis civilisation. Every Sector Lord knows it. Children learn it. The answer only needed to be demonstrated once in its full form — though similar situations arose across the millennia, and the result was always identical.

**The creation of the Rroh'groka:**

The assassination attempt forced a reckoning. The three created the Rroh'groka — Life's Hand — from genuine fear as much as political calculation. Handpicked from across all sectors. The finest fighters the species produced, selected young, trained exclusively for this purpose. Given the highest vial access of any Keth'alis outside the three themselves.

The final test requires killing your closest friend. Not as cruelty — as severance. There is no going back after that. No old life to return to. The Rroh'groka's only family is the Rroh'groka.

## PART THREE — THE KETH-ZARA (THE ORIGINAL THREE)

### 3.1 Who They Are

The three original Eternal Ones who discovered bio-energy harvesting and led the war against the Furlings. They have ruled the Kigal Galaxy for 5,000 years. They are the oldest living beings in the galaxy.

They came from the smallest, most overlooked House. Every other House had underestimated the Ngo'verath. That history sits in the three as something cold and permanent.

**How they rule together:**
The three do not scheme against each other. After 5,000 years of shared experience, shared guilt, and shared power, they function less as three individuals and more as one organism with three perspectives. They have lived so long inside the role of gods that the role is them now. They don't perform divinity. They simply are what they became. This unified front is one of the most genuinely alien things about them — ordinary power structures always fracture eventually, but the three have not.

**The godhood:**
Vaketh and Draveth have fully inhabited the godhood. The weight of speech, the deliberate distance, the ceremonial presence — these are not performances. They are simply what these beings are now, five millennia into their existence. Selvaris has inhabited it too — until recently. Something has changed in him, and the gap between the performance and the person is beginning to show.

### 3.2 The Three Individuals

---

**VAKETH SORVAAN** — *"The First Who Sought"*

The scientist who first discovered that sentient bio-energy could extend life. Originally motivated by the genuine desire to help his people surpass even the Furling gift. Now the coldest and most pragmatic of the three — someone who made a choice long ago and has spent 5,000 years refusing to regret it.

- **Domain:** The Core Sectors — most populated worlds, highest harvesting facility density
- **Title:** *Veth'sorak* — "The Prime Eternal" (formal Keth'alis); *"First of the Endless"* (subject populations)
- **Personality:** Analytical, utterly unsentimental, views the galaxy as a system to be optimised
- **Relationship to Nibiru:** Wants to possess it and extract everything useful. Particularly interested in the Furling technological knowledge that was destroyed at the source — Nibiru's archive may contain what 5,000 years of reverse-engineering could not recover.
- **Inner truth:** Was the first. Carries that. Never speaks of it.

---

**SELVARIS KORATH** — *"The Sustainer"*

Was a physician. Wanted to cure aging — not for power, but because watching his people die in their thirties had broken him over and over across a career spent losing patients to time. Refined the extraction process and made it efficient. The most conflicted of the three, carrying the deepest guilt.

- **Domain:** The Outer Sectors — expansion territories, breeding worlds
- **Title:** *Mev'sorak* — "The Sustainer" (formal Keth'alis); *"Keeper of the Vials"* (subject populations)
- **Personality:** Controlled, precise, increasingly withdrawn from public life
- **Current status:** See Part Eleven — The Fracture. Selvaris is the engine of the current crisis.
- **Relationship to Nibiru:** The most personal relationship of the three. Nibiru represents the road not taken — the civilisation the Athel'kin were being guided toward before he and the other two changed everything. He wants the biological research. He also wants the reckoning.
- **Inner truth:** Still thinks of himself as a healer. That is perhaps the most horrifying thing about him.

---

**DRAVETH ASHKORI** — *"The Architect"*

The geneticist who made the dependency permanent and irreversible. Some say this was deliberate — that he understood exactly what he was creating and chose to do it anyway, because a dependency that could be reversed would always be reversed eventually. The most ruthless of the three.

- **Domain:** The Frontier Sectors — military operations, conquest, suppression of resistance
- **Title:** *Thral'sorak* — "The War Eternal" / "Shaper of Permanence" (formal Keth'alis)
- **Personality:** Convinced they had no choice; contempt for guilt; views the Furlings' philosophy as the real crime
- **Relationship to Nibiru:** Must control or destroy it. The Furling archive contains the complete record of the Keth'alis war, the bio-energy discovery, and the dependency from the Furling perspective. That knowledge in the wrong hands is existentially dangerous to everything the three have built.
- **Inner truth:** Made the dependency permanent. He has never explained why to anyone. Not even the other two.

### 3.3 How They Rule

The three do not micromanage. Their authority rests on three foundations that have been true for 5,000 years:

**They are the only source of life.** Control what keeps everyone alive and you do not need an army for most things.

**They cannot be killed.** Every rival, every challenger, every lord who ever moved against them is dead. The three remain. Five thousand years of watching everyone else die while the three endure has created a genuine sense that they exist in a different category from ordinary mortality.

**They remember everything.** They were there. When they speak about history it is not scholarship — it is memory. That is genuinely god-like to a species where even the oldest Eternal Ones are beginning to forget the early centuries.

### 3.4 The Tithe

Every sector pays tribute to the Keth-zara. The tithe is framed as religious offering, not tax.

**Grade requirement:** The three require A+ grade bio-energy as their tithe. The primary obsession of every Sector Lord is producing enough A+ grade to meet their tithe obligation.

**Consequence of failure:** Missing a tithe triggers the same response as the First Challenge — total erasure. The lord, his circle, his family, his legacy. Every Sector Lord knows this. It has happened more than once across 5,000 years. The result has never varied.

### 3.5 The Godship

The three did not create a religion. A religion grew around them because the circumstances demanded it. True believers in the first generation wrote texts, built temples, created ritual. Within a few centuries, living memory of Vaketh, Selvaris, and Draveth as Ngo'verath healers was completely gone. They had always existed. They were simply *the way things are.*

Most Keth'alis believe the three are divine, or close enough that the distinction is irrelevant. They give life. They cannot die. They have always been here and will always be here. The one or two cases per generation of someone testing that faith and being unmade reinforce the belief rather than create martyrs.

## PART FOUR — GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE

### 4.1 The House Lineages

The five original Houses did not disappear after the war. They transformed across 5,000 years into something more complex — political factions, noble lineages, cultural identities that cut across geography. A Keth'alis from Rrozok lineage feels something about that heritage even now, particularly if there are long-lived survivors who remember the old days and have been nurturing that identity deliberately.

The Houses-as-lineages are a source of persistent political tension beneath the Sector Lord structure. They are not officially recognised — the three prefer a geography-based governance model that dilutes House loyalty. But 5,000 years of immortals with perfect memories means House identity never truly died. It went underground. And underground resentment across millennia becomes something very complex.

### 4.2 The Sector Lords (Keth'ngo)

The galaxy is divided into 20–50 major sectors, each ruled by a Sector Lord — an Eternal One who survived the original war and earned their territory during the consolidation period. All Sector Lords are approximately 5,000 years old. There are no new Sector Lords — positions have not been created since the consolidation. When a lord is removed, their sector is redistributed to an existing lord or divided, never given to a new Eternal One.

**What they control:**
- All harvest operations within their sector
- Their own military force
- Trade and distribution of ngeva within their sector after tithe
- Genetic assessment programmes and world classification decisions
- Internal justice and administration

**How they relate to each other:**
They compete through harvest efficiency, quality of populations, vial surplus, and political favour with the Keth-zara. Trade between sectors is constant and complex. Alliances exist and shift. Rivalries are persistent. Assassination and political manipulation are more common than direct military conflict. The three tolerate internal politics among the lords as long as tithe is paid and the system functions.

**What they cannot do:**
Challenge the three. Withhold tithe. Attempt to harvest independently of the system.

### 4.3 Below the Sector Lords

Layers of administration manage individual worlds, harvest facilities, genetic assessment, and population movement. These are not Eternal Ones — they are ordinary Keth'alis of sufficient wealth and loyalty to be trusted with administrative responsibility. This area is not yet fully developed.

### 4.4 The Military

**Overall scale:** Approximately 1 billion military personnel — 10% of the total Keth'alis population.

**Structure:**
- The Rroh'groka answer only to the three. They are not part of the sector military structure.
- Each Sector Lord commands their own military force, funded from harvest surplus after tithe.
- The three maintain a central military force beyond the Rroh'groka for major operations.

**Motivation:** Military service is the best available option for poor Keth'alis. Guaranteed vial stipends, advancement through demonstrated capability, bonus vials for successful operations. Officers with centuries of experience are common. The force is ruthlessly efficient and has no moral hesitation about what it does.

## PART FIVE — THE BIO-ENERGY SYSTEM

### 5.1 The Harvesting Process

1. Harvesting ships arrive at subject worlds without warning — random schedule, no pattern, deliberate psychological terror
2. Keth'alis farmers deploy on the ground, paralyse subjects with *ruk* (paralysis weapons — handheld crystalline rifles firing purple energy bolts — see Section 8.3)
3. Paralysed subjects are drawn up into the harvesting ship via large tentacle appendages that descend from the hull
4. Subjects are placed in vertical, semi-transparent extraction chambers aboard ship
5. Connected via tubes and conduits to extraction systems
6. Kept semi-conscious throughout — not fully aware, but not unconscious
7. Bio-energy slowly drained over 3–7 days
8. Subject dies as a desiccated husk
9. Extracted energy refined and sealed into crystalline vials

**The fundamental equation:**
> 100 sentient beings = 1 vial = 1 year of life extension

**The harvest is fatal.** Subjects do not survive extraction.

### 5.2 The Full Harvest Pipeline

**Stage 1 — Mass Collection:**
Everything that can be paralysed is taken. No filtering at ground level. Speed and volume is the priority.

**Stage 2 — Filtering (aboard ship):**
Collected subjects are assessed. Two streams:
- *Immediate processing* — the majority. Harvested for bio-fuel. Fatal.
- *Selected stock* — a minority chosen for breeding programme placement based on physical health, age, and genetic indicators. They do not know they have been spared. They do not know what they have been spared for.

**Stage 3 — Breeding Worlds:**
Selected subjects are relocated to breeding worlds — frequently worlds whose original populations were entirely harvested out, with infrastructure left intact and repurposed. New populations are seeded from multiple species, specifically chosen combinations the Keth'alis have found produce good yield. These populations are managed at a distance — fed, housed, medically maintained. They have no idea what they are. They think they are simply another harvested world. They may even consider themselves relatively fortunate.

**Stage 4 — Premium Harvest (~40 years):**
After roughly 40 years — a full generation — the breeding world population is at peak yield. A coordinated large-scale extraction moves in. The infrastructure that kept them alive is no longer needed. Some breeding worlds are completely cleared. Others have a remnant population left to begin the cycle again.

### 5.3 World Classification

| World Type | Population Experience | Keth'alis Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Breeding World (20%) | Stable, managed, false security | Premium A+ yield, 40-year cycle |
| Managed World (60%) | Random ships, terror, attrition | Bulk standard yield |
| Reserve World (20%) | Light-touch, appear "lucky" | Genetic reserves, pristine bloodlines |

### 5.4 Vial Grades

| Grade | Description | Who receives it |
|---|---|---|
| A+ | Exceptional — prime cultivated stock, optimal genetics | Keth-zara tithe only |
| A | High quality — healthy managed populations | Sector Lords and elite |
| B | Standard — average managed world harvest | Middle and working class |
| C | Below standard — older stock, less healthy populations | Survival tier |

### 5.5 Vial Properties

- Small crystalline containers, portable and stable for years without degradation
- Glow with captured life energy — brightness indicates grade
- Consumed via drinking, injection, or dermal absorption
- Function as literal currency — stored, traded, inherited, stolen
- Vial theft is a capital crime

### 5.6 Consumption Tiers

| Tier | Population % | Rate | Effect | Reserves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Survival | ~60% | 1 vial per 10 years | Prevents death; visible aging continues | 0–10 vials |
| Maintenance | ~30% | 1 vial per 3–5 years | Reasonable health, slow aging | 10–50 vials |
| Premium | ~8% | 1 vial per year | Peak health, minimal aging | 50–500 vials |
| Elite | ~2% | 2–5+ vials per year | Absolute perfection, no aging | 500–5,000+ vials |

### 5.7 Physical Deterioration Without Bio-Energy

- **Within months:** Rapid aging begins, energy pathways darken visibly
- **3–6 months:** Severe aging, weakness, organ failure begins
- **6–12 months:** Death inevitable
- **No recovery possible** — dependency is permanent and irreversible from first consumption

## PART SIX — GALACTIC CONTROL

### 6.1 Population Scale

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Keth'alis population | ~10 billion |
| Controlled galaxy population | ~10 trillion sentient beings |
| Annual harvest rate | 10% of controlled population |
| Beings harvested annually | ~1 trillion |
| Vials produced annually | ~10 billion |
| Annual Keth'alis minimum need | ~1.58 billion vials |
| Surplus | Reserves, elite extravagance, research, black market |

### 6.2 Population Management

Genetic assessors visit worlds regularly — clinical specialists who determine which individuals become breeding stock (relocated to breeding worlds), harvest candidates (taken for extraction), or relocation candidates (moved for diversity purposes). Families are never certain which fate awaits them.

Cross-seeding programmes move species between sectors to prevent genetic bottlenecks. Premium bloodlines are tracked across centuries. Some populations are cultivated across generations for optimal A+ yield.

### 6.3 What Subject Populations Know

Knowledge of the Keth'alis varies significantly depending on a species' history under their control.

**Most subject species know:**
- The red-skinned ones are the farmers — the face of the Incarnate at ground level
- Brownish-skinned ones are overseers — seeing one means something more serious is happening
- Older ones are paler. White ones are the worst. Don't draw their attention.
- The harvesting ships — recognisable from a distance, their arrival means the worst
- That people taken do not come back
- The *ruk* — the paralysis weapon carried by every farmer

**Resistance groups and scholars additionally know:**
- The skin colour shift is tied to age and duration of harvesting — white means ancient
- That healing doses exist and a wounded Keth'alis may not stay wounded
- Rough tier awareness — that white-skinned ones rule sectors at the highest level

**Almost nobody outside the Keth'alis knows:**
- The overdose effect and its consequences
- The exact mechanics of the dependency
- That they were once the Athel'kin — peaceful, short-lived, and helped by the Furlings
- The internal political structure — Sector Lords, House lineages, the three

**The discovery that the Keth'alis were once the Athel'kin** — that the beings harvesting the galaxy were once peaceful, creative, short-lived people who were helped by the same Furlings who built Nibiru — is one of the most powerful revelations available in the archive. It lands differently if subject species have no idea.

## PART SEVEN — SOCIETY & ECONOMY

### 7.1 Social Hierarchy

Class is measured in vials, not birth. The same Keth'alis born destitute can rise through military service or fortunate employment. The same wealthy individual who makes one catastrophic decision can fall. This creates a society with genuine social mobility — and genuine social terror.

| Class | Population % | Vial Reserves | Reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Destitute | 30–40% | 0–10 vials | Dose-to-dose survival, visibly aging, desperate |
| Working Class | 40–50% | 10–50 vials | Soldiers, labourers, administrators; one crisis from poverty |
| Middle Class | 10–15% | 50–500 vials | Professionals, officers, engineers; comfortable but not immune |
| Wealthy | 3–5% | 500–5,000 vials | Perfect preservation; business owners, high officials |
| Eternal Ones | <1% | 5,000+ vials | Sector Lords and the three; absolute power |

### 7.2 Economics

Vials are the universal currency. All employment, trade, investment, inheritance, and transaction is denominated in vials. Banking, loans, and insurance exist built around vial reserves.

The black market is massive — vial theft, assassination for inheritance, counterfeit attempts (which almost always fail — genuine vials have properties that cannot be faked). Vial theft is a capital crime. Inheritance assassination is technically illegal but practically common among the wealthy.

Reproduction requires a licence — minimum vial reserves must be demonstrated before reproduction is permitted.

### 7.3 Psychology — How Keth'alis Live With What They Are

**The majority — rationalisation:**
*We use resources optimally like any species uses food. The semi-conscious state is merciful. We maintain populations — we are conservators, not exterminators. The Furlings created this situation by interfering with our natural lifespan. We didn't ask to become dependent. One in ten is sustainable.*

Cultural education teaches harvesting as necessity. History is taught as Furling abandonment rather than Keth'alis aggression.

**The divided — guilt:**
A minority carry genuine guilt. Trapped by biological dependency, they cannot stop — cessation equals death. Some seek redemption through small kindnesses. A very few secretly assist resistance movements. All live with the weight of complicity they cannot escape.

For the three and the oldest Eternal Ones, 5,000 years of this weight has produced different outcomes: creeping numbness, philosophical distance, private haunting, or in a few cases complete disconnection from anything resembling conscience.

**The enthusiasts — embrace:**
A portion has moved past rationalisation into genuine belief in Keth'alis superiority. Harvesting is not necessity but right. Completely disconnected from the suffering that sustains them.

## PART EIGHT — PHYSICAL CHARACTERISTICS

### 8.1 Appearance — Base Form

**Height:** 6–7 feet tall. Slender but powerful — the frame underneath is muscular and built for speed.
**Features:** Wolf-like features in the facial structure and jaw. Beautiful in a way that registers as slightly wrong.
**Skin:** Appears organic but has a subtle wrongness — too smooth, almost porcelain. Slightly translucent in places, with visible bioluminescent energy pathways beneath the surface, particularly around joints, neck, and spine. Pathways are most visible after feeding and in lower light.
**Seams:** Visible lines where organic tissue interfaces with bio-synthetic components — at major joints, the base of the skull, along the spine. Elegantly designed but deeply unsettling up close.
**Eyes:** Beautiful but inhuman. Slight luminescence in low light. When focusing or accessing bio-energy, HUD-like qualities appear. Pupils dilate unnaturally. Eye colour shifts subtly based on bio-energy saturation.
**Movement:** Too precise. Too graceful. No wasted motion. Can be eerily still when not moving. Creates an uncanny valley effect even for beings who have grown up around them.

### 8.2 The Skin Colour Progression — LOCKED

This is one of the most important visual and narrative markers in the Keth'alis system. At a glance, any subject species can read approximate age and power level from skin colour.

| Stage | Colour | Age / Status | Who sees them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Born | Deep red / crimson variants | Newborn, pre-first dose | Rare |
| Young Incarnate | Red shifting to rust/terracotta | First decades of harvesting | Common — most farmers |
| Mid-age | Brownish, leathery, muted | Centuries old | Occasional — overseers |
| Elder | Pale brown to ivory | Thousands of years | Rare at ground level |
| Keth'zara & oldest Eternal Ones | Pure white | 4,000–5,000+ years | Almost never seen |

**The bio-energy aging mechanism:**
The transformation is driven by the bio-energy dependency. Between doses, aging begins — visibly. A Keth'alis running low starts to show it. A dose rapidly regenerates cells — injuries close, vitality returns. Over centuries and millennia, the cumulative effect of the dependency on the body's pigmentation produces the characteristic colour shift toward white.

**The moral weight of white skin:**
Every Keth'zara who has lived 5,000 years has consumed the equivalent of 400,000+ lives minimum just to survive. The white skin is accumulated atrocity made visible. Subject species who know this — resistance scholars, older populations — understand that a white-skinned Keth'alis represents not just individual power but millennia of the system's worst consequences.

**Strength and bio-energy:**
Baseline strength is modestly enhanced compared to the original Athel'kin. Nothing dramatically superhuman at normal dosing levels. A Keth'alis who takes multiple doses in rapid succession gets dramatically enhanced strength, speed, and regeneration — but suffers consequences afterward. The specifics of this overdose effect are to be determined.

### 8.3 Combat and Weapons

**Physical capability:** Backhand strike can throw a human 6+ metres. Enhanced speed and reflexes. Can pounce and leap — close distance faster than most targeting can track. Devastating in ground combat against smaller species. Cold, emotionless efficiency in a fight.

**The Ruk (weapon):**
The standard paralysis weapon carried by all Keth'alis farmers. A handheld crystalline device — grown rather than manufactured in appearance, distinctly non-human in aesthetic. Fires a purple viscous energy substance that on contact instantly paralyses and renders the target unconscious. Non-lethal by design — damaged harvesting stock is worth less. Subjects remain alive in a suspended state until bio-energy extraction. The word *ruk* simply means "weapon" in the Keth'alis language — no ceremonial title, no special name. It is a tool. Subject species across the galaxy have adopted the same word from sheer exposure.

**The Harvesting Ship:**
Large, slow-moving vessels that descend over settlements without warning. Enormous tentacle-like appendages extend from the hull, seeking paralysed bodies and drawing them upward into the ship. The slowness is deliberate — it doesn't need to be fast because nothing can stop it. Recognisable from a great distance. Subject populations know exactly what a harvesting ship on the horizon means.

## PART NINE — THE RROH'GROKA (LIFE'S HAND)

The elite guard of the Keth-zara. Created after the assassination attempt of the consolidation period.

**Selection:** Handpicked from across all sectors. The finest fighters the species produces, identified young. Selection cannot be refused. It is the highest honour in Keth'alis society.

**Training:** Extended, brutal, and total. All previous loyalties — House, sector, family — are systematically severed. The final test requires killing the trainee's closest remaining friend. There is no going back.

**Identity:** The Rroh'groka's only family is the Rroh'groka. Their entire existence is the three. They receive the highest vial access of any Keth'alis outside the Keth-zara themselves.

**In the current crisis (see Part Eleven):**
The Rroh'groka answer only to the three — collectively. The holy civil war creates an unprecedented problem for them. Some will fracture along with the Keth'zara, their personal loyalties and genuine religious faith pulling them toward one side or another after 5,000 years of service. Others may attempt to hold the unity of the three together by force, refusing to acknowledge the fracture. A divided or uncertain Rroh'groka is one of the most destabilising things that could happen to Keth'alis society.

## PART TEN — TECHNOLOGY

### 10.1 What They Have vs. What They Want

The Keth'alis have spent 5,000 years working only with what they captured in the field during the war — reverse-engineered equipment, scattered research, weapons technology taken from defeated Furling positions. The Furlings deliberately destroyed their foundational knowledge before the exodus. The Keth'alis cannot fully replicate Furling capability because they never had access to the deep science behind it.

| What they have | What they don't have |
|---|---|
| Reverse-engineered Furling field tech | The foundational science behind it |
| Functional but limited portal technology | Galaxy-to-galaxy travel capability |
| Captured Furling ship designs they've copied | Understanding of how to advance beyond them |
| 5,000 years of their own independent development | The Furling archive that would accelerate everything |

**Why inter-galactic travel is impossible for them:**
The Furlings' long-range portal capability was developed with Alteran assistance — the Ancients helped the Furlings extend their short-range portal network to galaxy-spanning range. The Keth'alis captured Furling short-range technology and have developed it further, but without the foundational Alteran-Furling collaborative science, true inter-galactic capability remains out of reach.

### 10.2 Nibiru as Technological Prize

The complete Furling archive — 150,000 years of scientific knowledge — would advance Keth'alis civilisation by potentially thousands of years. Vaketh understands this with cold clarity. The biological research specifically would be invaluable to Selvaris. The military and defence sections would interest Draveth. Every faction in the current civil war has a reason to want control of Nibiru.

## PART ELEVEN — THE FRACTURE (THE HOLY CIVIL WAR)

### 11.1 Selvaris and the Breaking Point

Selvaris Korath was always the most conflicted of the three. A physician who wanted to cure aging, who spent his entire career watching patients die before their time, who made a choice that saved his species from mortality and condemned a trillion beings per year to death in its place.

For most of five millennia, this conflict was managed. Private. He performed the godhood alongside the other two. He oversaw the outer sectors and breeding worlds. He inhabited the role of the Sustainer. The other two noticed something shifting in him across the centuries — the gradual drift of someone who was always the quietest of the three, becoming quieter still. But gradual change is easy to rationalise. He was contemplative. He was tired. He had always been different from the other two.

Then something happened. A specific moment, more recently, that tipped private conflict into action. The nature of this moment is not yet determined, but it crossed a line even he didn't know he had — something that made the abstract personal again, that reminded him after millennia of distance what a single life actually is.

He has not announced anything. He has withdrawn further from public life — plausible, even expected from a god, since rarity is part of the mystique. But he has begun acting on what he feels. Secretly. Carefully. With the full intelligence of a being who has survived 5,000 years of the most dangerous political environment in the galaxy.

**What he is doing:**
Selvaris is secretly researching an alternative to sentient bio-energy harvesting. Synthesising it. Extracting it from non-sentient sources. Finding a substitute entirely. He doesn't know if it's possible. The Furling biological research in Nibiru's archive — if he could access it — might be the thing that makes it viable.

**Why this is the most dangerous thing he could do:**
A working alternative doesn't just free subject species. It delegitimises everything. Five thousand years of a trillion deaths per year, and it turns out it wasn't necessary. The entire architecture of Keth'alis civilisation — the religion, the tithe, the Sector Lord structure, the class system, the biology of dependency itself — rests on the premise that there is no other way. Vaketh would understand this instantly. Draveth would understand it with dread.

### 11.2 Do the Other Two Know?

This is undetermined and represents a significant story choice. Two very different versions:

**If they don't know:** Selvaris has been hiding something from beings he has shared everything with for 5,000 years. This is almost impossible — which means he is extraordinarily careful, or they suspect and are choosing not to see it yet, or the three have drifted further apart over millennia than they appear.

**If they do know:** Why haven't they acted? Does Vaketh calculate that moving against Selvaris is more destabilising than tolerating his private doubts? Is there still enough of the original bond between them that even Draveth hesitates? Are they watching to see how far it goes before they respond?

### 11.3 What a Civil War in Progress Looks Like

This is not two armies clashing openly. The Keth'zara are gods. Open military conflict between factions loyal to different gods requires ordinary Keth'alis to point weapons at each other over a theological disagreement — that takes time to get to. What is already in motion:

**Political manoeuvring:** Sector Lords choosing sides based on their own interests, House lineage loyalties, and calculation of who will win. Some are playing both sides.

**Assassination and accidents:** Political rivals being removed in ways that are plausible but not provable. Loyalty tests disguised as administrative decisions.

**The religious fracture:** Clergy loyal to Selvaris's faction vs. clergy loyal to Vaketh-Draveth orthodoxy. Temples as politically charged spaces. Daily ritual phrases becoming loaded — which version are you performing, and who are you acknowledging?

**Resource warfare:** Lords cutting off vial supply chains to rival-aligned sectors. Harvest fleets redirected toward military positioning rather than subject worlds.

**The Rroh'groka problem:** Unprecedented uncertainty in the one institution that has always been completely unified.

### 11.4 What This Means for Subject Populations

The random harvesting schedule becomes even more unpredictable. Some worlds are hit repeatedly as rival factions compete for yield. Others go untouched for years as fleets are redirected.

Resistance movements that have been suppressed for generations find the boot on their neck has lifted slightly — not because anyone freed them, but because the Keth'alis are distracted.

This is the window Phoenix arrives through.

### 11.5 The Three Factions' Relationship to Nibiru

When word reaches the Keth'zara that Nibiru has been found — and it will, eventually — the three will respond according to who they are:

**Vaketh:** Take it immediately and extract everything useful. The technological prize overrides all other considerations.

**Draveth:** Control it or destroy it. The Furling record of the war and the dependency, told from the Furling perspective, cannot be allowed to circulate freely. The archive is a weapon pointed at everything the three have built.

**Selvaris:** Get there first. Alone. Before the other two know. The biological research. The reckoning. The place that represents what the Athel'kin could have been.

The players are inside Nibiru. Every faction has a reason to want what they're sitting on.
