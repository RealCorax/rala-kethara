# 06.04.01 What We Hold Sacred at the Root | Core Furling Sacred Traditions

*Compiled by Oma'vel Therishal, Era 88. This record is held open to all visitors.*

The Furlings were not a religious people in the sense that they organised worship around specific deities or doctrines. They were, however, a people for whom certain things were sacred -- held apart, treated with particular care, approached with reverence.

What they held sacred:

Life. Not as a concept but as a particular, irreducible fact. This living thing, here, now. The Furling reluctance toward killing -- which was real and deep -- was rooted not in doctrine but in the immediate experienced preciousness of any specific living thing encountered. The second principle, Compassion is Strength, is in part a formalisation of this instinct.

Memory. The dead are not gone while they are remembered. The memory vessel ceremonies, the oral traditions, the archive itself -- all of these are expressions of the understanding that memory is a form of continued presence. To remember carefully is an act of care for the remembered.

The meal shared. This is perhaps the most ordinary of the Furling sacred things, and the most consistently maintained. A meal eaten alone is *vel'skar* -- life-broken. A meal shared is the simplest form of the claim that the Whole is One.

The made thing. When a person makes something with attention and care -- a pot, a poem, a building, a garden -- and gives it away, they have done something sacred. The Furlings did not believe in the hoarding of good things. What you make well, you give. This is the fifth branch of this division in physical form.

These are not requirements or commandments. They are descriptions of what the Furlings, when they were most fully themselves, found worth protecting.
