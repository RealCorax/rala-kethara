# Furling Creation Myths

*Compiled by Vel'thara Omashal, Era 94. This record is held open to all visitors.*

Before the Furlings understood what a star was, they had already named theirs and told stories about it. Before they knew their world orbited that star, they had already described the relationship between ground and light in mythological terms that, in their own way, captured something true about it.

The creation traditions of the Furlings are not contradicted by the scientific record. They answer a different question. Science asks how. Myth asks what it means.

## The Four Major Creation Traditions

The Furlings developed four primary creation traditions, each arising in different regions of Mira and reflecting different aspects of the culture that produced it.

The *Two Beings* tradition holds that all of existence arose from the encounter between two presences -- one of light and motion, one of stillness and depth. The world is what their interaction produced. Individual Furlings have been arguing about what this means for approximately 140,000 thara'keth.

The *Breath of Fur* tradition holds that the first Furling breathed out and the universe condensed from that breath. This is considered the most intimate of the creation traditions -- it places the origin of everything inside a single body's act of exhaling. It is also considered the most philosophically demanding, since it implies that everything that exists is in some sense a continuation of that first breath.

The *Shattered Light* tradition holds that existence began as a single unified light that broke into pieces, and that all of experience is the movement of those pieces back toward each other. The tradition has inspired more Furling art than any other single source.

The *Wanderers' Gift* tradition holds that the Furlings were placed on Mira by beings who came before. This tradition was complicated but not invalidated by the discovery that the Alteran had sent seed ships through the galaxy. Most Furlings who hold this tradition consider the Alteran connection to be an interesting data point rather than a definitive answer.

All four traditions are held in the archive as cultural documents of profound importance. They are not the scientific record. They are the other kind of true.
