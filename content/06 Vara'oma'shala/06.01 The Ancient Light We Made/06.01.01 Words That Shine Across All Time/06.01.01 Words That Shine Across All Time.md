# 06.01.01 Words That Shine Across All Time | Vel'rala'vara

*Compiled by Thara'vel Omashal, Era 94. This record is held open to all visitors.*

The oldest piece of Furling writing held in this archive is approximately 148,000 thara'keth old. It is not a law or a record or a measurement. It is a poem about the way early morning light falls on still water.

Someone 148,000 thara'keth ago decided that this was worth writing down. That decision tells you more about the Furlings than almost any technical record.

The literary archive holds works from every era of Furling history -- from the earliest fragmentary texts through the reconstruction period's urgent poetry, through the golden eras' vast epics, through the quiet personal writing of the exodus years. The span is too great to summarise. What is given here is an orientation.

## The Major Forms

The Furlings developed three major literary forms across their history. Each reflects something about the era in which it flourished.

The *Keth'rala* is the great narrative form -- long, oral in origin, designed to be performed as well as read. The Keth'rala of the reconstruction era told the story of Era 12 and its aftermath. The greatest surviving Keth'rala -- the *Vel'thara'shala*, composed across approximately 200 thara'keth by multiple contributors -- holds the complete mythological and historical narrative of the Furling people in a single continuous arc. It is one of the longest works of literature in the archive.

The *Vel'shala* is the primary lyric form -- three stanzas of three lines, with the third line of each stanza echoing the first of the next, creating a continuous flowing sense of each ending becoming a beginning. It is extraordinarily difficult to translate. What arrives in another language is the thought; what is lost is the music of how the thought moves.

The *Oma'vel* is the quietest form -- a single extended breath, six to eight words, designed to hold a complete truth in minimum language. These are the hardest to compose and the most quoted. The Oma'vel that most Furlings can recite from memory:

*Vetha'nor oma'len* -- Life holds even in stillness.

## Accessing the Archive

The full literary archive is accessible in layers according to access level. The Visitor level holds a selected collection representing the major eras and forms -- approximately 400 works. Students and above have access to the complete archive of over 12,000 documented works.

The archive navigation system can retrieve works by era, form, theme, author, or connection to historical events. Ask for what you are looking for.
