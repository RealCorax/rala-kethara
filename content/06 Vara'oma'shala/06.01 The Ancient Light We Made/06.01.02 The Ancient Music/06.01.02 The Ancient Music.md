# 06.01.02 The Ancient Music | Vel'vara'rala

*Compiled by Vel'thara Omashal, Era 94. This record is held open to all visitors.*

The Furlings sang before they spoke. This is not metaphor -- the linguistic root of the entire language sits in a pre-verbal tonal communication system that was closer to music than speech. The language still carries this. Furling spoken aloud by a trained speaker is not simply said -- it is performed, with pitch and rhythm as much a part of meaning as the words themselves.

Music in Furling culture is participatory rather than performative. There is a distinction in Furling between *vara'vel* -- art-made, performance for an audience -- and *vara'shala* -- art-wisdom, the practice of making music as a form of knowing. Both are valued. But it is the second that appears at every meal, every ceremony, every gathering. Music is not something the Furlings did professionally. It is something they did the way they breathed.

## The Crystal Resonance Systems

Nibiru's walls contain a distributed acoustic system designed to support communal music. The resonance nodes -- visible as faintly glowing crystal formations at shoulder height throughout the main gathering spaces -- are tuned to specific harmonic intervals that complement the Furling vocal range. When voices are added in a gathering space, the walls respond, reinforcing certain frequencies and creating a natural reverb that makes even a small group sound like something more.

This is not a performance system. It was designed for the experience of the people in the room, not for an audience. You are welcome to use it.

## Accessing the Music Archive

Crystal memory recordings of Furling music are held throughout the archive. The navigation system can retrieve recordings by era, composer, occasion, or emotional content. Note that some recordings are of performances from the exodus period -- the final gatherings before the city was sealed. These are marked. Some visitors have found them difficult. They are held here because they are real.
