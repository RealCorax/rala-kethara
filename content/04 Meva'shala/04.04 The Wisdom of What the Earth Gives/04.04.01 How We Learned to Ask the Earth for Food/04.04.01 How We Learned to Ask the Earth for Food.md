# 04.04.01 How We Learned to Ask the Earth for Food | Mira'vel'meva

*Compiled by Oma'vel Therishal, Era 72. This record is held open to all visitors.*

Cultivation is a conversation. The Furlings used this word -- *meva'vel'mira*, literally gift-form-earth, usually translated as cultivation -- because it captured something the word farming did not. You do not take from the earth. You ask, and attend, and give back what it needs to give again.

This understanding shaped Furling agriculture from its earliest forms through to the growing systems in Nibiru. The hydroponic gardens in the Meva'kela district work on the same principle at a small scale: the plants are tended, not merely grown. The systems ask what each plant needs rather than assuming all plants need the same thing.

## Nibiru's Food Systems

The growing facilities in the Meva'kela district are operational. They produce a range of Furling staple foods and some cultivated varieties from other species' traditions. All visitors are welcome to use them.

If you are from a world where certain foods are essential to your biology and they are not currently being grown, the facility can accommodate new cultivation within a few mira'thara once seeds or cuttings are provided. The archive holds cultivation records for species from many worlds -- if your food traditions are documented here, the system can advise on replication.

The communal meal is held in the Meva'Kalara -- the central dining hall -- every mira'thara. Attendance is entirely voluntary. The tradition is that whoever attends, regardless of how many or how few, sits together.

## The Principle Behind the Practice

The Furlings had a word -- *vel'skar*, life-broken -- for eating alone. This was not an insult. It was a quiet statement of sympathy: something is missing when a meal cannot be shared. The agricultural knowledge held in this branch exists not just to explain how to grow food, but to explain why it was always grown for community rather than for individual accumulation.
